- Who pays: Bedroom and intermediate techno/EBM/acid producers on Ableton, Serum or Vital
- Pay signal: A paid rack shop with a discount code sits under an EBM bassline tutorial (157). Free MIDI+preset lead magnets get heavy grateful uptake (158,160,164). One viewer openly struggles to make good bass (162), and others ask for specific presets (185).

Sell on Gumroad or Ko-fi, with a free 5-pattern lead magnet. Also use it in his own studio as a sketch library.

## 2. Simple, single-purpose Max for Live utilities  (fit 5/5, ~4 wk)

- Pain: Many M4L devices are overcomplicated or theory-heavy. Users want small, clear tools, such as silencing audio on stop and macro morphing, that just work.
- Who pays: Ableton Suite users who are newly upgraded or buying M4L
- Pay signal: Users just upgraded to Suite or bought M4L (174,180) and are hunting device recommendations. Devs of small devices get thanked for daily use (171). Complaints say devices are 'too much' (175).
- Product: build it: a bundle of 3–5 tiny M4L devices aimed at dark club production, e.g. a stop-silencer/panic, a macro morph with snapshots, and an EBM accent/velocity groover. Price at €9–19 on Gumroad or maxforlive.com. Use it too.
- Known competitors (first guess): B.S Macro Morph, Chiastic Slide, Melda free utility, Iftah's free devices, Sting 2

## 3. Project versioning / anti-work-loss safety net  (fit 3/5, ~4 wk)

- Pain: Crashes, missing undo, and cloud sync overwriting newer versions wipe hours of work. This hits sample edits, chops and whole sessions.
- Who pays: Mobile and desktop producers who sketch ideas on multiple devices
- Pay signal: These are paid-app 1–2* reviews: 'wish I knew before spending the money' (5), 'deleted all my WIP 4 times' (47), 'an entire day of work lost' (39).
- Product: both: use it for iOS (export backups manually via AudioShare/Files). Build a small desktop app or script for Ableton that auto-snapshots .als plus collects samples into timestamped versions, with a diff and restore UI. Sell as a cheap utility.
- Known competitors (first guess): Splice Studio (discontinued), git-based Ableton setups, Ableton's own backup folder
- Check next: Check whether Ableton's backup folder already satisfies users (search forums for 'lost Ableton project'). Gauge interest in a one-click version history.

## 4. MIDI clock / Link bridging for hardware + iOS rigs  (fit 3/5, ~5 wk)

- Pain: Sequencers don't reset on stop/start and there's no MIDI clock input in AUM. Hardware like Roland Aira isn't recognised, so mixed rigs drift or fail.
- Who pays: Hybrid hardware/iOS users with grooveboxes and Aira gear
- Pay signal: Users paid $20 and the app failed to connect to a Roland J-6 (35). Others call missing MIDI clock 'outrageous' (32) and 'needs to be fixed' (30).
- Product: use it first: Link-to-MIDI-clock bridges and hardware sync boxes. Building a full bridge is possible but hardware-sensitive, so maybe only a desktop Link↔clock utility with an explicit start/reset behaviour option.
- Known competitors (first guess): midiLink Sync, Link2Midi, E-RM multiclock, Squarp/Blokas Midihub
- Check next: Test existing Link↔clock apps against a Digitakt/Aira setup in his studio and note what still breaks (reset on stop).
- Evidence: 4 posts from appstore id1055636344 (listed), appstore id1449584007 (listed)

## 5. Plugin state recall and stuck-note guard  (fit 3/5, ~3 wk)

- Pain: Plugin parameters reset to default on play or reopen, keys get stuck inside instruments, and instruments randomly go silent in DAWs.
- Who pays: Producers using wrapped or flaky third-party instruments in Ableton, Logic or Studio One
- Pay signal: These are complaints about expensive paid instruments ('fairly expensive VI', 72; Moog Model D via paid wrapper, 70,74).
- Product: build it: an M4L device that snapshots chosen plugin parameters per scene and re-applies them on load or play, plus an all-notes-off panic on stop. Use it on his own unstable synths.
- Known competitors (first guess): Ableton rack macros, Melda MUtility, generic MIDI panic devices
- Check next: Check M4L API limits for reading/writing third-party plugin parameters (only configured params), then prototype.

## 9. Mobile sketchpad with step entry and MIDI export to the DAW  (fit 2/5, ~5 wk)

- Pain: The paid Ableton Note lacks step entry, MIDI/AU, voice memo import and stable sync. Users start most ideas on the go and need them in the main DAW.
- Who pays: Producers capturing ideas on phone/iPad who finish in Ableton or on an MPC
- Pay signal: These are paid app complaints ('waste of my money', 27; '90% of my ideas start in Koala', 10; 'no MIDI or AU makes it not a serious tool', 23).

- Known competitors (first guess): Ableton Note, Koala Sampler, Drambo, Groovebox, Elastic Drums

## 10. Usable sample preview and quick chop/stem tool  (fit 2/5, ~3 wk)

- Pain: Sample previews are too short to judge sounds. Users want simple chop/reverse/pitch/envelope and drum removal in one place.
- Who pays: Sample-based beatmakers and producers digging through libraries
- Pay signal: Paid users say they can't use the app at all because of the preview (18). Another calls it a 'waste of money' over failing to remove drums (9).
- Product: use it: existing stem splitters and Ableton Simpler cover most of this. Build it only as a small web sample-browser with full-length preview + chop-to-Simpler export if he wants a free funnel tool.
- Known competitors (first guess): Ableton Simpler, XO, Atlas, LALAL.AI, Koala
- Check next: Check whether desktop sample managers (ADSR Sample Manager, Sononym) already solve the preview complaint before building anything.
