## Research (text only)

**Spotify editors**, "Behind the Playlists" ([blog](https://artists.spotify.com/blog/behind-the-playlists-your-questions-answered-by-our-playlist-editors)):
- Give the who, what, why, when, where and how of the song.
- Any interesting story around the artist or the song.
- They like to learn about context and community.
- Be specific about location and cultural sound, so the right local team (here: Germany / Berlin) finds it.
- Include press, video plans, release schedule, promotions.
- Followers and monthly listeners do not factor into their decisions.

**Spotify support** ([pitching-music-to-playlist-editors](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/pitching-music-to-playlist-editors/)):
- One song per pitch. 7+ days before release = Release Radar for followers.
- Edits up to release day may not be seen: treat the pitch as final.
- More detail gives the song a better chance.
- Genre, mood and culture tags matter (they route the pitch).

**SubmitHub guide** (third-party, weaker source):
- Order: hook / feeling, story or creative detail, 1-3 honest sound-alikes or micro-genre terms, 1-2 niche editorial playlists it fits, one factual promo line.
- About 500 characters, conversational, in the artist's voice.
- Avoid: generic phrasing, AI-sounding lines, overselling, naming huge playlists, pleading.

## Instrumental dance tracks: what changes (research 11-17, Amir: "they are instrumental")
No Spotify source gives instrumental-specific rules. The points below come from third-party guides only (weaker), plus our own archive.
- **Tags carry more of the load** when there are no lyrics: the genre desks route by tags. Pick the most specific genre, not just "Electronic" ([Redstar](https://redstar.media/news/playlist-pitching-strategies-for-edm-artists), [Dynamoi](https://dynamoi.com/learn/playlist-pitching/how-to-write-a-spotify-playlist-pitch)). Fill every field: instruments, mood, instrumental = yes ([We Rave You](https://weraveyou.com/2024/02/pitching-on-spotify-for-electronic-music-artists-dos-and-donts-to-consider/)). Don't pick moods that contradict each other.
- **Copy the tags of artists already on the target playlist**, and don't pick a style that's "a total lie" ([Jesse Cannon, 2026](https://musicmarketingtrends.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-write-a-spotify-editorial-playlist-pitch-in-2026-part-2-of-2)).
- **The text describes the sound and how the track is used**, not the lyrics: instruments, groove, energy, and where it plays (club, late night, DJ sets). Restraint: make them want to listen; don't over-describe.
- **Club context counts as culture**: city, venue, residency, DJs who play it. Club bookings signal "cultural relevance, not just streaming metrics" ([Total Audio Promo](https://totalaudiopromo.com/resources/electronic-music-pr/electronic-music-playlist-pitching-guide-for-spotify-apple-and-deezer)). This fits Spotify's "context and community" line.
- **Sound-alike = a track already on the target playlist**, plus why this one sits next to it (Total Audio Promo).
- **Micro-genre terms show you "get the genre"** (Jesse Cannon).
- **BPM**: several guides say put BPM (and key) in the text. ⚠️ This clashes with our rule (no key ever; no BPM in copy unless Amir asks). The pitch is never public. Amir decides.
- **Electronic editorial playlists named in sources** (none verified yet): mint, Altar, Electronic Rising, Housewerk, Melodic Techno, REC, Diggin'. None of them is obviously dark disco.
- **Our own archive**: The Deceiver pitch (06-15, 393 chars) = feeling words + sound + "Produced in Berlin" + "belongs in late night EBM, industrial and dark techno sets" + series line. Tags were EBM/Techno/Industrial, Energetic, Synthesizer, instrumental. Whether it got any editorial placement isn't recorded.
