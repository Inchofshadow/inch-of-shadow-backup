## 3. Career Goals — Priority Order

### Goal 0 — Grow Audience and Revenue (TOP PRIORITY)
Increase followers, listeners, streams, and sales across all platforms. This is the metric that unlocks everything else — labels, management, bookings, festivals all look at numbers.

Numbers are proof. Proof that people care about the music. Every other goal (labels, management, agency, festivals) becomes easier with growing numbers. This isn't vanity — it's leverage.

**Key targets:**
- Grow followers on Instagram, SoundCloud, Spotify, Bandcamp, YouTube, Beatport
- Grow Spotify monthly listeners, saves, and playlist placements
- Generate revenue through Bandcamp sales, Beatport/Traxsource purchases, and streaming royalties

**Platform priority for underground electronic:**
1. Bandcamp — direct sales, fan email list, Bandcamp Fridays = zero commission
2. Beatport/Traxsource — DJ charts, credibility in electronic music scene
3. Instagram — visual presence, direct artist-to-fan connection
4. SoundCloud — reposts, community engagement, free tracks build audience
5. Spotify — editorial playlists, algorithmic reach, saves/follows matter
6. YouTube — music videos, visualizers, premieres, evergreen discovery

### Goal 1 — Build a Professional Catalog
Release consistently in 2026. Every release airtight — artwork, metadata, SEO, platform presence, pre-release campaign. 3 singles/EPs in 2026. Each goes through the full Release Manager pipeline.

A manager, label, or booking agent needs a catalog to evaluate. The catalog is the product. Everything else is marketing the product.

### Goal 2 — Get DJ Support from the Right People
Get tracks played and charted by DJs in the scene — dark disco, acid, electro, EBM circles.

In underground electronic, DJ support is the primary discovery channel. A track in the right DJ's set or chart is worth more than 10,000 Spotify streams. It signals to labels, promoters, and audiences that you belong.

**What "done" looks like:**
- Promo copies sent to 30-50 high-match DJs (score 4-5 in SI) before each release
- Tracks appearing in DJ charts on Beatport, Traxsource, RA
- Feedback from DJs
- Growing network who expect your promos

### Goal 3 — Secure Media Coverage and Premieres
Get tracks premiered on blogs, YouTube channels, and podcasts covering your genres. Get reviews and features.

Premieres give reach beyond existing audience. A premiere on a respected channel validates music to labels and managers. Press is permanent — it shows up when people search your name.

**What "done" looks like:**
- At least 1 premiere per EP on a relevant blog/channel
- Reviews or features on 2-3 media outlets per year
- Press kit ready to send at any time

### Goal 4 — Get Label Releases
Sign 1-2 EPs or tracks to established labels in your genre. Self-release the rest.

A label release gives credibility, reach, and their promotional machine. Mix strategy: self-release some, label some — keeps independence while leveraging label infrastructure.

**What "done" looks like:**
- Demo submissions to 15-20 aligned labels (filtered from SI by score 4-5)
- At least 1 label offer or release deal by end of 2026
- Relationship with 2-3 label A&Rs who know your sound

### Goal 5 — Sign with Management
Find management that understands underground electronic and can handle bookings, strategy, and industry connections.

Management frees you to focus on music. A good manager opens doors you can't open alone. In electronic music, management often comes after you've proven you can release and perform consistently.

### Goal 6 — Secure Booking Agency and Regular Gigs
Sign with a booking agency. Expand from Berlin to other European cities. Both DJ sets and live shows.

A booking agent gets you into venues and festivals you can't access directly. They handle negotiations and have relationships with promoters across Europe.

### Goal 7 — Play Festivals (Summer 2027 Target)
Secure 2-3 festival slots for summer 2027. Dark disco / acid / techno / EBM festivals and stages.

Start with smaller festivals that book emerging artists, local Berlin all-nighters, ADE off-program showcases, and label showcase slots. Most festival submission deadlines October-January for summer.

## 4. Genre Transition — Underground to Pop

### The Timeline

**Now — Month 7:** Underground electronic phase. Release singles every 4-6 weeks. Build catalog, Spotify followers, SoundCloud community, Instagram audience, Bandcamp email list, Beatport DJ credibility.

**Month 8:** The Bridge. Release a transition single.

**Month 9+:** Singer-songwriter / pop production era. Amir will sing his own songs. Pop-leaning production.

### Why the Transition Works

This is not a contradiction — it's a strategic evolution. The underground electronic catalog:
1. Builds real algorithmic history on Spotify
2. Builds a real email list via Bandcamp
3. Builds real Instagram followers and engagement
4. Builds real scene credibility (DJ support, Beatport, RA)

All of this carries forward. The pop phase launches into an existing audience that already trusts Amir's taste, not into a cold room.

### What Changes vs What Stays the Same

**What changes in pop phase:**
- Primary discovery platform shifts: SoundCloud/Instagram → Instagram/YouTube/(TikTok)
- Spotify pitch genre changes: dark disco/acid → indie pop/electropop/dream pop
- Content type changes: atmospheric/aesthetic → personal, lyric-focused, vocal performance
- Instagram hooks change: sonic/aesthetic → emotional resonance, lyric lines
- YouTube content changes: visualizers, DJ sets → lyric videos, acoustic versions, vlogs
- Press targets expand: underground electronic blogs → general indie music media, lifestyle press
- Beatport: primary in electronic phase → less relevant in pop phase
- TikTok: optional now → important in pop phase

**What stays the same (the mechanics don't change):**
- The cross-platform flywheel works identically
- Consistent release cadence still drives algorithmic growth
- Save rate still drives Spotify algorithm
- Bandcamp + email list still highest-value owned channel
- Meta ads still primary paid growth tool
- YouTube still long-term SEO and catalog
- The 60-minute Instagram engagement rule
- The SoundCloud 48-hour upload window
- Budget framework

### The Transition — Two Approaches

**Option A: Sonic Bridge Single**
Release one track that is clearly "you" but introduces the vocal/pop element. Something fans of the electronic work can hear and understand as an evolution, not a break. This gives your existing audience a reason to follow you forward.

Precedent: TSO (Dazed's 2026 artists to watch) — started with alt-pop, transitioned into electronic beats gradually. Each release built on the previous identity rather than abandoning it.

**Option B: Clear Era Announcement**
Be transparent with your audience about the evolution. A Reel or Instagram post: "I've been making electronic music. I've also been writing songs. This is where those two worlds meet." Audiences respect artistic evolution when it's framed honestly.

**The recommendation:** Option A or a combination. The sonic bridge single is the most powerful tool because it lets the music communicate the transition rather than you explaining it.

## 6. TikTok — The Pop Platform

TikTok is not covered in detail in the marketing guide because it is not essential for underground electronic. It becomes important for the pop/singer-songwriter phase.

### Why TikTok for Pop Specifically

- Gen Z and millennial pop listeners discover heavily via TikTok
- Lyric clips and emotional "this hits different" content virals on TikTok more than any other format
- TikTok → Spotify conversion is direct and well-documented: TikTok is for discovery, Spotify is for depth
- Singer-songwriter vulnerability content (writing process, studio moments, emotional reactions) is TikTok's native language
- Every step between TikTok and Spotify loses 50-70% of potential listeners — your job is to minimize friction

### When to Activate

Not now. Start preparing content approach when recording the pop material. **Launch TikTok 6-8 weeks before your first pop single.**

### How TikTok Fits the Ecosystem

TikTok acts as a second discovery layer alongside Instagram Reels, reaching a slightly different (younger, more pop-oriented) demographic. The content can be adapted from Instagram Reels but must feel native to TikTok — trending sounds, duets, text-based hooks, vulnerability, "this lyric describes my life" formats.

### TikTok → Spotify Conversion

The technical mechanism is similar to Instagram:
1. Use your music as audio in TikToks
2. Viewers who like the sound search for it on Spotify
3. They save it
4. Spotify sees external traffic spike + saves = quality signal

The bio must always contain a smart link (Feature.fm) pointing to Spotify. CTA in every video description: "Full song on Spotify — link in bio."

### TikTok Profile Setup (For When You Launch)

- Same artist name: Inch of Shadow
- Bio: genre + location + current single
- Link in bio: Feature.fm smart link (same one used across all platforms)
- Content: 15-30 seconds, native vertical, original audio preferred

## 7. The Audience Carry-Forward Strategy

The most important strategic insight for the transition: **your existing audience is an asset, not a liability.**

Underground electronic fans who followed your journey to this point are more engaged than any cold pop audience you'll build. They already invested in you as an artist. If the transition is handled with integrity, they stay — and they become early advocates for the new material.

### What Loses Audiences in a Transition

- A sudden, unexplained genre shift with no acknowledgment
- Pop music that feels like a cynical commercial pivot
- Abandoning the aesthetic/visual identity that attracted them
- Stopping activity in the scene you came from (no more SoundCloud, no more DJ sets, no more engaging with the community)

### What Retains Audiences Through a Transition

- Transparently communicating the evolution (the story of how you got here)
- A sonic bridge single that honors both identities
- Continued engagement with the scene you came from (DJing, supporting artists, SoundCloud activity)
- Visual identity that evolves rather than resets
- Framing it as growth, not abandonment: "I've been making this music. I'm also making this other music. Both are me."

### The Math

Your underground catalog builds:
- X Spotify followers → all become Release Radar recipients for first pop single
- Y Bandcamp email list → all can be messaged about first pop release
- Z Instagram followers → all see the first pop Reel
- SC community → all follow you to hear what's next

The pop phase does not start from zero. It starts from the foundation built by the electronic phase. Every person in your audience already trusts your taste. The transition asks them to follow your evolution — not start over.
