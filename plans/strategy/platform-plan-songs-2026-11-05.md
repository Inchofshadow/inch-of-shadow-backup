## The short answer on "which platform is the engine"

No platform is the engine. The song is. Almost every breakout in our scene that the research found grew
through other people picking a song up (TikTok edits and outfit clips, a cover, a festival or venue posting
the clip), often years after release; one (ThxSoMch) started with the artist's own screen recording of his
session [REPORTED]. What a platform does is give that pickup a place to happen and a way to turn into a fan
we keep. So each platform gets one job, and the measure for each job is different. TikTok can carry real
exposure, through others using the sound and through our own raw posts (section below), but its followers
turn into listeners at about half the rate of other platforms [REPORTED, MIDiA: 26% vs 45%]. So TikTok is
where exposure can come from; Spotify and the email list are where it has to land.

## Why these jobs and not others

- **Spotify holds the fans** because a follower gets one song per artist per week in Release Radar [OFFICIAL],
  and since July listeners can filter that playlist [OFFICIAL], so the follow is necessary and the day-28
  engagement number tells us whether it still worked. Countdown Pages (3,000 listeners, EPs and albums only) and
  Marquee (5,000) are closed to us [OFFICIAL]; Discovery Mode is reported at about 25,000 [CLAIMED]. No paid
  shortcut at 94 listeners.
- **Bandcamp is the paying core** because it has no feed to lose a fan in, it pays per sale, and its own guide
  asks for 8 weeks' notice with a draft link [OFFICIAL]. The 10-21 Bandcamp plan already stands.
- **Instagram brings traffic** because the 17 profiles read today show the same thing at every size: a face or
  hands into the lens beats everything, announcements are the floor, and the largest numbers come from
  collabs started by bigger accounts [SEEN]. Nearly 45% of recommended Reels are under a day old [REPORTED,
  four write-ups of Meta's Q3 call; the transcript itself was not read], so the release morning is the moment.
- **TikTok makes the sound usable** because that is how Molchat Doma, Lebanon Hanover, Mareux and Sinnerella
  grew there: other people's videos, not the artist's own posts [REPORTED]. The artist's own account mattered
  for certification, the Music Tab and the clip choice [OFFICIAL]. Boy Harsher have 177K YouTube subscribers
  and 8.7K on TikTok [SEEN]: the scene does not live there as followers.
- **YouTube carries the video** because on 13 channels in our scene one finished official video per single
  sits at the top and every teaser, "out now" and ticket Short sits at the bottom [SEEN]. Shorts Previews let
  pre-release Shorts carry the real audio with a "Notify me" button [OFFICIAL].
- **SoundCloud stays the electronic home** because our 470 followers came from electronic seeds and SC's own
  2026 report names hard techno, rap and Gen Z indie as its growing scenes, not post-punk [OFFICIAL]. Its new
  follower-only release tool is the one songs-lane use that costs nothing [OFFICIAL].

## Could TikTok still turn out to be the big engine? (Amir, 6 Nov: "TikTok can carry, we may get exposed there")

Yes, and the plan treats it as a real carrier, not only a sound library. Two paths run at once:

1. **The sound path** (the one the scene evidence shows): the sound live on TikTok through Symphonic, the
   right 60 seconds chosen per song, clean clips others can duet or reuse, and "videos by others" watched for
   months. This is what grew Molchat Doma, Lebanon Hanover, Mareux and Sinnerella there [REPORTED].
2. **The own-account path** (the exposure Amir means): the same reels we make for Instagram, posted clean on
   TikTok 2-3 times a week during a content round, in the formats TikTok's own material names for musicians
   (performance clips, a real place, the story behind a song, text for muted viewers) [OFFICIAL]. Face and
   process into the lens, as on IG. ThxSoMch's first viral post was a screen recording of his own session
   [REPORTED]: own-account exposure does happen for one-person acts, and it starts with raw work, not polish.

What makes it an engine if it catches: the result must land where we keep it. Every TikTok clip carries the
song's name and the sound page; the bio links to Spotify and the email list; TikTok for Artists gives song and post
analytics [OFFICIAL]; whether "Add to Music App" saves show per song there is to check in the app. What we still cannot do: buy it or plan on it, and TikTok
followers turn into listeners at about half the rate of other platforms [REPORTED, MIDiA 26% vs 45%], so the
scoreboard stays Spotify followers and the email list even if TikTok is where the exposure comes from.

Timing: the account opens with the first song (D8), certified with a track already out; the go/no-go after
8 weeks (TikTok research) stays, measured on both paths: videos by others per sound, and own-account reach
and saves.
