Robert Smith · The Cure

artist + engineer saySmith and Steve Lyon, 1996 · Mike Hedges, 1980

The voice, in Smith's words (1996)

  • He has used an AKG C12 since Wish, because it is much more forgiving.
  • His voice has a hard edge which peaks at about 3kHz.
  • The only outboard he knows he used on three albums is a Massenburg 8200 EQ, used for notching out rather than adding.
  • Lyon: he lives with a vocal for 3-4 weeks, then sings it through. The backing vocals take 20-30 minutes because he knows his own voice so well.

Source: SOS, The Cure: recording Wild Mood Swings

'A Forest' / Seventeen Seconds (1980), Mike Hedges

  • Vocal on a Neumann FET 47. One take; if it was wrong, it was redone. Very little comping.
  • The vocals sit deep in a reverb-heavy mix.
  • Stereo tape delay with two times: left into right, both back to the desk, and a fade between the half-speed and double-speed repeats.
  • Up to seven flangers following the envelope of the sound, plus backwards reverb.

Source: SOS Classic Tracks: The Cure 'A Forest'

Compression, de-esser, distortion?

No engineer source names any of them for his voice. I searched both SOS pieces, the Faith production notes (they only say his vocals sounded "too happy" and were finished at Abbey Road) and the Disintegration coverage (no David M. Allen gear interview turned up).

  • The Wild Mood Swings studio gear list includes a Urei 1178 and a Focusrite Red 3 compressor, but nobody says they were on his voice. ❓
  • His own rule points the other way: notch the 3 kHz edge, don't add.
  • The 1980 grit is in the effects (flangers, tape delay on tape delay, backwards reverb), not in a vocal distortion.

❓ Live 2007: an SM58 for decades, and a Fairchild 660 among the front-of-house vocal plug-ins. This comes from a search snippet only, because the AudioTechnology page blocked our text fetch.

Dave Gahan · Depeche Mode

engineer saysMarta Salogni · Memento Mori, 2023

Tracking

Neumann U87 → API pre → LA-2A → Massive Passive EQ
  • He sings into effects that are already set up, some of them quite extreme: tape machines, Eventide H3000, Dimension-D, AMS RMX16, Lexicon.
  • The vocal is recorded dry, and each effect is printed on its own track.
  • The booth was made of rugs and mic stands, and later a heavy curtain booth.

Mix inserts, in her order

EQ → comp → EQ → de-esser → EQ → Devil-Loc → Radiator → Soothe → EQ + Valhalla Delay

There is a lot of volume automation, including the ends of phrases, so the words cut through. The effects are automated per section.

Source: SOS Inside Track: Depeche Mode 'Memento Mori'

What people online do tutorials

billyxo, "how to do crystal castles type vocals" (YouTube, 2024, about 31k views). I read it from the auto-captions and frames:

Auto-Tune retune 0, key set, transpose +1 + a harmony layer under it → gate → comp → multiband, high band only → limiter → EQ → flanger → convolver "ghost" → delay → sidechain-gated rhythm
  • The transpose is there to pitch it up, sound more like a female. He sings one note lower so it lands on pitch.
  • The layer vocal uses the same chain, but a reverb instead of the convolver, plus a stereo widener at about 70%.

forum guess Gearspace (search summary only ❓): distortion, a short spring reverb on the highs, and upper mids boosted so the yell has presence. One reply says the oldest tracks stack several takes and run the yells through Auto-Tune.

Weak source: an affiliate plug-in blog suggests Little AlterBoy for pitch and formant, SketchCassette, Valhalla Delay + VintageVerb, RC-20, and clipping the vocal until it breaks.

Lana Del Rey

engineer saysKieron Menzies + Dean Reid · Lust For Life, 2017 · Laura Sisk · Norman Fucking Rockwell!, 2019

Lust For Life: tracking

Shure SM7B → BAE 1073 → UREI 1176 (+ a flat API 550B just for the sound; no EQ on the way in)
  • She chose the SM7B after years of looking, because it has the dark aesthetic that she likes.
  • She monitors through the AIR Spring Reverb so she hears herself the way she will sound on the record. Menzies calls that reverb one of the characteristic parts of her vocal sound.
  • She records 3-4 takes, usually keeps take 2 or 3, and there is very little comping. The stacked backing vocals define much of her sound.

Lust For Life: mix starting point (Reid)

de-esser → EQ (EQ3 or Waves SSL) → LA-2A / CLA-2A → Waves C4 'Pensado Vocal' → sends to effects

Spoken parts get a FutzBox radio sound. A vocoder track runs on Waves Morphoder. Valhalla VintageVerb is used a lot.

Source: SOS Inside Track: Lana Del Rey 'Lust For Life'

Norman Fucking Rockwell!: Laura Sisk

Wunder CM7 S (U47 copy) → 1073 → Tube-Tech CL-1B fixed 1 ms / 50 ms, 3-5 dB max

In the box: barely any EQ, a little more compression, and reverb per song. The mic's dip at 8 kHz helps with sibilance on quiet takes.

Source: SOS: Laura Sisk, recording Lana Del Rey's Norman album

The Weeknd

engineer saysCarlo 'Illangelo' Montagnese · 'The Hills', 2015 · Ethan Stevens · 'Creepin'', 2022

He is recorded raw, with no EQ and no compressor, and only checked for clipping. The mic may have been a U67, but it doesn't matter as I ended up treating the vocals so heavily.

'The Hills': verse lead inserts

Auto-Tune EFX (subtle) → Pro-DS → Pro-Q2 (lo-fi roll-off) → Vocal Rider → API 560 "fuzz" → Pro-MB, pumping
  • Parallel: Little Radiator for overdrive, then a UAD 1176 slamming it, blended under the lead.
  • Five sends: Altiverb with H-Delay pre-delay; Doubler → MicroShift, with the top cut so the doubled esses stay out of the way; long filtered Timeless tape delays.
  • Chorus: the same core plus Reel ADT, Decapitator, Volcano and a flanger. The verse is soft and low and the chorus is screamed high, so it had to sound overdriven.
  • 'Creepin'': a long reverb, then mids only, then Saturn 2, then a compressor ducked by the dry vocal, so the tail opens up in the gaps.

Sources: SOS Inside Track: The Weeknd · SOS Inside Track: Metro Boomin 'Heroes & Villains'

Where Auto-Tune goes

engineers saySOS 'Vocal Fixes', Oct 2003: an old article, but the methods still apply
  • As a colour, in parallel. Josh Binder runs a chromatic Auto-Tune with a mellow response even on singers who are in tune, for a chorus/flanging effect. He prints it and blends it into the comped vocal: It's not there to fix the vocal; it's there to be part of the vocal sound.
  • On the monitor path while recording. Donal Hodgson (Sting, Counting Crows): it helped one singer stop worrying about pitch and relax. In the mix he automates bypass and response time so only some syllables get fixed.
  • On what the singer hears, not on the take. Seth Firkins (Future's engineer, SOS 2017) always has it on, but on an aux the singer hears: Auto-Tune → quarter-note delay → room reverb at 12-15% wet with 12 ms pre-delay. His confidence is up, and he can experiment a lot more. He prints the Auto-Tune afterwards. His mix bus order is EQ (top lifted above 5.2 kHz) → de-esser at 5.5 kHz → compressor → effects.
  • First and subtle. Illangelo puts Auto-Tune at the top of The Weeknd's chain, before the de-esser and EQ.
  • Light, and against the track. Joe Barresi (Queens of the Stone Age) keeps the correction close to the real pitch, nudges the track 2-4 ms earlier to make up for the delay, and tunes while the track plays.

Source: SOS: Vocal Fixes, Modern Vocal Processing In Practice

The Weeknd, After Hours: your exact setup engineer says

Shure SM7B → Apollo Twin preamp → sometimes Manley VOXBOX plug-in → everything else in plug-ins, in Ableton

Illangelo: they tried a Sony C800, but the SM7B hit better. He bought a Tube-Tech CL 1B, but the small Apollo + SM7B rig was easier, faster and better. You have the SM7B, the Apollo Twin and UAD Manley VOXBOX (Unison, needs the Apollo ❓).

Source: Mix: Illangelo on producing 'After Hours' (2021)

Depeche Mode live, Memento Mori tour engineers say

  • Front of house: a short plate for depth, with a second, characterful reverb blended in.
  • Monitors: a UAD Manley VoxBox on Dave, and a chain of Shadow Hills pre → tape emulator → API 550A. Mic: an SM58.

Source: Mix: Mixing Depeche Mode's Memento Mori world tour (2024)

Lana Del Rey tutorial

EQ: shelf +10k, boost ~5k, cut 1.1k + 272 Hz → comp 4.4:1, low threshold → send 1: 2.5 s plate + 1/4 echo, a bit louder L → send 2: long reverb, automated up on key lines

Tuning: no automatic tuner, because it kills her bends. Use Melodyne or the Auto-Tune graph and don't push notes to perfect pitch: Del Rey's style is to sing slightly flat.

Source: MusicRadar: drowsy Lana Del Rey-style vocals (2012)

The Cure, by ear forum guess

  • A normal reverb with a good pre-delay, so the tail doesn't run over the voice.
  • Heavy compression on a close-miked, breathy vocal. This is the only source anywhere that names compression on Smith, and it's a guess by ear.
  • A long ping-pong delay into reverb, EQ'd thin, about 3 repeats, fully wet.

Source: homerecording.com thread

The Weeknd and Travis blogs and preset sellers

  • The Weeknd: retune at about 15-25 ms for natural tuning; a short plate with 30-60 ms pre-delay at 10-15% wet; delays with the highs rolled off; reverb and delay ducked under the voice (RysUp).
  • Travis: retune 0, Auto-Tune on the dry vocal first, then delays, then reverb. The delay is doing as much work as the pitch correction (Antares blog). A trailing echo on its own track at about 500 ms with 20-30% feedback. The sources disagree on the reverb.
  • Crystal Castles: still no vocal source. Tutorials suggest a parallel bitcrushed copy, filtered narrow, under the clean voice ❓.

Nick Cave · Push The Sky Away

engineer saysNick Launay, 2013
Neumann M49 → Neve 1081 → Tube-Tech CL1B → Pro Tools
  • Every vocal is an original take, sung live with the band. Cave plays piano while he sings, so the piano mics sit taped inside the closed piano.
  • Mix: a little spring reverb, an EMT plate or a Roland RE-301 slap: I used the same effects on the vocals.
  • He prefers a Tube-Tech over an 1176 on vocals, because the 1176 brings out the esses. The Tube-Tech sounds very warm and works great on Nick's vocals.
  • Plug-ins: Waves DeEsser, an EQ used for notches, Crystallizer and Devil-Loc: I do a lot of controlled distortion!

Source: Paul Tingen: Nick Cave & The Magical Fabrique (AudioTechnology, 2013)

Midwest emo · Sunny Day Real Estate, American Football

band members, not engineersNo vocal chain documented anywhere

❓ I read "Real Estate" as Sunny Day Real Estate, and "Football Party" as American Football.

What the chains share

  • A dynamic mic for loud, hard singers (Talbot, Casey, and Lana's SM7B for darkness). Condensers for the soft parts.
  • One colour compressor early (LA-2A, Distressor, CL-1A/1B), often with a dirty copy blended in parallel.
  • Cut the harsh band instead of boosting (Smith at 3 kHz, Salogni with soothe).
  • Effects are part of the performance: Gahan and Lana sing into them.
  • Riding the level by hand matters as much as the plug-ins (Salogni, Tolomei).
  • 1073 → CL-1B shows up three times (Lana, Travis, Future). You own both.
