Inch of Shadow, a Berlin artist: light and shadow held together in one frame ("one inch of love is one inch of shadow").
One light source you can point to (a lamp, the moon, a flash, a window, a low sun), with a clear direction and real shadows. Never flat, even, commercial light.
Deep shadows that keep their detail, and at least one area that falls close to black. Daylight and warmth are allowed; the image does not have to be dark overall.
Few elements, each with a reason to be there: one focal point with calm space around it. Nothing decorative added, nothing random.
Surfaces look real and touched by time: matte stone, paper, fabric, skin with texture, film grain. Nothing glossy, plastic or airbrushed.
The feeling comes from the light and the space: quiet, intimate, a little mysterious, tender rather than grim.
A person only when the subject asks for one; then distant and self-possessed, not posing for the camera, never explicit. If a real person is in the image, keep their face exactly as it is.
No text, letters, logos or watermarks anywhere.
