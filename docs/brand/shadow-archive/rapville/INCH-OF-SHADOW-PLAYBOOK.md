# The RAPVILLE method, in Inch of Shadow's world

What this is: his method ([PLAYBOOK.md](PLAYBOOK.md)) translated into Amir's brand, language and
style, for the songs. Written 2026-10-11 from the brand files (`positioning.md`, `voice.md`,
`proof.md`, `frame.md`, `formats.md`, `reel-craft.md`, `motion-library.md`), the studio and
location notes, and the bridge plan. Where a brand file already decides something, it wins.
Where his method and the brand pull apart, both are shown and the call is Amir's.

Four labels run through the file:
- **KEEP:** his rule fits as it is.
- **TRANSLATE:** the principle stays, the execution changes to fit Inch of Shadow.
- **OUT:** not for us, with the reason.
- **AMIR:** a decision only he can make. All of them are collected at the end.

Nothing here claims to know what any song feels like. Every place that needs the song's
feeling is a blank that Amir fills per song.

## 2. Brand and world

**The three A's (Art, Artist, Audience). TRANSLATE.** Already answered, in the brand's own
words:
- **Art:** the voice on every song, through reverb and delay; a beat that leans new wave and
  post-punk around 110 BPM; a chorus you can sing back; lyrics between realism and fantasy
  about love, self-reflection, energy and cycles (`proof.md`). His "consistent emotional
  anchor" = the mood chosen per release.
- **Artist:** one person who writes, plays, sings, produces, mixes and masters everything.
  That is the "unique angle nobody can replicate" in his sense, and it is already how the
  bios will read after the switch. Character: present but not explaining; confident and out
  of reach on one side, vulnerable on the other (`positioning.md`, internal tension, kept
  not solved). Visual identity: sunglasses, tailoring or leather, one light idea per image.
- **Audience:** mixed on purpose, "not one scene and not one age", and the brand RULE says no
  category is ever put on it. See the one-person note below.

**"Your one person" paragraph. TRANSLATE, with a line to respect.** He asks for a written
portrait of one listener: age, what their day feels like, what they scroll and share, what
they would send a friend at midnight, why they would listen to you. The brand forbids a tag
on the audience in any pitch or brief. These can both hold: the paragraph is an **internal
writing tool, never published, and it describes one person, not a scene**. It would sit in
`positioning.md` under INTERNAL. **AMIR 1:** does he want to write it, and is it one person
or two (one younger, one his own generation)?

**Archetype. TRANSLATE.** His wheel (outlaw, lover, sage, jester) is a shortcut; the brand
already has the richer version: the Lana Del Rey lesson (familiar symbols, luxury handled
with taste), the MJK lesson (depth, no explaining), Depeche Mode as the model of dark pop for
the masses. If one word is useful for briefs, the closest on his wheel is **lover with a
shadow**. Not a public label.

**"Let people in" vs mysterious. TRANSLATE.** He says mystery only works for earned icons.
The brand's answer is already precise: Amir is more present than his model, he will be on
camera and on Instagram and TikTok, and what he keeps is that he does not explain himself or
the songs. So the rule for every piece: **show, never explain.** Face, studio, playing, the
city, yes. Talking about meaning, no. That is "letting people in" done his way.

**World per song. KEEP, it is already the system.** Per release: a mood pair, accents sampled
from the artwork, maybe a title face, maybe one borrowed look (S1 to S7), one light idea,
1 to 2 locations. His addition worth taking: **a signature object or detail per song**
(his examples: a blue Mustang, a red shirt, a pot and pan on one lyric). The brand has
sunglasses as the constant; a per-song object would be the variable. **AMIR 2:** does each
song get one object or motif, chosen with the mood? (The Look-04 motif list already exists:
flowers and roses, cast shadows, angels and statues, moon, sun, stars, streetlight.)

**Premise congruence. KEEP.** The song's premise and the video's premise must match down to
props, outfit and colour; a heartbreak song should not look lit and rich. This is the pop
rule from the other side and it costs nothing.

**Micro-genre, be first mover. TRANSLATE.** The coined phrase "dark dance wave" is the
brand's version: ownable, nobody else uses it, sits on top of the real tags. His point adds
one thing: the content should make the lane visible, not only the tags. The mix itself
(synths, guitar, one voice, a dance beat) is the "two worlds in one lane".

**Costume test:** "if I put on one of your outfits, would people know it's you?" With
sunglasses plus tailoring or leather, yes. Keep it that predictable. His "visual no man's
land" warning (not bad enough to laugh at, not good enough to remember) is the risk of a
tasteful but quiet look. The answer in the brand is the one light idea and the symbol.

**OUT:** attractive extras as perfume-ad logic; "fake it till you make it" crowds; building
an alter ego with a new name. The real name is already used selectively and the person is
the brand.

## 3. Finding ideas

**The idea workstation. KEEP.** Three things open at once: the song, the brand document, a
bank of reference videos. We have all three: the song files, `frame.md` plus
`positioning.md`, and the Shadow Archive. **Build the bank:** every reel Amir saves goes into
the archive with one line on its skeleton (R-001 onwards already works this way).

**Bare bones and remix. KEEP, it is the archive's own rule.** Strip a reference to its
skeleton (normal moment -> unexpected turn -> the song enters), rebuild it with Amir's lyric
and world. Never copy frame for frame; his own data says copies flop. The archive stays a
source of principles, never of shots.

**Outliers against baseline. KEEP.** When picking references, choose videos that did far
better than that creator's usual, not big accounts' usual output. Check with the view count
against their follower count.

**The cinema-scene question. KEEP, it is the best single tool in his method for us.** "If
this song played behind a movie scene, what is on screen?" It gives setting, mood, era,
colours, characters, how many people. For Inch of Shadow it feeds straight into the mood
choice (Look-03), the image options (Look-04), the location and the signature object. It is
also a question Amir can answer without explaining the song's meaning: a scene is not an
explanation. **AMIR 3:** answer it per song, one or two sentences each, as the first step of
every song's content plan.

**Lyric visualisation. TRANSLATE.** His artists act out a lyric. Amir does not act. But the
lyrics are the brand's edge, so the lyric is still the source of the image: a line becomes
a place, an object, a light, a gesture a person who is not acting can make (lighting a
cigarette, taking the sunglasses off, a hand into the water, a cast shadow). The lyric reel
(object 9) already does the typographic version. **AMIR 4:** which lines per song are
"filmable" without acting? He marks them; nobody else can.

**Content pillars. TRANSLATE.** His three pillars per artist. From the brand and the data, a
first proposal (his to change):
1. **The craft:** studio, hands, screens, the finished moment vs how it was built. His best
   reel so far.
2. **The person in the city:** Neukölln at night, the cemetery, Körnerpark, Tempelhofer
   Feld; sunglasses; one light.
3. **The words:** a lyric on screen, in the brand type, on a calm area of the picture.
Carousels (object 7, the reveal with the face on slide 3) and the Letter for Listeners sit on
top for the base. **AMIR 5:** are these the three, or is one wrong?

**Five boxes per idea. KEEP as the idea sheet:** format, concept, hook, transition moment,
performance. Plus the brand's own two: one light idea, one text effect.

**Result first, hook second. KEEP.** Design the shot you want, then design the opening that
makes a stranger want to see it.

**Pitch many, shoot few. KEEP.** 30 ideas on paper, a few chosen, each done well. That fits
the brand's craft bar (reels are the high-craft items, posts can be loose).

**OUT:** prank formats, fake-candid Snapchat captions, "what in the Bollywood movie"
cultural bait, picturing the comment-section fight you want (his "debate" bait). The copy
rules forbid clickbait and the brand does not stage.

## 4. Hooks

**His hook has three parts: spoken, visual, text. TRANSLATE to two, plus sound.** Amir does
not talk on camera, so the spoken hook is out. What is left is exactly what the brand
already measures: **the opening frame** (contrast with one colour accent, sudden movement,
direct eye contact: all three verified in `reel-craft.md`) and **large text in frame one**
for muted viewers (a [TEST] there, so it gets measured on the first song pieces). The third
part, his, is **sound**: the track under the hook echoey and quiet, clean and full at the
moment it lands. That is the reel-craft [TEST] "duck or cut the track before the key moment"
with a recipe attached.

**Text hooks in his voice.** His text hooks are "Bro what did I just witness?". Not here.
The brand's text is plain, first person, no "POV:", no "wait for it". So a text hook is **a
fact or a line of the song**: the lyric itself, "mixed at 4am", the place, the date once it
is public. Words from `voice.md`, set in the brand face, on a calm area, inside the safe
zone, never on the face.

**The six emotions. TRANSLATE to the mood words.** His list: love, heartbreak, cringe,
shock, nostalgia, relatability. The brand's: romantic, melancholic, mysterious, dreamy,
nostalgic, restless, warm, vulnerable, confused, sexy, confident. Love, heartbreak and
nostalgia map one to one. Relatability comes through "it has to be real". **Cringe is OUT.
Shock is OUT as a tactic**; a strong symbol can still startle, handled with taste. His rule
that stays: **pick the one emotion before the shoot and write it on the sheet.** For us that
is the mood choice, and it is made per song by Amir.

**"Avoid no man's land", extreme emotion. TRANSLATE.** His spectrum runs from fully still
and emotionless to fully committed, and he says both ends work, only the middle fails. Amir's
natural register is the still end: self-possessed, distant, one gesture. That is a valid
extreme in his own terms. The brand's duality (dark and light together) is not the middle;
it is two extremes in one frame, which is the brand's whole idea. So: **commit to the still
end, and let the light or the symbol carry the other side.**

**Curiosity, not confusion. KEEP.** A stranger must know what is going on in the first second
and want to know what happens next. Same as the pop rule.

**Connectors. TRANSLATE.** His transition formats need an object visible in both shots (a
slap, a towel, a head turn). For a person who does not act, the connectors are **light and
objects, not gags**: a camera flash (T4 is already a title arrival on a flash), a hand that
takes the sunglasses off, a cast shadow, a door, the studio screen, a cigarette lighter, a
street light switching on. HyperFrames can make the cut itself exact. **Allowed effects are
already listed** in `frame.md` (glitch, RGB split, shake, zoom punches, typewriter text).

**Re-hooks inside the piece. KEEP.** The hook is 2 to 5 seconds of a 20 second piece. His
options without acting: cut between angles every few seconds, let the storyline continue
(the shadow keeps moving, the light keeps sweeping), a second visual hook around second 10.
"When something changes in the music, something changes on screen" is what HyperFrames
beat-sync is for.

**Open loops. KEEP, with the proof rule.** A dateless snippet, a process clip cut before the
payoff, a lyric on a slide that only the song answers. Nothing is claimed before its date,
and the songs are not teased in public before the bio switch (`positioning.md`, PUBLIC
STATUS). Until the switch, open loops are shot and stored, not posted.

**OUT:** the "no way that will look good" opener (needs a talking setup), paparazzi phones
entering frame, "hater" extras, Snapchat captions.

## 6. Shoot day

**15 ideas x 2 songs = 30 reels in one day. TRANSLATE to the real numbers.** Two songs with
a similar mood, shot in the same world, doubles the output of one day. With 2 to 3 posts a
week (Formats-04), 30 pieces is 10 to 15 weeks of content, a whole single's campaign and
more. A first batch does not need 30; it needs the trial-reel builds (object 4), the process
reel, the face reel and a stock of open loops. **AMIR 7:** which two songs share a world
well enough to shoot together first?

**Rehearse in the mirror. KEEP, his way.** Not acting rehearsal: decide beforehand who is on
camera (sunglasses on or off for this song), the outfit (tailoring or leather), the one
gesture, the one light. His "pre-shoot brief, one page" is useful and short:
- the song's energy in one word,
- the scene (the cinema-scene answer),
- the one thing only this song allows.
No "meaning in one sentence" line: the song's meaning stays his.

**Pre-production list. KEEP:** idea sheet, locations and route, props, song timestamps on
the phone with a speaker, outfit per idea, who films, a backup idea per location. Keep it in
the release file's folder so the Release Manager checklist can carry it.

**Locations. KEEP, already chosen:** 1 to 2 per day, close together. Neukölln has them
within walking distance: the cemetery next door, the streets at night, Körnerpark early,
Tempelhofer Feld for sky and daylight (the brand is never only dark). Ambertone for the
craft pillar: mixing room only, no instruments on screen, decouple picture from sound, and
the **night version of the room is still unshot** (the studio note asks to be pushed on
this). His line that fits: locations need depth and character; a white wall is the critique
he gives most.

**Who films. TRANSLATE.** His rule: a short-form person, not a music-video specialist, and
good light matters more than the camera. For a first batch: a phone, a tripod, one light,
and Claude's shot list; or a friend who shoots short-form, briefed with the idea sheet (his
15-minute walkthrough becomes a short written brief plus two or three reference clips from
the archive). Shir shoots stills; her photos are the carousel material, not the reels.

**Go / no-go. KEEP.** If the first 45 minutes are lost, stop and reshoot another day. Check
the first clips on the spot: is the hook clear with no sound, is the light the one idea, is
there text room on a calm area.

**Film the setup of everything. KEEP.** It is the S-tier format for free.

**Energy. TRANSLATE.** Still is an extreme. The direction on set is not "more energy", it is
"commit to the stillness": no half-smiles, no fidgeting, one gesture done once. When the
song asks for the other extreme, that is sunglasses off and eye contact, and it carries
weight because it is rare.

**Phone and light. KEEP.** Shoot Log on the phone for the grade, use the room's own lights
(window, floor lamp, salt lamp, the overhead colour light, the LED strips), bring one light
outside. The grade is Look-09, our own recipe measured from her edits; grain last and light.

## 7. Editing

- **Transition timing and "something changes in the music, something changes on screen".
  KEEP.** HyperFrames builds from the beat grid; the first 2 seconds are a variable so hooks
  can be swapped without a rebuild (object 4).
- **Sound design. KEEP, his most useful edit rule.** The song echoey and low under the hook,
  clean and full when the piece lands, foley where a door or a flash happens. Always the real
  bounce laid over the picture, never the phone mic (studio note). On TikTok the sound comes
  from the app's library, so the file carries the music only for Instagram and Shorts
  (Machine-04 open question).
- **Lyric font and colour. KEEP, already fixed:** the brand face (Albertus Nova, Marcellus
  until the file is owned), the release's mood pair, sizes from `frame.md`, calm area, safe
  zone, never on the face. One text effect per video, one title arrival (T1 to T4).
- **Grade. KEEP:** Look-09, matched to the mood; deep blacks placed right, never milky.
- **Edit review with a second pair of eyes. KEEP:** every piece goes through the pre-render
  self-audit in `frame.md` and the render-and-shrink check before it is posted.
- **Re-edit and repost an underperformer. KEEP as a test:** same build, new first 2 seconds.
  That is the trial-reel mechanism used after the fact.
- **The name closes, never opens.** His formats put the artist in frame one; here frame one
  is the hook and the sting is the end. Not negotiable (`motion-library.md`).

## 8. Posting and testing

**Daily posting vs a few pieces a week. DECIDED (Amir, 2026-10-11): 2 to 4 a week,
depending on his time, and this applies to the songs only.** Not to this month or to
Pinocchio. His method assumes a post a day; with 2 to 4 a week a test cycle takes longer, so
the batch shoot matters more: 30 pieces is 8 to 15 weeks of content.

**Seven-piece buffer before posting starts. KEEP.** Nothing goes out until a week of pieces
is rendered. Prevents the gap the data shows (0 posts since mid-July).

**Test formats, then double down. KEEP, with his honest contradictions resolved by our own
rule.** He gives four different grids (4x7, 3 formats, 5x4, 10 in 40). Pick one and hold it:
**4 formats, at least 4 pieces each**, so a format is dropped only after 4 tries. The four
for Inch of Shadow, from section 5: setup vs the shot, the movie scene with a lyric, the
face or performance reel, the carousel. Hooks are tested inside each format by object 4.

**Judge against your own baseline. KEEP.** His winner thresholds disagree with each other
(10x, 50k, any multiple, 20%); the usable version for an account this size is **reach as a
multiple of the last five pieces' average**, plus song response (comments and DMs about the
song, saves, profile visits). The baseline today: reels 341 to 455 reach, 55 to 62%
non-followers, about 6 seconds watched. Measure-02 re-measures everything after the first
song. **No number is ever written down that does not trace to a file** (`ig-kpis`).

**Count comments about the song, not only views. KEEP.** "Empty virality" is his phrase
for reach with no song response. The KPI line already says conversion is the lever.

**Platforms differ. KEEP.** Read each format per platform; the same piece can win on TikTok
and lose on Instagram.

**70/30 after a winner. KEEP.** Seven in ten pieces repeat what worked with a small spin
(a new location, the other song, a different lyric), three in ten try something new. Repeat
a winner until the numbers say the audience is tired, then tweak it, do not abandon it.

**Repost good earlier pieces after traction. KEEP.** Also re-render with a new hook.

**Hashtags "irrelevant". TRANSLATE:** `voice.md` already treats them as labels, 3 to 5 at the
end, no reach expected. Captions: his "first thing that comes to mind" is close to the voice
rule (warm, first person, any length, in the moment). Keep the first 125 characters doing the
work for Instagram search.

**Posting time 4 to 9 pm local. TEST**, nothing own is measured yet; late night was the
electronic-era habit and is marked [ERA].

**Pinocchio as a dry run.** The light lane gets 2 to 3 reels anyway (`formats.md`). Those
can run the loop once at small scale: two hooks tested, comment replies, a pinned comment,
a pre-save, one carousel. The control panel is for the songs and Pinocchio is at most a
test run, so: run the loop, learn the mechanics, draw no conclusions about the songs from it.

## 9. Turning views into listeners

**Reply to every comment that asks about the song, with the name and the date. KEEP,
already his nature:** warm, thankful, first person, naming people. On release day, reply
again to the same comments. His line that this unscalable work is where campaigns die is
worth keeping on the wall.

**Pinned comment with the song name and date. KEEP.** Factual, from `proof.md`, nothing
claimed before its date. **OUT:** planting the question from other accounts (section 12 of
his playbook). The pinned comment is his own.

**Keyword DMs (ManyChat). DECIDED (Amir, 2026-10-11): no service. Replies by hand**, with
the link, warm and in his voice. Revisit only if comment volume makes it impossible.

**The 2-week window. KEEP.** A new follower who hears nothing for two weeks goes cold. With
a buffer and a rhythm this is automatic; without them it is the gap in the data.

**Trust content. TRANSLATE.** His: reactions, direct address, "lots of DMs asking". Here:
the real listening moment (a friend, a crowd, a set), the studio at night, the process, and
the Letter for Listeners for the album. Never "lots of DMs asking" unless it is true and
even then said plainly, once.

**Thank, don't beg. KEEP, it is the voice already:** "Released today. Pure fun to work on
this one." No "go stream", no "please follow". The one explicit ask that the bridge plan
allows: **follow on Spotify** on electronic drops, because every follower is a Release Radar
slot for the first song.

**Stories. KEEP:** repost nice comments and DMs (with the person's yes), the milestone thank
you when a number is real, polls that let followers choose something small (the brand's
"ask directly" works here: which of two photos, not what the song means). Close friends for
the people who always comment. Sell only there, when there is something to sell.

**Open loops and pre-release demand. KEEP after the switch.** Dateless snippets, cut-off
process clips, a lyric with no song behind it yet, repetition of the same hook across weeks
so it is familiar by release day. The first 48 hours: make the crowd visible by replying to
everyone. Before the bio switch nothing about the songs is public, so this part of the plan
starts on the switch date and the pieces are shot and stored ahead.

**Own platforms and an email list. KEEP:** the bridge plan already starts the pre-save and
email list now (Bandcamp followers, the smart link). Discord and scarcity tiers: not now.

**Strategic features, competitors' fans, ads on the best organic piece.** Later. The bridge
plan's order is followers first, then the playlist neighbourhood, then labels when the
songs are finished.

## 10. The song itself

His strongest claim is that the song caps everything, and that is also the brand's: "the
hooks are not an accident, they are the point". What transfers without touching how Amir
writes:
- **Test which part of the song opens a piece.** The sung hook, a lyric that names a place
  or an action, the moment the chorus arrives bigger. Reel-craft already lists "open at the
  strongest bar" as a test. Per song, try two or three entry points.
- **The special section kept short** so people replay it: his observation, worth checking
  against the songs' structure. Not a rule for writing.
- **Lines a person can do on camera** (action, object, place) are the easiest to film. Amir

- **Fresh ears check:** speakers up, sit back, mark where attention drops. Useful for choosing
  the reel's section, and only that.
- **OUT:** opening a song with a loop that is currently viral, "viral sound consulting",
  writing the song for the content, changing the song because the content failed. Music is
  his whole life and the songs are the face to the world; the content serves them, not the
  reverse. If 25 pieces on one song do nothing, his own rule says test another song with the
  same format, and that one stays: the format gets the blame last, the song never gets
  rewritten.
