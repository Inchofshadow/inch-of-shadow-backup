# Her First EP Ever and How She Hit 9M Views in 30 Days
URL: https://youtu.be/po6P7I1gM6U | Length: 9:35 | Views: 682 | Date: 2025-12-10
Chapters: Project launch strategy / Defining the artist brand / Testing viral content formats / Scaling successful experiments / Multi-platform distribution / Music impact and conversion / Conversion tactics and wrap

## Core idea
Case study of Desiree, a film director switching to music with 289k followers from her old niche. The 30-day plan was brand, then experiment, then double down, then convert. They tested formats (and songs) on a first batch of 15 videos, scaled the winner (TV transition with a shadow connector) in a second shoot and cross-posted to all platforms. The song choice decided whether views turned into streams.

## Lessons and tactics
1. (00:14) Switching niches (from film-director content to music) with an existing audience is hard. Treat it as a relaunch.
2. (00:45) Four-phase plan: **brand -> experiment -> double down -> convert**, done within the first 30 days.
3. (01:08) First 48 hours = branding. Desiree = **"girl boss" archetype**, so go as extreme as possible on the brand ("no men allowed", independent queen, sassy). The brand matches what the songs are about (free independent woman, not needing a man, breakup handled by going out with friends).
4. (01:47) First 20 days = content strategy and **format testing** to "build the foundation for virality". Formats tested:
   - **Toxic breakup** with an extra engagement layer: **split screen** of behind-the-scenes vs the high-quality shot.
   - **Sassy performance**: real curlers in her hair, sassy on the phone pretending to call the man.
   - **Transition content**: ex comes to the door with flowers, she throws them back, shuts the door, sings sassily to camera.
   - **TV transition**: she's in front of a TV; a prop casts a shadow on her neck that matches the shadow in the performance shot (filmed on location first, then the TV setup is matched to it). The shadow is the connector.
   - **Clones**: two versions of her plus a text hook.
   - **Visual hook**: someone opens a door to reveal her, zoom into her face for the performance.
5. (03:36) **Two-shoot structure**: shoot 1 = first 15 videos; post them; then shoot 2 = last 15 videos, doubling down on what worked.
6. (03:56) Winner from batch 1: the TV transition with the shadow connector "outperformed completely".
7. (04:06) In the second shoot, test new **spins** of the winning format:
   - **Bandwagon effect spin**: house party, more people in the background = social proof.
   - **Seasonal spin**: Christmas version (mid-November): friends in reindeer outfits, Santa hat, friend throwing fake snow.
8. (04:51) Adapt a format to the season: summer = happy and sunny (as with Eric Bellinger); Halloween = Halloween party in the background; Christmas = as above.
9. (05:55) Post on **all platforms**. It gives more leverage for the time spent recording each idea.
10. (06:11) Test **songs** as well as formats. About 20 videos on the song "Pride" produced one viral video and hardly 2,000 streams. Then a video with a different song brought 10,000 streams overnight / within 48 hours.
11. (06:48) Conversion hacks are pointless on the wrong song. A viral video only gets streams if the song is good. He calls people who say the song doesn't matter "wrong": the art and craft still matter.
12. (07:10) Conversion hack 1, for Twitter and TikTok: **plant** as many comments as you can asking for the song name, reply to all of them, then **pin** the one with the most likes. Illusion formats like the TV transition push people into the comments, and a percentage of those convert to streams.
13. (07:52) Conversion hack 2, for Instagram: set up **ManyChat**, tell people in the caption to comment a specific word (e.g. "send"), and ManyChat auto-sends them a DM (with the song).
14. (09:02) No quick trick: branding work, a whole month of experiments, then double down. You can't think about streams until people are "addicted" to the brand and videos, and in turn the music.

## Examples he uses
- Desiree: claimed 289k followers from her film-director content; title claims 9M views in 30 days, intro says 4M.
- TV transition with bandwagon/house party: claimed 4M+ views on Instagram; Christmas spin claimed only 32k ("disappointing but better than average"); claimed 4.7M views on Twitter for the same content.
- Song "Pride": ~20 videos, one viral, claimed under ~2,000 streams; another song (name unclear): claimed 10,000 streams within 48 hours.
- Eric Bellinger: summer/sunny version of the format.

## Process / checklists
- 30-day launch: days 0-2 branding (archetype, extreme) -> to day 20 shoot 1 (15 videos) and format + song testing -> pick the winner -> shoot 2 (15 videos) with spins of the winner (bandwagon, seasonal) -> cross-post everywhere -> conversion (planted/pinned comments; ManyChat keyword DM).
