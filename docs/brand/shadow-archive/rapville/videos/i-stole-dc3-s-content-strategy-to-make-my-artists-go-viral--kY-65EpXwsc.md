# I stole DC3's content strategy to make my artists go viral
URL: https://youtu.be/kY-65EpXwsc | Length: 16:51 | Views: 1,950 | Date: 2025-07-30 | Presenter: Niall (co-founder)

## Core idea
Music in a genre sounds similar, so visual identity is the separator; think like a content creator, not only a musician. He reverse-engineers DC3 Media: a clear ideal listener, a mission and an enemy, "main character" energy, three content pillars (transitions, performances, carousels), and hijacking culture by "stealing like an artist". Then: find your angle, identity, and a micro-genre with less competition.

## Lessons and tactics
1. (00:23) Problem: everyone posts the same content (generic hooks, music video snippets, performance videos), so there is no visual identity; music within a genre can only differ so much, so visuals are what separate you.
2. (01:04) Growth stalls because you're "too busy trying to be a musician" and not enough a content creator.
3. (01:54) Two types: "content artists" vs "music artists". Push yourself to be content-minded.
4. (02:59) Content is "the highest lever" for the music, translating into streams, not just audience.
5. (03:29) Exercise: write down your three favourite creators (people you genuinely enjoy, or think are about to blow up). Reverse-engineer the why: what specifically you like, why, and how it makes you feel. Then recreate that feeling for your own fans.
6. (04:08) First understand why people buy into the creator. DC3 surface level: owns the screen; clothing style of "playful sophistication" (suit and tie plus flat cap, a deliberate small childish detail); and his Christianity, which gives him a mission and an enemy.
7. (04:53) Decide your ideal listener. DC3's: young Gen Z UK rap lovers who think a bit deeper about life and God. Visuals (nostalgic tracksuit, grey British flags) signal his role.
8. (05:17) Mission and enemy: DC3's enemy is the devil; mission is to redeem UK rap, block haters/industry, carve his own lane. The message comes via lyrics, the reach via funny scenarios and visuals.
9. (05:59) What only he gives: makes you laugh, then makes you think. Entertainment (Snapchat method, funny scenarios) is the dopamine Gen Z wants; then the message lands.
10. (06:41) Music that soothes pain rather than masking it; content engineered to amplify the feeling of the song (the "shiver down your spine").
11. (07:24) Answering the exercise for DC3: starts funny then delivers the message; he sets trends rather than copying and it looks effortless; makes you feel "in on something" (the joke, his world). He is the main character.
12. (07:47) Three content pillars: transitions, performances, carousels (transitions and performances the most important).
13. (08:06) Transitions formula: he is always in turmoil/disaster in the hook (getting punched, phone stolen, starting a house fire), then it transitions on time into the song where he overcomes it.
14. (08:29) Performances are never a plain lip-sync or music video clip; each has a visual hook: phone put to his ear, notes shown next to him, a second DC3 in frame, posters with a text hook. Always engineered for social.
15. (08:52) Carousels act as "checkpoints": moments in his life he wants the audience to see; they let people further into his world.
16. (09:36) Being old-school mysterious no longer works; let people in, be someone they look up to. That is how listeners become superfans, who buy tickets and merch.
17. (09:59) Positioning drives superfans and monetisation. Treat music and content as one thing: create the feeling of the music through the visuals.
18. (10:21) Hijacking culture (he calls it potentially the most important part): DC3 recreates iconic looks (Fresh Prince of Bel-Air, Kendrick Lamar, Stormzy's red tracksuit, Michael Jackson, a Jay-Z song with his Christian twist). Chosen on purpose (e.g. people compare him to Kendrick). Comments notice the references.
19. (11:10) "Steal like an artist": never just steal; add your own brand and twist.
20. (11:34) Finding your angle, ideal-listener questions: what do they love, what do they hate, what is trending for them, what emotions do they want to feel, why listen to you over famous artists.
21. (12:12) Identity (he prefers this word over "brand") questions: what emotions are you trying to make people feel; what is your mission; what is your enemy; what styles and demeanour do you have that are unique.
22. (12:33) Make a mood board: inspiring artists, styles, and moments in culture you can steal like an artist.
23. (12:39) Building strategy per pillar: Transitions: define the feeling your hook provokes (DC3 = turmoil and danger) and match it to the song's feeling (e.g. heartbreak and love song -> heartbreak and love hooks; high-energy song -> shock or danger). Performances: decide your visual hook, your subtle storytelling and your demeanour (DC3: confident, slightly disinterested/"over it", plus energetic dancing); pick what you like doing on camera. Carousels: life checkpoints or an important message.
24. (14:08) Music matters more than anything in the content; if it isn't good enough you won't go viral.
25. (14:12) The more mainstream your music, the more competition. Make it more unique via a "micro genre diagram": main genre (e.g. UK rap / rap) then micro-genres (e.g. jazz rap, nostalgic rap). In a micro-genre you know exactly who you are talking to and can be the best with less competition (DC3 = Christian rap).
26. (15:45) Implementation claim: post every day, with understood positioning, unique angle and a target micro-genre. He contrasts this with artists posting five reels a month of music-video snippets.

## Examples he uses (numbers claimed)

- Andre/Adri: "500 view jail", one content switch -> 710,000 and 100,000-view videos, ~1M views in 7 days; 4,000 -> 30,000 monthly listeners; single "Easy" 116,000 streams; new EP 200,000 streams; selling out small venues.

- Laura: "300 view jail" -> 127,000 on her first video (400x).

## Process / checklists
Positioning workflow:
1. Three favourite creators -> reverse-engineer what/why/how it feels.
2. Ideal listener: love, hate, trending, emotions wanted, why you over famous artists -> unique angle.
3. Identity: emotions, mission, enemy, unique style/demeanour.
4. Mood board: inspiring artists, styles, cultural moments to steal like an artist.
5. Pillars: transitions (feeling formula), performances (visual hook + storytelling + demeanour), carousels (checkpoints).
6. Micro-genre to reduce competition.
7. Post daily.
