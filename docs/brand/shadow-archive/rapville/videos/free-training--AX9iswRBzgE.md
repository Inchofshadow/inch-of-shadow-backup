# Free Training
URL: https://youtu.be/AX9iswRBzgE | Length: 15:03 | Views: 1,241 | Date: 2025-04-24

## Core idea
Mostly a sales VSL for the Rapville growth service. The teachable claims: a phone and Wi-Fi are enough to go viral without ads; content strategy (not equipment or "shadowbans") is the bottleneck; re-editing/re-positioning existing videos can lift them; and songs can be "engineered" to go viral (e.g. starting with a currently viral loop) so others use the sound.

## Lessons and tactics
1. (00:00) Goal framed as first 1M views in 30 days with no ads, no fancy equipment, just a phone and Wi-Fi, using a content strategy built around the music.
2. (00:21) Claim: possible without compromising style, looking cringe, or paying for ads.
3. (02:16) Consistency alone is not enough; be "consistent with great ideas" (Adriano was already posting consistently but had no strategy).
4. (02:28) Pre-/post-release goal: get the audience to make videos with your sound (Slo's sound reached 1,500+ TikTok videos, claimed).
5. (02:51) Engineer the song to be viral "in essence": they told Slo to use a specific loop that was currently going viral at the start of the song, then remix it, and capitalise on the moment.
6. (03:10) Community-made derivatives (a fan's slowed + reverb re-upload) are a sign the community is driving engagement.
7. (03:32) A video that underperformed can be analysed, re-edited and reposted: Anna's video went from 2,000 views first time to ~43-45k after tweaks (claimed).
8. (04:05) He says 2023-24 social media was "easy" and the 2025 algorithm change made it more competitive; a strategy change is the response.
9. (04:48) Re-editing and better positioning of self/brand helps "cut through the noise".
10. (05:10) Evidence of TikTok driving streams: YouTube comments like "heard it on TikTok".
11. (05:30) If shooting style isn't engaging enough, re-record with a new approach.
12. (06:16) Claim: get content right and it drives engagement to other platforms, instead of paying for ads or playlists.
13. (06:27) Who it is not for (his list): (a) music that isn't chart-quality or is recorded on a "dodgy microphone"; no marketing fixes bad music; (b) believing you are shadowbanned; he calls it an excuse for bad strategy, and says at least 200 views per post means you can go viral; (c) thinking you need a 4K camera/videographer every time; iPhone + Wi-Fi is enough; (d) unwilling to invest (four-figure minimum); (e) ego too big / not open to coaching; (f) too scared to be "a little bit cringy" and try things outside the box.
14. (08:46) Why past efforts failed (his view): buying information (courses, mentors) and implementing alone; cycle of jumping between mentors. Big artists have teams; the artist should focus on making the music great.
15. (10:00) "You can't build a community with ads"; content is the final key. Cases of artists with streams but no community/fan base (Denny, Andy dropped by Universal).
16. (11:06) Their approach in three parts: (1) understand the market (research audience, brand, genre, what makes people stop, watch, and check the music); (2) apply "psychological triggers" while building a fan base, "intentional content creation" not gimmicks; (3) volume: a weekly personalised content plan, shot by the artist or by them on location, edited by their team.
17. (13:19) "Viral sound consulting": suggested tweaks to the song to engineer it to go viral (optional).
18. (13:54) Review videos before posting to know what to improve.

## Examples he uses (all numbers claimed)
- Rapville founder: started at 14 in a small English town as a rapper; a song placed in a movie; launched a label, managed 50+ artists; now 19; artists he worked with: 1B+ views total; grew Rapville to 40,000+ followers; tools used by "thousands"/5,000+ artists; a recent video of his got 100,000+ views; "the TikTok kid" to UK labels.
- Adriano (Italy): ~800 views/video with high-quality cameras, thought shadowbanned; new strategy: 67,000 then 600,000+ in 7 days; 200 to 4,000+ followers in 7 days; comments asking where to find the song.
- Slo: 2.3M+ streams; 1,500+ TikTok videos on his sound; charted / #1 trending sound briefly somewhere in Asia; fan slowed+reverb upload got 14,000 views; ~400,000 YouTube views unpromoted.
- Anna: "200 view jail"; re-edited video 2,000 first post, then ~42,900-45,000 overnight.
- Chloe: ~7,000 views/video after 2025 change; 800,000+ on one video; 970,000+ in 60 days.
- Aston (Australian, signed): ~20,000/video; then 1.3M, 1.1M, 600,000 videos; song 16M+ Spotify streams (he says not all due to them).
- Rashad: 40,000 to 82,000 followers in 90 days, 129,000 by month four; 100,000+ monthly listeners (numbers in captions partly contradictory: also says started ~7,000 followers).
- Denny: 150M+ streams but no community. Andy: signed to Universal 2016, toured, dropped for no fan base.

## Process / checklists
Deliverables list (service): market research; custom content strategy; script and caption writing incl. what/when to post, hashtags, what to say/do; in-person shooting anywhere; professional editing; weekly performance review calls; viral sound consulting; access to in-house AI social media tool; fan-base growth strategy; ongoing feedback incl. pre-post video reviews.
