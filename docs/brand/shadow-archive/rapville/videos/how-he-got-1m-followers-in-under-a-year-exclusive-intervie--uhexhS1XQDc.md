# How He Got 1M Followers In Under a Year | Exclusive interview with Citizen
URL: https://youtu.be/uhexhS1XQDc | Length: 14:31 | Views: 4,423 | Date: 2026-03-25

## Core idea
Interview with Citizen the Artist (musician/filmmaker, 1M followers in under a year, no ads, claimed). His points: borrow structures but inject yourself; lean hard into what is uniquely yours; treat posts as data; write songs that introduce something new just before boredom; diverse styles are fine if the brand frames them; team and collaboration keep belief and fun alive; content is an extension of the songs.

## Lessons and tactics
1. (00:48) "Cringe" fear: he calls cringe a stupid recent term; trying hard gets shamed. Content that is merely promotional is not inherently cringe; much of what feels cringe surfaces because negative comments drive engagement.
2. (01:37) What reads as unintentionally cringe is when the audience feels you're faking. Don't go too far from who you are; don't try to be Citizen.
3. (01:50) You can take "structural frameworks" from content you like, but inject yourself; an original element means viewers can't compare or judge you against something else.
4. (02:07) He escaped a period of trying random things via a branding shift: ask "what about me is unique?" You need talking points about your brand; if there's nothing to discuss, don't expect views.
5. (02:24) Run "headlong" into what makes you unique (for him: filmmaking). Next step for him: inject more real personality because he may come across too polished.
6. (02:54) No single secret; mindset. Treat content as collecting data: every post returns feedback (retention, skips, where people swipe away). Use those metrics to understand reactions and change what isn't working.
7. (03:24) Advice to his younger self: unwavering self-belief; avoid success from something untrue to you; "you only lose when you give up"; many give up too early.
8. (03:58) Belief stays strong through collaboration: belief attracts like-minded people. Filter out people who drag you down (e.g. who call you cringe) without becoming a lone wolf.
9. (04:44) Songwriting for streams: he rejects "attention spans are shorter" (people watch long films, follow cinematic universes). Don't force songs shorter; let a song be its natural length (he keeps ~3 minutes if he wants).
10. (05:11) Keep moments inside the song at a steady pace: introduce a new sonic element just before the listener gets bored of the last one (switch the flow, the rhyme scheme, or something in the production), like Reels switching before boredom.
11. (05:43) Check with fresh ears: headphones off, speakers up, sit back, or listen in the car; imagine first listen; note what bores you.
12. (05:56) Shortening a loved section isn't "killing your darlings": a short special section (e.g. ~5 seconds) creates re-listens. He doesn't know what Spotify's algorithm does, but streams per listener rise; "So Light It Up" got ~4-5 streams per listener (claimed).
13. (06:38) Diverse styles: rely on your taste. He leaned the brand into being multi-medium (name "Citizen the Artist", film + music), which let him be sonically diverse; fans "expect the unexpected".
14. (07:55) Diversity hasn't held him back (500k+ listeners quickly, claimed). He follows what people connect with most rather than maximum appeal; shaving down what he finds interesting would become inauthentic and read as cringe.
15. (08:45) Team: best friend Scotty (camera/lighting, long-term collaborator), girlfriend Grace (actress, plays his girlfriend in the videos), brother Elliot (script writing, sounding board for big ideas). Shoots with one person missing are noticeably harder.
16. (09:49) Ads: never ran one; "absolutely not necessary" to build a career; anything can be learned, alone or with the right team. Might try ads later when functioning more like a label.
17. (10:27) Background: homeschooled with his brother; parents pushed him to learn what he was interested in.
18. (11:13) Compete with yourself: a "part two" of a video is a chance to do it better than the first, not just to farm views.
19. (12:01) Collaboration over going it alone (the "go alone faster / together further" idea; he mangles it in captions).
20. (12:18) Read DMs including negative feedback; weigh it respectfully and inspect whether something can be improved. Feedback he gets: "too mysterious, who are you?"
21. (12:52) The root is fun; his team takes the work seriously but is goofy.
22. (13:10) To make content fun: content is visuals paired with what you're already best at, an extension of your songs into an "outer universe"; don't ask who does it best and paste that on. Music and content are two mediums communicating the same brand and emotions.
23. (14:06) Content is more fun with the right people around; solo tripod shoots are lonely.

## Examples he uses (numbers claimed)
- Citizen: 1M followers in under a year, no ads; a song with 10M+ streams independently; passed 500k monthly listeners; "So Light It Up" ~4-5 streams per listener; turned down record deal offers (reason given: his collaborative team).
- Intro describes his content as transition cuts, local moments and visual effects with no AI.

## Process / checklists
Song-retention check: listen fresh (speakers or car) -> mark where boredom starts -> add a new sonic element just before (flow, rhyme scheme, production) -> keep a loved section short to drive re-listens.
