# Building a Viral Music Content Strategy for a Stranger in 21 Minutes
URL: https://youtu.be/Yw_SG6SlR1o · Length 21:53 · Views 894

## Core idea
Live consult with neo-soul/R&B artist Miss Blanche (2-3K views per video). Start from the brand of the song (not the artist), then use the "copy and remix" framework to adapt proven viral formats (reaction method, transition method) to the song's lyrics, then run a 30-day experiment, review on views and retention, and double down with a 70/30 rule.

## Lessons and tactics
1. Music videos no longer work for going viral; look at what is going viral and replicate it with your music and brand (00:00-00:07, 03:12-03:23).
2. Foundation = the branding of the SONG, not the artist's long-term brand (which gets "philosophical"); only ask content-relevant questions (01:37-01:56).
3. Song-brand question: "If this song played behind a movie scene in a cinema, what is on screen?" Then pin down: settings, mood (here sexual tension, teasing, romance), era (70s), colours (warm: gold, brown, orange, yellow), characters (a passionate couple) (02:00-02:55).
4. "Copy and remix framework": copy what is working and remix it; you don't need to understand the algorithm, only why this specific video went viral (03:26-03:46).
5. Copy step: research 3 top creators in your niche -> their 3 most viral videos each = 9 examples -> 3 remixed ideas per example (he says this totals 28 videos; 9 x 3 is 27, as stated) (03:48-04:23).
6. Remix step: make each copied format relevant to your song (04:32-04:40).
7. Format 1, "the reaction method" (example: JBKE, "playing my song for my childhood piano teacher"): (a) text hook in the first seconds telling viewers to watch for the reaction, (b) "retention editing": zoom in on reactions, (c) text above the reacting person's head as if showing their thoughts, (d) an extreme reaction at the end (crying). He says the reaction is likely orchestrated (same "teacher" in about nine videos) (04:46-06:31).
8. Why content goes viral: make people wait for something so they try to predict; the payoff either surprises or satisfies. The hook matters, but watching to the end is next most important; "predictability" is his key word (05:37-06:10, 10:23).
9. Diagnosis of her failed reaction video (1:16 long, reaction arrives after 20 s): the person reacting wasn't on screen at the start, so there was no predictability element (07:09-07:32).
10. Three parts of a hook: (1) spoken hook, (2) visual hook = what is on screen that explains or makes you predict what the video is about (context), (3) text hook (07:34-08:03).
11. Don't rely on the spoken hook alone: people see before they hear ("light travels faster than sound") (08:18-08:28).
12. Subtitles are not a text hook; the text hook gives context and a reason to watch ("is this relevant for me?"), which viewers decide in the first ~3.5 seconds, else they scroll (08:29-08:50).
13. Format 2, "the transition method", two versions: (a) the Snapchat-text version, (b) the "TV" version (outside of / into a TV). Works if congruent with branding and lyrics (08:55-09:43).
14. TV transition anatomy (Citizen example): an odd setup (girlfriend shaving cheese on his head), he runs back toward the TV so viewers predict the shot will happen on the TV, curiosity builds, then the payoff with a "connector" element (falling "snow" that is actually cheese); comments debating whether it's real boost it (10:08-10:52).
15. Remix idea for her lyric "Undress me with your eyes": two guys carry a towel in front of her as she runs backward toward the TV; towel drops on the song drop; cut to the TV shot before anything is shown; the towel falls in the post-transition shot too, only shoulders visible (11:10-12:10).
16. What plays on the TV must be congruent with the track's start (70s aesthetic); this doesn't add virality but helps artist branding, which converts better to streams later (12:14-12:40).
17. Snapchat method anatomy: starts with a candid shot, transitions into a cinematic shot (13:37-13:47).
18. Her idea remixed: friend filming candidly from a door (or through a window) with Snapchat-style text like "Can't believe I caught this on camera", "Am I weird for filming this?", "How did I catch this?", "She didn't lock her door"; first 3 seconds must make clear what is about to happen (13:24-14:06). Window = spying; people are curious about inside other people's homes (14:21-14:32).
19. A transition needs a trigger that initiates it (e.g. throw a pillow at the phone and transition on impact; Citizen's face-slap) (14:48-15:25).
20. Two types of viral videos, both valid: (a) ideas that fit the lyrics, (b) a purely viral idea not tied to the song (15:35-15:47).
21. Strategy step 1, experiment: post 30 videos in 30 days; test 3 formats (he recommends 4 different formats every week), with several posts of each format per month (16:42-17:02).
22. Step 2, review after 30 days on two metrics: (a) views, only counting clearly outsized results (50-100K more, ~10x, not +200); (b) watch-time retention: % who stay to the end and % who rewatch (17:04-17:45).
23. Step 3, double down on the 1-2 best formats with the "70/30 rule": 70% repeat what works, 30% test new formats (18:22-18:41).
24. Repeat winners: sometimes the exact same video, sometimes a new setting or actor; "you can never rinse it enough" because each video reaches ~90% non-followers (claimed) (18:43-18:58).
25. Transitions are not "too seen"; the method still works (19:24-19:58).
26. Cross-niche replication: formats that work in one genre can be replicated in another genre that hasn't seen them ("blue ocean"), because genres don't cross-pollinate; take what worked in rap/R&B and apply it to your music (20:13-20:55).

## Process / checklists
1. Brand the song (cinema-scene question: setting, mood, era, colours, characters).
2. Copy: 3 creators x 3 most viral videos = 9 examples.
3. Remix: 3 ideas per example, tied to the song/lyrics.
4. Experiment: 30 videos / 30 days, several formats per week.
5. Review: views (only ~10x outliers) + retention/rewatches.
6. Double down: 70% winners, 30% new tests; reuse in new settings/actors.
Hook check: spoken + visual + text hook; payoff at the end; transitions need a trigger.
