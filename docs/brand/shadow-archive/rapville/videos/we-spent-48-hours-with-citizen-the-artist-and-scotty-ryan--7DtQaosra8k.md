# We Spent 48 Hours With Citizen The Artist and Scotty Ryan
URL: https://youtu.be/7DtQaosra8k | Length: 33:44 | Views: 3,474 | Date: 2026-04-06

## Core idea
A strategy-plus-shoot vlog with Scotty Ryan (Rapville head of viral content / Citizen's cameraman) and Citizen, promoting the song "Time", aiming for a 1M-like video. The method: identify the song's emotion, turn it into visuals, design the cinematic RESULT first and then a "no way that will look good" hook leading into it, keep the situation familiar but the result surprising, and improvise on location.

## Lessons and tactics
1. (01:28) They say ads don't really work for music marketing; strong videos do (no ads, no paid influencers).
2. (01:42) Start by asking what the song feels like: that sets the content, place, look, colours and vibe.
3. (02:04) The closer a song is to one very specific feeling, the better. "Time" feels like driving fast in a car. Citizen watched car edits while making the song, planning content from the start.
4. (02:24) Step one: identify the emotion. The final video must feel like the song.
5. (02:39) Step two: "emotion to visual": which real things sell the emotion (fast car, cool location), so viewers think "I want to be there" / where they would be listening to it.
6. (03:12) Branding question: imagine your song as the background of a movie scene in the cinema; which scenes come to mind? Also decide how many people are in it (just you, someone sad, a hyped crowd).
7. (03:59) The idea "has to be crazy"; just driving a fast car lip-syncing doesn't get views anymore.
8. (04:29) "Crazy" is not "never seen before": take a familiar situation and add a twist. "Random isn't viral." Second rule: make it surprising (how the song enters is unexpected).
9. (05:00) Format used: behind-the-scenes hook -> music-video result. The intro can just be the filming setup, but the result must feel like the song; this is hard and time-consuming.
10. (05:34) The situation should be familiar enough to create an expectation (e.g. someone hanging over a cliff -> you fear they fall), then the result answers that expectation in an interesting way.
11. (06:36) "Viewer feeling" graph: start with curiosity in the first 3 s, then a spike of surprise at/after the transition. Curiosity = I know what's going on but want to know what happens; confusion = I don't know what's going on (bad). Aim for a result better than expected.
12. (08:08) Hook design for "Time": make the start feel like "there's no way that's going to be good" (odd camera moves, goofy actions) so the viewer expects a result.
13. (08:25) A caption like "trust the process" signals that a result is coming.
14. (08:43) Keep the camera clearly visible in the hook: seeing a camera filming something interesting makes people want to see the shot.
15. (08:43) Keep the hook frame simple/empty so nothing confuses ("is that part of it?") and there's room for the like/comment/share buttons.
16. (09:12) Center the subject; people look at the middle of the phone. Keep content clear of platform UI zones (the Reels label at top, description at bottom).
17. (09:28) Multiple angles/locations in the hook communicate that several results are coming. Interviewer compares it to "Sprite challenge fail" compilations that hold all results until the end; Scotty agrees: viewers stay waiting for multiple results.
18. (10:32) The result must look good; if the viewer expects bad and gets bad, nothing is surprising. Change the emotion: it must look incredible and shock them.
19. (10:51) Every visual element ties back to the song's feeling (cool, fast car, Miami, glasses, a "GTA 5" type character, "you made it in life").
20. (11:36) Ideas can come from an effect already seen (camera moving back and forth); Citizen designed the song around a sample that matched that effect.
21. (12:09) Keep the same core emotion across the batch of videos: "no way that's going to look good" -> "that looked amazing".
22. (12:24) Order of creation: step one = result, step two = hook. Design the cinematic result that matches the lyrics/feeling first, then brainstorm hooks that lead into it.
23. (13:00) Brainstormed result/hook pairs: bonnet angle of Citizen driving fast (result) with hooks such as a videographer sprinting with a shaky phone to keep up, or a 2 m tripod strapped to the roof pointing inside; orbit drone shot (result) with hooks like camera people passing the camera around the car or Citizen throwing the camera up; a sped-up "3-2-1-go" setup mouthing the song fast, then slow-mo, to make people rewatch to understand how.
24. (13:49) Experiment: you won't know what works; that's core to every strategy.
25. (14:03) On location something will go wrong; improvise by checking "is this the result I wanted?" If not, change the idea and know why; don't just hope.
26. (17:07) Plan in detail right before filming (the day before is too early for unknowns like train times), keep a written shot list (Grace as notetaker), stay agile when shots or lighting don't work.
27. (17:56) Play with the song's theme: song "Time" -> freezing time, slow motion (train-crossing concept: time freezes as car crosses, performance clip, time unfreezes, they survive).
28. (17:56) The hook must be crystal clear (crossing bells, direct shot of train, direct shot of car); a slightly out-of-frame train pan was re-shot on the next train.
29. (20:27) Shot-list check on set (Snapchat hook with train, wide train result, head-on train, car angle, mediums of each actor, etc.).
30. (21:09) VFX approach: chop forward/reverse on beat; comp a static lip-sync mouth onto moving footage. "Good VFX is basically magic tricks": hide how the effect works and how much is practical vs faked; that drives comment discussion, engagement and repeat/loop plays.
31. (21:43) Control light: block outside light (jacket, half-open convertible roof) and add your own.
32. (22:30) Use tiny moments (an eye close-up during the ticking part) right before the big hero shot; contrast makes the big moment feel bigger.
33. (23:25) After the hook, add a short relatable moment introducing the characters (singing along in the car, which is why they didn't see the train) so viewers care when peril arrives; end with a relief beat (the two characters looking at each other).
34. (24:49) Pick simpler shots when time is short if earlier effects already "earned" attention, but always capture a couple of extra options/plates for the edit.
35. (25:44) Improvise kit: forgot suction mounts, used a tripod (preferred the higher angle).
36. (25:59) Shooting through a window: use a polarizer to remove reflections; he uses a clip-on variable ND filter for phone that also polarises; may need colour correction (slight yellow). "No excuses": phone shooting is enough; it's about ideas and clarity.
37. (27:23) (Elliot) Small details matter: control when and how a look happens and where the camera is; avoid arbitrary decisions; only what is in frame matters, the setup can be janky.
38. (28:41) Day-two example: camera pulled fast out of the car's tailpipe (car seems to speed off), intercut/comp with fire blown down a paper towel tube; accept "creative liberty" comments.
39. (29:12) Save posts you find for inspiration and try them; scroll your feed for ideas; credit the source when you borrow.
40. (29:28) Not every hook deceives: a simple version is just "hook setup" (shots of mounting the camera around the car, duct tape and all) then cinematic results.

## Process / checklists
Strategy steps: (1) identify emotion -> (2) emotion to visual (props, locations, movie-scene question, number of people) -> (3) brainstorm: result first, then hook; familiar situation + surprising result; viewer-feeling curve curiosity -> surprise.
Hook checklist: "no way that'll be good" feel, clear situation, visible camera, empty centred frame, caption that promises a result (e.g. "trust the process"), avoid UI zones, multiple setups for multiple results.
Shoot day: detailed plan right before, written shot list, stay agile, re-shoot if the hook isn't crystal clear, grab extra plates, control light, polarizer/ND on phone when shooting through glass, intro-the-characters shot, relief end beat, small-moment-before-big-moment.
