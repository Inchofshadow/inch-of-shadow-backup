# f*ck trends… here's how to build a world-class brand (private recording)
URL: https://youtu.be/sb4dPn1ZFgE | Length: 20:28 | Views: 728 (uploaded 2026-06-07)
Speaker: Niall, RAPVILLE co-founder, at an internal team "mastermind" with strategists (Junior, Isabella, others).

## Core idea (2-3 sentences)
Going viral is now "the easy part"; the problem is conversion to streams and fans. Blatantly copying trends flops, innovating on a format with lyrical congruency goes viral. The fix is world building: extend the song through the visuals via an emotional anchor, persona/characters and a consistent visual identity; then extend into a "universe" by keeping the winning format and changing the world per song.

## Lessons and tactics
Part 1, the problem: copying trends (01:16-07:49)
1. The biggest mistake across their artists is blatantly copying trends (01:22-01:28). A near-identical copy of Citizen's TV transition got 5k views; a strategy that matched the song's lyrics got far more (01:28-02:28).
2. Lyrical congruency is why it worked, not the format itself (02:19-02:28).
3. A frame-for-frame copy of a Citizen video, though executed almost perfectly, was one of the lowest performers for Eric Bellinger's "The Moment I Knew"; the scenario (saving your girl from death) did not quite fit "the moment I knew" (02:33-03:30). Keeping the same format (Snapchat method transition) but innovating with their own idea did much better (03:31-03:40).
4. Novelty: people who stay through this kind of transition have already seen Citizen's content, so you must innovate (04:38-04:43).
5. Re-hook / payoff: continue the storyline after the transition (e.g. something carried on in the DSLR shot) to give a further reason to watch; odd details (a foot in the corner) drive comments (04:29-05:01).
6. TV transition (invented by Citizen and Scott, per him): the way to go viral now is innovating, not copying (05:04-05:13). Isabella's version: made it fit her world for the single (pink wig, Y2K), backdrop matching the aesthetic, slow-mo and Y2K text in the edit, so it reads like a mini music video (05:31-06:10).
7. Start with movement/chaos: her opening clip was chaotic (friend with hairdryer, she fell over, giggling); a similar version where she was already sitting in frame performed worse; initial movement makes it feel in the moment, "on a mission" (06:12-06:35).
8. Not filming the TV transition in front of the actual backdrop makes people question whether she is on location (06:36-06:53). People watch because they want to be in the world, not for the gimmick (07:00-07:07).
9. "Strip a video back to its bare bones and then rebuild" (07:10-07:15). Her differences: Snapchat text at the start, zero budget, and a VHS camera for the shot instead of a cinematic camera (07:16-07:30).
10. Conclusion: copy = flop, innovate/novel = viral; build a "one-of-one brand that nobody else can copy" (07:30-07:46).

Part 2, the fix: build worlds (07:46-20:22)
11. World building = extending the song through the visuals (07:54-08:00).
12. Study artists going from zero to viral on social, not superstars with algorithm leverage (Post Malone, Bad Bunny, Lady Gaga, Central Cee) (08:00-08:21).
13. Blueprint for a world (08:23-08:42): (a) emotional anchor - the feeling of the song; (b) persona and characters who live in the world; (c) visual identity - location, clothes, props, signature objects. Keep all consistent across all videos.
14. Emotional anchor: content does not go viral because it makes the viewer feel nothing (08:44-08:49). Find it in the lyrics: Eric Bellinger's "The Moment I Knew" -> moments you fall in love (08:58-09:10); AJ's lyric along the lines of "today ain't go by good, but life can't be that bad" -> worst-case scenario moments (e.g. phone-swap trust test, getting caught cheating) (09:58-11:24).
15. Reactivating a big but dormant account: Bellinger had 1M followers but ~5k views (checked analytics, not bots - content just was not good); emotional, lyrically congruent, relatable content re-relevanced him (09:15-09:44).
16. "If you're showing song out now cover art thumbnails, you're cooked"; you need emotional content (09:44-09:49).
17. In AJ's video he stays in frame acting in his world (not a performance video); re-hooking happens in the strategy, not just in the edit (10:27-10:41). A classic sample (Ice Cube original) also makes the hook stronger (10:43-10:51).
18. Do not do all the work in the hook and then lose viewers when the music comes in; content looks cringe and gimmicky when the hook has nothing to do with the lyrics (11:30-11:42).
19. Persona and characters (11:44-14:18): Mike Manos (spelling unclear) breaks props and runs like a madman -> rebel/outlaw persona; his characters are all clones (masked running man); "You don't need resources. You need to be resourceful"; a tripod is enough (11:57-12:24).
20. Use the "brand archetype wheel" (12:24-12:39): Mike Manos = outlaw; Eric Bellinger = lover, or sage (wisdom in the game); AJ = jester ("the kid who can't catch a break").
21. Kim (spelling unclear): mysterious persona plus luxury (clothes, set-ups, supercars) plus attractive girls in videos to appeal to the masses; distinct, clear, consistent (12:40-13:01). The look is built into the music itself (songs about a luxury brand and money) (13:25-13:47). Attractive people in videos signal who listens; like a perfume ad (13:48-14:18).
22. Visual identity, "might be the most important" (14:22-14:26). Clothing: Laza (spelling unclear) wears red, blue, green, black and white in different styles yet reads as the same artist; looking expensive, clean and consistent gives "return value" (14:27-14:45). Colour aesthetic: Isabella's pink makes her memorable/recognisable (14:54-15:00).
23. Signature objects: Citizen's car for the "Time" rollout (shot in Miami with Rap-A-Lot), the consistent element in every video for the song; previously his signature thread was his girlfriend (15:01-15:32).
24. A signature effect can activate UGC: Citizen's slow-mo "frozen in time" effect; 15k reels made on the sound; they also call it his emotional anchor (15:33-15:53).
25. Location: needs depth and character and must complement the persona; not your back garden or local park (16:12-16:29). Mike Manos uses an archway in all videos, which suits the clone effect (16:31-16:41). Citizen in Miami = luxury/cool; also leveraged the GTA 6 trend (Miami aesthetic, blue Mustang) (16:42-17:14).
26. "Your streams are less than your followers because you have no visual consistency" (17:14-17:18). A world converts because it is familiar and has return value; people are more likely to watch when they see it again (17:18-17:30).
27. Touchpoints = conversion: going viral once is not enough. A rule he attributes to Google: 7 hours of an artist's content consumed, 11 interactions in total (comments, likes, saves) across four touchpoint platforms (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, music videos, short form) before you can expect much conversion; he calls it accurate for merch sales (17:30-18:25).
28. Visual consistency = familiarity/recognition; emotional consistency = viewer feels the song through the visuals; that connection inspires the viewer to jump platforms (18:25-18:47).
29. "Performance reels are dead because there's no emotion"; emotion is the single most important element of a strategy (18:51-19:00).
30. After 15-30 pieces for a song (the song is "rinsed"), extend into a universe: keep the winning format, change the world, vehicle and vibe per song (19:02-19:27). Citizen: Sunkisses = foresty nature world; Vertigo = snowy wintery world; Time = blue car, palm trees, Miami GTA world (19:27-19:37).
31. Different songs with different vibes are not a problem; an unpredictable account is good as long as there is a consistent thread through all the content (19:50-20:12).

## Process / checklists
World blueprint per song: 1) emotional anchor from the lyrics; 2) persona (archetype wheel) and characters; 3) visual identity: clothes, colour, location with depth, props, signature object/effect; 4) keep consistent across 15-30 pieces; 5) remix formats (strip to bare bones, rebuild in your world, add novelty); 6) next song: keep winning format, new world.
