# getting an artist 8M views in first 8 posts
URL: https://youtu.be/RrcXl8bcKXg | Length: 11:25 | Views: 717 | Date: 2026-08-02

## Core idea
Three things build a streaming community: branding with a unique edge, hooks based on psychology, and a "world" built on emotions people return to. He walks through all 8 posts made for Arjuna (storyline / hook / transition connector / emotion / result) and the world-building and conversion rules that turn views into streams.

## Lessons and tactics
1. (00:00) Three needs: (1) branding that gives a unique edge, (2) hooks using human psychology, (3) a world built on emotions people come back to.
2. (00:19) Problem framing: since short-form, followers matter less and every post starts from zero; an artist with 2M followers can average 5,000 views unless he adapts.
3. (01:03) Branding is "the DNA" of the content. Split into three pillars: the art, the content direction, the visual identity.
4. (01:20) If the song lacks an angle (e.g. a love song in a saturated market), find one outside the music: an element nobody else can replicate ("unique angle"); here the artist's wife.
5. (01:44) Content direction: avoid "phone TikToky" low-fi content (he calls it a mistake that puts you in the same box as everyone); lead with cinematic, storyline-driven content with a recurring character.
6. (02:13) Visual identity: simple everyday locations (supermarket, pub, park).
7. (02:25) Global/cultural targeting is a branding decision: the song's audience is Indian rather than English, so use Bollywood-themed elements to target them.
8. (02:35) A single recurring wardrobe item (red shirt) because red stands out.
9. (02:57) Best format for storylines and characters: the "transition format" (hook scene, then transition into performance).
10. (03:42) Break each video into: storyline/premise, hook type, transition connector, emotion. Hook types named: Snapchat caption, text, skit-style (no text), TV BTS, DIY BTS. Connectors named: fall, slap, AI connector, head turn, prop, splash, abrupt (no connector). Emotions used: shock, love at first sight, public cringe, rejection, love, creativity.
11. (04:33) Put a culturally targeting line in the hook dialogue ("what in the Bollywood movie?") to reach the most likely listeners.
12. (05:49) Let the song play echoey/quietly in the background while the hook plays out; he says hooks perform worse without it.
13. (06:47) "Setup and the payoff" drives views; after 4-5 attention-getting videos with consistent threads, viewers who saw earlier ones are more likely to watch new ones.
14. (07:57) Views do not automatically equal streams; "world building" does. Rapville's world-building rules: (a) a consistent emotional anchor (here rejection and love, inverses that both fit the song), (b) consistent characters (artist as main, wife as love interest, even when the storyline flips), (c) consistent visual identity (same clothes; different but simple locations that live in the same world).
15. (09:01) Consistency raises the chance of second, third, fourth touch points; compared to seeing an ad repeatedly before buying.
16. (09:44) "Conversion formats": the most important is the reaction format, someone falling in love with the song in real time; engineer it so the reactor starts unsure, then loves it, mirroring the viewer.
17. (10:06) After the viral videos, a performance-driven video (just selling the song, no distractions) converts better.
18. (10:24) Keep testing storylines and formats; the goal is compounding, not views. Read the data to decide the next video.

## Process / checklists
1. Branding: art, content direction, visual identity; find the unique angle; decide targeting.
2. Content: transition-format storylines, each specced as storyline / hook / connector / emotion.
3. World building: consistent emotion, characters, visual identity.
4. Conversion: reaction format, then performance video.
5. Keep testing; read data for the next video.
