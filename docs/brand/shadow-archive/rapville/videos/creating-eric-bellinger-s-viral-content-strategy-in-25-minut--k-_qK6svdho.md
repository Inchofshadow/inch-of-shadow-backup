# Creating Eric Bellinger's Viral Content Strategy in 25 Minutes
URL: https://youtu.be/k-_qK6svdho · Length 26:42 · Views 756 · Speaker: RAPVILLE strategist (not named; works from the UK at a whiteboard)
Transcript: local speech-to-text (no YouTube captions)

## Core idea
End-to-end walkthrough: brand Eric Bellinger with art / artist / audience, then design six ideas on a whiteboard, each with format, concept, hook, transition moment and performance, then review the posted results two or three weeks later. The emotional anchor ("the moment I knew") and the unique angle (his wife) run through every idea; the main lesson is engineering re-hooks into the performance, because the hook is only five seconds of a 30-second video.

## Lessons and tactics
BRANDING
1. Split branding into three: the art (music), the artist, the audience (00:50-01:09).
2. Art: find the "consistent emotional anchor"; usually the hardest part, here given by the lyric itself ("the moment I knew"), which lets you picture many scenarios (01:27-01:52).
3. Art: find the transition moment in the song: a build-up, and the cut lands right on the key lyric (01:54-02:14).
4. Artist: the unique angle here is his wife; real chemistry beats hired actors and models, and few artists who sing about love have a partner to bring in (02:16-02:53).
5. No partner? There are other ways to find a unique angle (03:03-03:09).
6. Character and values: maturity; he doesn't need to be the coolest in the room, he is the wisest (03:10-03:35).
7. A "wise" brand needs 15+ years in the game, so it is hard to develop but also hard to copy: a "one-of-one" brand is the goal (03:35-04:07).
8. Maturity also in the relationship content: deeper than club-and-bottles imagery, which is an edge in an "industry of immaturity" (04:07-04:29).
9. Audience: use the data (70% women, 30% men), so appeal to women and to the mature part of the audience (04:31-04:58).
10. Still aim at Gen Z, who he says drive almost all trends and engagement (05:01-05:12).
IDEAS (each: format, concept, hook, transition moment, performance)
11. Write the emotional anchor ("moment of love") and the unique angle ("wifey") on the board so they appear in every idea (05:46-06:13).
12. Idea 1, Snapchat hook, "Walmart fall": friend films and dares him to ask a girl out, flips camera, he taps her shoulder, she turns, he falls over; cut so it looks real; full transition to a bird's-eye view of him singing on the floor (06:13-07:20).
13. Re-hook in the performance: her feet in frame kicking him to continue the storyline; don't let the viewer get bored once the music starts (07:20-07:47).
14. Idea 2, Snapchat: a "spicy" concept; she swipes the camera as she kneels, a swipe/360 transition back to him (07:54-08:32).
15. Don't let the viewer get bored; the hook buys 5-6 seconds, then people scroll two seconds into the music; continue the story with her hands on him (08:36-09:01).
16. Avoid "concept no man's land": an idea that is not annoying, emotional, funny or sexy, with no angle, is just boring (09:03-09:21).
17. Idea 3, stream transition: a universally relatable situation (gaming while your partner brings food), turned into music marketing; fake livestream with comments, he calls for food, she brings it, he starts singing, the hug is the transition (09:29-11:29).
18. Scroll with a marketing lens: when you see a trend or concept, ask how it could market your music (10:08-10:18).
19. No re-hook option: record the performance three times and cut between the clips every few seconds to stay dynamic (11:29-11:51).
20. Idea 4, Snapchat: back from tour, wife films herself saying she cooked him dinner, the banquet is in frame so viewers predict he'll walk in; he does, bear-hugs her = transition (12:09-13:17).
21. Sometimes lines are scripted to make sure people say the right thing (12:42-12:49).
22. The transition is the hook; a connecting movement from the first shot into the second makes it smooth, and links all these videos (13:17-13:27).
23. Re-hook as a comment driver: during the hug he leans over to bite the food; small comical details get comments, and people reading them wait for the end (13:27-14:03).
24. Idea 5, "BTS tease": the video never transitions, so viewers go to the profile to find it, where they follow and binge the ecosystem (14:05-14:41).
25. BTS tease concept: behind the scenes of recording idea 4, with a helper crawling around, a dropped spoon; zoom-ins on small moments keep people hooked (14:43-15:14).
26. Efficiency: a BTS tease costs no extra shoot time because you film the BTS of a video you're making anyway; "the most lazy strategy", predicted to get the most views (15:14-16:03).
27. Idea 6, Snapchat, "perfect Starbucks order": filmed seemingly by a stranger, she cuts him off with an absurd order; excitement builds then explodes into the song (16:19-17:10).
28. Performance from several angles (left, behind, front) so viewers experience the story and it stays dynamic (17:13-17:43).
29. Summary: storyline, romance, hooks where you can predict what happens, smooth transition timing; the hook is five seconds, so engineer re-hooks into the performance (17:49-18:49).
RESULTS REVIEW
30. Review each video two or three weeks after posting: views, likes, comments (19:39-19:50).
31. Knees transition critique: the performance had no depth (white wall), and it didn't look like something anyone would Snapchat, so the Snapchat caption felt out of place (21:35-22:00).
32. Starbucks critique: it would have done better if it looked more like a stranger filmed it (24:47-24:55).
33. The BTS tease got the most views but weaker likes (23:53-24:04).
34. Comments praised the strategy and consistency (25:03-25:08).
35. Prepare before shoot day: other agencies send a videographer who asks what to film; "fail to prepare, prepare to fail" (25:43-25:53).

## Process / checklists
- Brand board: art (emotional anchor, transition moment in the lyric), artist (unique angle, character and values), audience (data split, maturity, Gen Z).
- Per idea: format, concept, hook, transition moment, performance with a re-hook.
- Re-hook options: continue the storyline, a small comical detail, multi-take cuts, multiple angles.
- After 2-3 weeks: review each video, write one critique per underperformer.
