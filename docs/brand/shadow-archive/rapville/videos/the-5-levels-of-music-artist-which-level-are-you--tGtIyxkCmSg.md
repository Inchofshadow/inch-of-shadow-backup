# The 5 Levels of Music Artist (Which Level Are YOU?)
URL: https://youtu.be/tGtIyxkCmSg · Length 17:13 · Views 619 · Speaker: Nile

## Core idea
The gap between struggling and successful artists is almost never talent; they solve the wrong problem for their level. Five levels, each with a diagnostic and one move to the next: the Vault Artist, the Frozen Artist, Lost in the Noise, the One-Man Army, the Artist CEO.

## Lessons and tactics
1. Be honest about your level and make the specific move it requires (00:00-00:33).
2. LEVEL 1, "the vault artist" (00:46): finished songs on a hard drive, old songs under 1,000 listeners, local gigs; "I'm not a content creator, I'm an artist"; only posts announcements (studio photo, cover art) (01:01-01:20).
3. Objections (cringe, no clickbait, no dancing) argue against off-brand content, not content as a whole; there is a version that fits you (01:22-01:44).
4. The vault is "armor": a private definition of success (organic, word of mouth, talent alone) protects you from rejection; if nobody heard it, nobody rejected it (01:46-02:07).
5. Decide: hobby (legitimate; then ignore all this) or impact (needs a plan, not just talent) (02:26-02:53).
6. Level 1 exit task this week: for every finished song write one sentence: who it's for, where they live online, what type of videos would make them stop scrolling. That's the start of a content plan. Exit = knowing why and to whom you are releasing (02:53-03:15).
7. LEVEL 2, "the frozen artist" (03:25): knows content matters, has a plan, songs and ideas, but nothing goes out; still consuming marketing videos/podcasts. Diagnostic: you haven't posted this week (03:28-04:05).
8. Consuming marketing content feels like progress ("dopamine of working without the exposure of shipping"); you need to fear never trying more than being judged (04:05-04:41).
9. Fear's two disguises: (a) the rebrand: waiting for a new sound/logo/mood board; rebranding with no audience is redecorating a house nobody visits, it resets you to zero (04:42-05:04); (b) timing: no release scheduled so no reason to post (e.g. an artist releasing in 2027 asking to talk then); you compete with creators who post daily, not with other artists (05:04-05:34).
10. You choose which pain: judged by strangers for trying, or by yourself for never trying (05:47-06:00).
11. Level 2 exit: post something today, whatever is on your phone, unedited, unplanned (06:06-06:21).
12. LEVEL 3, "lost in the noise" (06:36): posting but not growing; three distractions, each closer to working than the last (06:39-07:06):
    a. "The 2019 playbook": playlist pitching, paid ads, expensive music videos; worked in 2019, now the label playbook without the label budget; these borrow attention that stops when spending stops; paid traffic to a song with no content world (no characters, no story, no reason to come back) = a leaky bucket; you bought listeners, not fans (07:06-08:14). Content is the only channel that compounds (07:46-07:53).
    b. "The salesman strategy": posting a lot but every post sells (snippets, performance reels, "song out now" covers); asks before giving; nothing entertains (08:16-08:51).
    c. "The begging formats": e.g. "if the algorithm shows you this you have great taste", "my fan base could fit on a bus", "can we normalize not skipping small artists"; makes your smallness the content; people follow artists they admire, not pity; may go viral but has no longevity or repeatability because nothing in it is yours (08:53-09:46).
13. Level 3 diagnostic: posting consistently for 90 days and you can't explain why your best video worked (10:04-10:11).
14. Level 3 exit: use whatever stops the scroll (hook, trend, edit), but what happens after the stop must be yours: your world, your story, something only you could put in; then if it runs you know why and can repeat it (10:11-10:35).
15. LEVEL 4, "the one-man army" (10:40): has a working format but feeds the content machine alone (shooting, editing, analysing, relationships, releasing, making music) and hits a ceiling (10:43-11:24).
16. Three fixes artists cycle through, in order, all failing: (1) work harder: ideas worsen, generic output, burnout back to level 3 content; (2) cut back: less output, running away when it should scale; (3) hire people (editor, videographer): you swap making for managing, "you bought a second job" (11:24-12:21).
17. The problem is architecture, not hours/energy/headcount: everything runs through you; be the owner (says yes/no to output), not the employee (12:21-12:47).
18. Level 4 diagnostic: you have a working format but you're the only one who can execute it (12:47-12:56).
19. Two exits (neither is more hands): (1) people who buy into your vision, already in your world (Citizen the Artist runs it with his brother, school best friend and high-school sweetheart) (13:00-13:35); (2) a professional team that carries all execution, runs in good and bad months, takes ideas from your world, and constantly analyses why things flopped or worked (13:36-14:12).
20. LEVEL 5, "the artist CEO" (14:36): doing less but growing faster. Bianca's month as the model workflow (see checklist).
21. Once the machine runs, level-3 tactics work: music videos land (audience waiting), paid ads convert (a world to walk into), PR works (story with evidence) (16:12-16:29).
22. Content is the foundation, not the ceiling; the question becomes where to put freed focus: the music or a new marketing channel (16:36-16:57).

## Examples he uses
- Unnamed client: 0 to 100,000 streams in 30 days; an EP debuted to over 2M streams (claimed) (00:33-00:41).
- Artist planning a 2027 release asking to talk then (05:13-05:20).
- Citizen the Artist: team of brother, best friend, girlfriend (13:10-13:23).
- Bianca: 13M views in first month (claimed; another video says 16M in 30 days) (14:39-14:49).

## Process / checklists
Level exits: L1 one sentence per song (who / where online / what stops them) -> L2 post today -> L3 borrowed hook + your own world after it -> L4 fix the architecture (vision-sharing people or a full team) -> L5 owner reviews.
Artist CEO monthly workflow (Bianca): branding call (how she speaks, cares, her world, what's different) -> becomes the base document -> ideas arrive, she kills off-brand ones, approves the rest -> pre-production call (videographer, locations, props, extras, day plan, timings) -> shoot day: shows up, is directed, focuses on her energy -> reviews edits, leaves revisions, approves -> assistant schedules/posts -> end-of-month report: what performed, what didn't, what changes next month -> she gives direction.
