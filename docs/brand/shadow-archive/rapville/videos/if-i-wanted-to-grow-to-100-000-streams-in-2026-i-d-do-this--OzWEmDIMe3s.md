# If I Wanted To Grow To 100,000 Streams In 2026, I'd Do This

Note: continuation of the same live workshop as GRQcms1PQ24. He applies the formats to a room member's (Nana's) sexy/intimate relationship song ("I see you undress me with your eyes"), then Q&A. Transcript ends mid-answer at 31:51.

## Core idea (2-3 sentences)
Go format by format through his format library, explain why each works (retention via audience prediction, connectors, congruence with the song), and judge whether it fits this particular song. Experiment with about five formats for 30 days (one post a day), then double down with 20 posts of the winners (70-75% what works, 25% experiment). "The audience is the director of your page."

## Lessons and tactics
Format list he names (00:14-00:23): public reaction, private reaction, song breakdown method, transition method, fake Snapchat, HQA (high quality authentic), bandwagon effect, slide post, universe building.

1. Public reaction: make it look like a public reaction with your song in it; works through watch time/retention because viewers predict what will happen (water balloon thrown, will he get beaten up?) (00:35-01:22). Adapt to the song's mood: for a sexy song, a girl walking up to a group of guys in public (01:24-01:59). He would not actually pick this one for this song; you do not need all formats, just enough to experiment (02:01-02:11).
2. Private reaction: e.g. "wrote this for my childhood piano teacher" reaction video, camera zooms in on reaction, fake tear; staged and the reactor appears in many videos (02:15-02:53). Fits a personal song better than a public reaction: song made for my boyfriend / crush, or played to boyfriend and his dad (adds humour + prediction) (02:53-03:21).
3. Transition method is detail-heavy: where you hold the camera, how you transition, even the sound effects (03:24-03:48).
4. TV transition = before/after and prediction: camera in front of a TV, viewers wonder what is being filmed and whether it will turn out good; odd action (cheese grated as fake snow) (03:59-04:33).
5. "Connector": an element visible in both the before and after shot (the grated cheese/snow, a fist, a towel) makes the transition connect and makes people believe it is real; debatable details drive comments ("is that cheese?") (05:10-05:59).
6. "Continuation of the hook": the hook element should continue through the video (snow falls the whole time); a one-time element (towel ripped off) is "not debatable enough". He rejected his own towel idea for this reason (06:01-06:24). His idea for this song: artist in a towel in front of a TV, two people rip it off on the drop, transition to a shoulders-up shot in a tasteful bathroom/Victorian setting (04:35-05:08).
7. Fake Snapchat method: before/after with a fake Snapchat clip (e.g. filmed from a window, on-screen text in the style of "there's no way this is real life"); candid-looking footage transitions smoothly into cinematic footage; connector (the robber's fist stays in frame) and continuation (he stays in the video interacting) (06:26-07:39).
8. Instead of lyric relevance, use BTS vs the shot: for this song, a reverse of the 90s-movie "servants dress them as they walk" scene; show BTS of people removing clothes and the cameraman "doing the most", caption like "what is she recording?", possibly in public for more question marks, then transition to the tasteful, well-coloured shot (07:53-08:48).
9. HQA content: super tasteful performance video that works with the song; right background and visuals; camera does not change, not a music video (09:00-09:49). Song-side unpredictability helps (e.g. a beat pad flies in, "when is he actually going to start singing?") (09:20-09:40). Find something in the song a person can physically do on camera (09:52-10:07).
10. You may change the song to work better with the content, or choose the filming location first and build the song around it, as long as it is authentic to you (10:07-10:24).
11. Bandwagon effect: look more famous by surrounding yourself with people who look like your target audience (10:30-11:19). Low-budget version: group of artists, one content day, an Airbnb and a camera, each person takes the seat and plays their song (11:24-11:37). Not right for an intimate one-to-one song; use it when the "movie scene" for the song has many people (11:50-12:09).
12. Slide post: three-picture slide post copied and remixed; text on slides forms a conversation between two characters (curiosity/prediction even with still images), visuals congruent with the music ("could be played on mute and you'd still hear the music"); position the song so the lyrics appear on screen on the third slide at the beat drop; you do not control slide timing but you control the song position (12:11-14:52). Copying a format is fine because it is remixed for a different brand and audience (12:29-12:40).
13. Slide post adaptation: change the genre label and images; use viral relationship/travel "relationship goals" theme-page style imagery plus quotes, add your words and genre; taps people in and out of relationships (15:04-16:03). He mentions AI-generating the images (reference: painter Jack Vettriano via Pinterest), calling it controversial (16:03-16:19).
14. Universe building through candid takes: you do not need to be in the video, and should not lip-sync; a candid take of the universe/mood from the brand question, with a text hook and a desire element (16:22-18:28). Ideas for this song: humorous text hooks from the guy's or girl's perspective about attraction (18:03-18:22).
15. Captions: usually do not matter, but test them; one TV transition got similar views on IG and Facebook; the Facebook caption saying the song is out now / stream it was tracked via UTM links to 10k Spotify streams in 2 days. Not always the case, so experiment; generally show personality in the caption (18:34-19:36).
16. The plan: choose a song, choose the right part of the song, experiment with about five formats, one post a day for 30 days; then double down with 20 high-quality videos of the formats with outstanding results; claimed route to 100k monthly listeners in 30-60 days (19:54-20:30). No billboards/posters/ads needed, "just need the content to work"; then build connection via comments and stories (20:43-21:22).
17. Experiment with parts of the song too; for Nana's song only the start worked for social, because of the drop and a riser (21:42-22:07).
18. Three things that make a song part work: lyrics must be clear; action-based lyrics are best (e.g. stepping into the party, took my heart and threw it on the floor) so people can visualise them; and unpredictability - a chorus repeats, the hook can be better (22:07-22:44).
19. Patience: first month is all experimentation; failures still give data; many stop after 10 videos (23:06-23:26).
20. Then 20 consecutive posts of the successful format; if performance videos fail and transitions work, do 20 transitions (23:28-23:52).
21. While doubling down: 70-75% what works, 25% experiment; a strategy is not exhausted until about a year; artists promote the same song for 2 years because it works, only ~5% complain (24:03-24:22).
22. Diagnosing failure, two causes: (a) execution - honestly judge whether the idea was communicated as well as possible; if someone else's same idea looks better, it is your execution (24:40-25:07); (b) the format does not suit the audience - test 4-5 formats at least four times each; if all four fail, drop it and double down on what worked even if only a 20% increase (25:10-25:28). Something worth doubling down on should beat usual content by at least ~50k views; otherwise keep experimenting (25:28-25:46).
23. Song breakdown format: Charlie Puth style (spin the globe, collaborate with someone from that country; tapping odd objects and sampling them); viewers feel part of the process and predict how the song will turn out (25:51-26:18).
24. Bilingual artists: run multiple accounts (e.g. Spanish and English); audiences rarely see both since the algorithm is geo-targeted to local territory (26:46-27:18).
25. Authenticity: the authentic part is the music; the promotional vehicle can be staged (like a planned radio "surprise" visit) (27:55-28:26). Audiences usually do not detect it, and if they do they praise the creativity ("caught me") (28:30-29:11). But if the same person plays different roles (teacher, mom) across videos, use a different face (30:09-30:17).
26. Story continuation across videos: viral videos reach people who never saw you, so a video that depends on last week's episode will not go viral; clarity of communication drives take-off. Exception: Citizen's team built an orchestrated friends -> crush -> girlfriend arc over many videos (31:06-31:51, cut off).

## Process / checklists
1) Song -> brand movie-scene question. 2) Pick the song part (clear, action-based lyrics; unpredictability; drop/riser). 3) Choose ~5 formats that fit the song's mood. 4) One post a day for 30 days. 5) Review each: execution or format failure? Each format at least 4 tries. 6) Double down: 20 consecutive posts of winners, 70-75% winners / 25% experiments. 7) Connect via comments and stories.
