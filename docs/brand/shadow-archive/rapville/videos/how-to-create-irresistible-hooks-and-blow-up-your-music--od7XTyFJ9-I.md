# How to Create Irresistible Hooks (and blow up your music)
URL: https://youtu.be/od7XTyFJ9-I | Length: 8:05 | Views: 1,600 | Date: not in header

## Core idea
Success on short-form depends on the first 3 seconds, and the hook works through emotion, not a library of templates. He gives a three-step "viral hook formula" (dopamine tease, solidify the scroll stop, dopamine blaster) built on one pre-chosen emotion, then two more "hacks": text hooks/captions and borrowing familiar viral formats.

## Lessons and tactics
1. (00:00) The first 3 seconds of the content decide performance; he frames this as the core of a music career now.
2. (00:17) Don't collect a folder of viral hooks; learn the psychology and tactics behind them so you can apply them to your own material.
3. (00:35) Five steps total; the first three combine into a "viral hook formula". Steps 4 and 5 are separate "hacks".
4. (00:48) Mental model: viewers scroll looking for the next dopamine hit of emotion, and their tolerance rises daily. Pick one emotion as the "anchor" of the video before you make it.
5. (01:08) The six emotions he lists: love, heartbreak, cringe, shock, nostalgia, relatability. Use them in the song as well as in the content.
6. (01:24) Step 1, "dopamine tease": in the first seconds be very clear what story the video tells ("storytelling clarity"). This lets the wrong viewers scroll and the right ones (who can connect) stay.
7. (01:44) Easiest way to find the story: start from what the song is about and build the scenario from there.
8. (02:23) Step 2, "solidifying the scroll stop": the story must progress within milliseconds; first stun the viewer, establish the scenario, and tease what happens next so curiosity crowds out every other distraction.
9. (02:59) Step 3, "dopamine blaster" ("the sledgehammer"): the emotion chosen at the start must hit the viewer at full force, ideally via a twist they would not expect.
10. (03:54) He claims successful artists all use this three-step formula; it isn't limited to transition/clickbait content. It also works for slide posts: "three slides are always the key".
11. (04:14) The formula lets the song be "felt", not just heard; content visually represents the music. If the music is bad, the formula won't save it.
12. (04:32) Applying it to every video takes reps and practice.
13. (05:28) Hack 4, text hook and captions: on-screen text instantly tells the viewer what they get and gives readable context, because people consume content differently. He says text can be the difference between 200 and 20,000 views (claimed).
14. (06:05) A text hook can double as an "audience targeting hack": naming a nationality in the text (e.g. "Hungarian") targets that whole nation; comments confirm it worked (claimed).
15. (06:20) Storytelling visuals plus a curiosity-driven caption beats going straight into a singing video.
16. (06:26) Hack 5, familiarity: people are driven by content they see every day. Familiarity = comfort = longer watch time; unfamiliarity = confusion = scrolling.
17. (06:47) Use anything viral on the internet (not just music) as inspiration, e.g. take a prank video format and ask how to use it to trick people into hearing your song.
18. (07:06) When you scroll, switch on your "marketing brain" and collect ideas, rather than scrolling mindlessly.
19. (07:27) He says his own channel grew with no ads and no boosting.

## Examples he uses
- Laura (his artist): song about confronting a friend's abusive boyfriend. Video: caption along the lines of not angering a Hungarian, she breaks into the boyfriend's house, then (twist) gets thrown out. Emotion: shock. Comments about Hungarians making music (claimed).
- Anna (his artist): shown as proof text hooks change results; numbers not stated in captions beyond the 200 vs 20,000 claim.
- "Jo": stitched a viral prank (water balloons thrown from a car) and turned it into music marketing.
- His own page: nearly 10,000 followers in 60 days; "hundreds of artists" helped one-on-one (claimed).

## Process / checklists
Hook formula, in order:
1. Decide the anchor emotion before shooting (one of love, heartbreak, cringe, shock, nostalgia, relatability).
2. Dopamine tease: storytelling clarity in first seconds, derived from the song's subject.
3. Solidify the scroll stop: stun, establish scenario, tease next beat.
4. Dopamine blaster: deliver the emotion at full force, with an unexpected twist.
5. Add a text hook/caption (curiosity + optional audience-targeting word).
6. Base the format on something already familiar/viral.
