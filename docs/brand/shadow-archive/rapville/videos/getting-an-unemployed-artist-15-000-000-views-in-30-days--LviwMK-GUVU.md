# getting an unemployed artist 15,000,000 views in 30 days
URL: https://youtu.be/LviwMK-GUVU | Length: 10:18 | Views: 2,000 | Date: not in header

## Core idea
Don't reinvent the wheel: copy formats that already worked for another artist (here Citizen the Artist), but tailor each one to your own brand and song, because frame-for-frame copies flop. Once a format wins, post it repeatedly with variations, and repost good videos.

## Lessons and tactics
1. (00:00) Viral content is over-complicated; "steal" what already worked for other artists instead of inventing from scratch.
2. (00:46) Stolen format 1, "setup versus the shot": show the setup, then the shot.
3. (01:05) Do not copy frame for frame. Their straight copy of Citizen's train sequence got 2,000 views (claimed); they learned to tailor to music and brand.
4. (01:30) Start tailoring from the artist's brand traits (for Gary: controversial, entertainment-driven, "can do anything") and from the song (title "Ocean" pointed to a beach/ocean scenario).
5. (02:05) Deliberately leave a detail that makes viewers "pressed" (in this case sand/beach background that makes people doubt the setup is real). Viewers nitpicking (wind, sun, lights wrong) drives comments, comments drive engagement.
6. (03:10) Mindset claim: if you can't go viral or sustain it, it's not the algorithm, it's your "ego" (trying to do music, content, admin and social life yourself).
7. (03:54) Their method: figure out the brand, understand the music's vision, research competitors to steal from, then build a strategy tailored to that brand.
8. (04:00) Month 1: post 40 videos and test 10 stolen formats to see what works personally. Month 2: get eyeballs, tap the younger audience that drives trends, gain followers, double down on what works. Month 3 onward: you own an audience (followers, Spotify monthly listeners, people waiting for releases).
9. (04:36) Stolen format 2: Citizen's storyline series with his girlfriend ("telling my best friend / crush / girlfriend about my new song"). Re-shape it to match the song's mood: Gary's song is breakup/controversial, not love, so they made a "gold digger prank" skit.
10. (06:24) He openly says the prank is fake and "engineered for virality", and argues Citizen's are engineered too; it still lets personality show.
11. (06:55) Artists fail because they fear posting too much or repeating themselves. Once a format wins, post it until it stops working ("until people are sick of it").
12. (07:14) To repeat a winner, "rinse" it: different locations, different vibes, small tweaks (e.g. same prank in a car with a steering wheel, other people involved).
13. (07:33) Content pieces are assets: after you get traction or a viral moment, repost good earlier videos. Their 2,000-view first video later got 166,000 organically (claimed).
14. (07:59) If formats you copied (e.g. the Snapchat method) didn't work, you haven't found your "unique angle"; tailor copied strategy to your music and brand.
15. (08:18) Make songs in the studio "engineered for content first", with content ideas in mind (a song tied to an image like an ocean is easy to build ideas on).
16. (08:41) Controversy is called one of the main drivers of TikTok; be willing to try new things with entertainment at the heart.
17. (09:03) If it's "all about you", you overweight each idea and blame the algorithm when it flops; put the audience first, give the most value (on social, that means entertainment).
18. (09:33) Copying only works with a foundation: music right and brand positioned to go viral.
