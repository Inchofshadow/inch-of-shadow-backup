# How to Get Your First 10 Million Views as an Unknown Artist (We Have Done It 6 Times)

Presenter: Niall (named in the description)

## Core idea
He argues virality is decided before you press record, not by the algorithm. Define "your one person", the specific listener the music is for. Content that speaks to them precisely gets shared, and sharing is the engine of virality. Three decisions follow from knowing that person: visual world, hook and format.

## Lessons and tactics
1. (00:00) Most artists solve the wrong problem: algorithm, posting schedule, trends, captions, hashtags.
2. (00:23) Three fallacies: good music will find the right person; consistent posting makes the algorithm push you eventually; copying big artists will partly stick. He says none of these is why artists blow up.
3. (01:03) "The algorithm does not decide what goes viral." The deciding choice happens before filming.
4. (01:10) Define **your one person**: the specific individual who, on seeing the content, feels it was made by someone who already knows them.
5. (01:28) Narrowing to one person does not lower views. Precision makes them **share**, sharing is the engine of virality, and the algorithm just distributes what's already spreading.
6. (01:53) Reframe the question from "how do I get the algorithm to push this?" to "how do I get my one person to share it?"
7. (02:00) Three decisions downstream of the one person:
   - **Visual world** (02:05): not vague aesthetic. What does the one person see every day that would make them stop mid-scroll and feel it's made for them?
   - **Hook** (02:18): not "another silly trend". The specific opening line, image or story they can't scroll past because it names something they feel but have never seen put into words.
   - **Format** (02:35): formats are remixed, not invented; every viral video runs on a format that has worked hundreds of times. Pick the format that matches how the one person consumes content. Right message plus right world in the wrong packaging still misses.
8. (03:01) Skip the one-person step and all three decisions are wrong or guesses.
9. (03:13) Case study method: start by defining the one person, not by looking at the music. Adriano's one person: moves through life like a film; has a relationship with beauty and slowness; finds most modern music too loud and obvious; wants an artist nobody has found yet because discovering first is part of their identity; shares music taste the way others share secrets.
10. (03:52) Execution for that person: no cool location or expensive lighting. A **3-slide carousel** using 17th-century romantic Italian paintings, with the painted characters forming a storyline:
    - Slide 1: "relax my love, it's just a chill song by an Italian artist nobody knows" (paraphrased; captioned twice with slightly different wording)
    - Slide 2: "but did you even listen to the lyrics?"
    - Slide 3: the lyrics, with the song kicking in on time.
11. (04:24) Why it works: the one person already has a relationship with those paintings and that aesthetic.
12. (04:43) The caption never says "listen to my music / stream my song". The "calm down, babe" opener signals this isn't a music post begging for streams. The lyrics question opens a **loop** that can only close by swiping and listening to the end of the snippet.
13. (05:15) The niche (Italian) framing was an advantage, not a limitation. This person doesn't respond to high-production performance reels. The slide format feels intimate, "something you would just stumble across", and that feeling drives shares.
14. (06:04) Finding the one person is hard. Sit with the music and ask who it will actually connect with, not who you want it to connect with. This may reveal a gap between the artist you are and the artist you want to be.
15. (06:34) For an existing but dormant audience, the one-person question becomes: who already follows, and what would make them feel the account came back to life for them (Eric Bellinger).
16. (06:58) Some one-persons respond to genuinely entertaining storylines rather than promoted videos (Robin Bra, name as captioned; stopped all ads).
17. (07:21) Earn attention on social, and playlists reach out to you instead of you paying for playlist promotion (claimed for Robin Bra's release).
18. (08:02) Action step: before your next content is filmed, write **one paragraph about your one person**: age; what their environment feels like; what they already scroll; what they already share; what they'd send a friend at midnight and why; what that says about them; why they listen to your music.
19. (08:23) If you can't write it, you don't have a content-ideas, schedule or videographer problem, you have a "one person problem".

## Examples he uses
- Adriano (Italian jazz rap): claimed 200 followers at start; first week / first three videos claimed 1M+ views; comments all in Italian asking for the song; claimed 2,000 to 100,000+ monthly listeners in four months, organic, no ads; now full-time and booking shows in Italy (claimed).
- Eric Bellinger: claimed 1M+ dormant followers; first video built on the one-person answer claimed 30M+ views (other videos say 25M).
- Robin Bra (captioned name; the same artist is called "RB" elsewhere): claimed spending thousands a month on ads with no stream movement; stopped ads; claimed 30k to 127k+ monthly listeners in 2 weeks; best-performing song of his career with the lowest spend; claimed playlists reached out to him.
- Paul Brady (Irish rapper): one person = Irish people; claimed 500k views across first five posts.
- Overall: claimed six unknown artists, six genres, 10M+ views combined.

## Process / checklists
- One-person paragraph (fields listed in item 18) -> visual world -> hook -> format -> film.
