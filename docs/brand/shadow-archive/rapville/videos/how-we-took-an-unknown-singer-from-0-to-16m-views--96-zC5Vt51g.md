# How We Took an Unknown Singer from 0 to 16M Views
URL: https://youtu.be/96-zC5Vt51g | Length: 15:27 | Views: 682

## Core idea (2-3 sentences)
A 30-day case study of Bianca, a fitness/lifestyle creator releasing her debut single "Renaissance". Every video is judged against her pre-existing baseline views; half the month is shot first to test formats, the data picks winners, and the second half doubles down on the winners by "spinning the angle" or merging two winning formats. Attention content and conversion content are different strategies.

## Lessons and tactics
1. Start from the artist's current baseline (average views on existing content) and judge every campaign video against it as a multiple (e.g. "2x baseline") (00:28-00:55).
2. If the audience follows you for non-music content, they will be confused when you become a singer; the content must bridge that gap and establish you as a legitimate artist to old and new viewers (01:00-01:19).
3. Know the brand and the song before making content: here the song concept is rebirth / new chapter, so the content is too (01:21-01:30).
4. Seasonal and timing relevance: a summer pop record has seasonal relevance; also consider day of week and what is happening in culture. Match the viewer's mood (happy party video on Friday night, in-your-feels on Monday) (01:32-01:56).
5. Three execution pillars for this artist: sunny locations to match the song, loud colours and extreme visual identity that stands out on the feed, and "girl boss energy" (e.g. sitting on her husband while he does press-ups); extreme visuals get you noticed the first time (01:58-02:24).
6. "Target audience mirroring effect": put people who look like the target audience (here girls) in the videos; when viewers see themselves they are more likely to watch (02:24-02:34).
7. Performance energy/conviction matters; rehearse in the mirror before filming and dial in a unique energy for camera; people do not want "another lip syncing artist" (02:36-02:58).
8. Shoot half the month's videos in one shoot, wait for the data, then shoot the second half; the goal is to find which format clicks with this song and audience, to build a repeatable viral format, not just one viral hit (03:01-03:25).
9. Formats tested, in groups:
   - Performance format: (a) clone effect - repeatable, needs no characters, locations or situations, good at selling music, "rip this format" (03:38-04:17); (b) performance grid - viewer scans five angles (splits, sofa, phone, book, dancing), each angle must be visually appealing and serve a different purpose; use a cool location rather than a white room (04:19-05:08).
   - Transition format ("the Rap Ville classic"): (a) Snapchat hook - caught recording in public, straight into the song; he prefers more tension and storyline in a Snapchat hook (05:10-05:50); (b) a second Snapchat hook with more surprise, shock, emotion (05:52-06:17).
   - BTS vs the shot format, "the most opportunity for creativity and going viral": (a) wing-mirror BTS hook - mistake: song was not playing echoey in the hook, so the transition was too abrupt and invites scrolling; the song should build into the transition (06:17-06:59); (b) split-screen POV - main camera performance on top, BTS of the eccentric videographer on bottom; concept good, but location too cluttered and background lighting weak (06:59-07:29); (c) TV transition / spinning-chair TV transition - the outlier; works to "launch brands off the floor" (07:31-07:58).
   - Bandwagon effect: friends/attractive people around enjoying it; psychology "if these people love it, I will too"; a "fake it till you make it" approach - look famous rather than leaning on smallness/authenticity; but it is about framing, the concept still has to be viral, well executed and on-brand, "you can't just buy a ton of chains" (07:58-08:50).
   - Visual hooks: (a) paparazzi hook - phones come into frame and break the scrolling rhythm; his favourite for visual identity, but weakest format (09:02-09:29); (b) the zoom - simple, effective in general, did not work for her (09:29-09:37).
10. Winners: transition format and bandwagon format; true outlier: TV transition; visual hooks were dropped after the first test (09:38-09:44, 14:59-15:03).
11. Doubling down is not only putting a spin on a format; sometimes it is merging two winning formats (e.g. Snapchat hook with storyline + friends vibing = Snapchat hook + bandwagon) (09:44-10:23).
12. Turn a universal meme into a content idea (the "husband meme" spin on a Snapchat hook) (10:26-10:50).
13. Use the seasonal angle in the double-down (pool party vibe for the summer song) (10:53-11:08).
14. TV transition spin: a fake walk in front of the TV, plus a "connector" - the boyfriend coming into frame in the background links the hook to the performance; connecting hook and performance is "a very important aspect" (11:13-11:52). Another spin: boxing / girl boss energy; he would have used a more extreme connector (punch the boyfriend, then performance) (12:05-12:30).
15. Biggest lesson: when the algorithm signals a video went viral, the question is not whether to recreate it but how - double down by "spinning the angle" (12:37-12:50).
16. Attention vs conversion: do not reject viral content because it "doesn't convert"; you need attention first. Viral hooks, formats and trends bring people into your world; moving them down the funnel takes different content (framing, psychology, sales, experience) (13:42-14:25).
17. The system: a good song alone is not enough; create high-quality content in batch so you are not chasing the post on the day; test for virality, double down on what works, throw away what performs badly (14:31-15:03).

## Examples he uses (all numbers claimed)
- Bianca, debut single "Renaissance": baseline stated as both 30-50k and 20-40k average views (00:34-00:47); shot in LA.
- Clone effect 87k (over 2x); performance grid 60k (2x); Snapchat hook 140k (3.5x); second Snapchat hook 80k (2x); wing-mirror BTS 80k (2x); split-screen POV 65k (1.7x); spinning-chair TV transition 2.4M (60x); bandwagon 85k (2.1x); paparazzi hook 55k (also said "just 50,000"); zoom: no number, "didn't work".
- Double-downs: Snapchat+bandwagon 71k; husband meme 70k; pool party 70k; TV transition with walk + boyfriend connector 17.5M (400x); TV transition boxing spin 100k after 24 hours (3x).
- 30 days: 30 posts, 21M views total; 10k views funneled to the music video; 13k Spotify streams on the track (13:00-13:20). Title says 16M; intro says 16M in first month.

## Process / checklists
1) Measure baseline views. 2) Define brand + song concept + seasonal timing + execution pillars. 3) Rehearse performance in the mirror. 4) Shoot half the month in one shoot across several format families. 5) Post, compare each video to baseline. 6) Bin losing formats, keep winners and outliers. 7) Second shoot: spin the angle of winners or merge two winners. 8) Next phase: add conversion-type content to move attention down the funnel.
