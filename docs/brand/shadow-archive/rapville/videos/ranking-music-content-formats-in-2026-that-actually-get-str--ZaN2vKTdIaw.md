# Ranking Music Content Formats in 2026 (that actually get streams)
URL: https://youtu.be/ZaN2vKTdIaw | Length: 20:35 | Views: 9,583 | Date: 2026-03-20

## Core idea
A tier list of 12 short-form music content formats, each scored 1-5 on virality/brand awareness, conversion (does it make people stream) and ease of producing. Top-of-funnel formats (setup vs shot, Snapchat method) win reach; emotion/social-proof formats (reaction, slides) win streams; generic text hooks and mini music videos are bottom tier.

## Lessons and tactics
Scoring axes (00:00): (1) views and brand awareness, (2) conversion (streams), (3) simplicity/ease of copying. He says tested across "hundreds of projects" (claimed).

The ranked formats (score = virality / conversion / ease, as stated; "n/s" = not stated):
1. (00:39) Snapchat method: an engineered scenario that looks like a real-life moment thanks to a Snapchat-style caption, then a seamless transition into the high-quality performance/music video. Works because it lowers the viewer's guard (looks like a real viral moment, not "song out now"). Intention: high reach, a "clickbait strategy", works with zero followers. Criticisms he has heard: "washed", copycat, cringe (done in public), "dumbed down". Make the scenario match the song's message (Laura's video = confronting an abusive ex, as in the song). Conversion: not always high-quality eyeballs, "not bad, not the best" (n/s). Ease 2/5: transition timing, camera quality in the transition, and how well the idea matches the song are what make it work. Tier A; "brand awareness, zero-follower strategy"; use it if you haven't built your brand.
2. (02:36) Behind-the-scenes vs the shot: show the setup, then reveal the finished high-quality shot. Works via curiosity payoff (open loop / close loop, question / answer). Intention: watch time, shares, "how did you do that" comments. Downside: if the final shot isn't far better than the BTS there is no contrast and it flops. 5 / 2 / 1 (the shot must look excellent; many failed attempts at the TV transition). Tier S; top-of-funnel brand awareness for artists with or without followers.
3. (04:22) Reaction videos: film someone (person next to you, mom, girlfriend, crush, a stranger, a group) reacting to your song in real time. Works via social proof and emotion. Their reaction should mirror what you want the viewer to feel. Downside: bad acting feels staged or boring, kills trust. Fix: rehearse and script it. 3 / 5 / 4. "Emotion-driven content always gets more streams." Tier S.
4. (06:01) Behind-the-scenes tease (BTST): post 1 shows the full finished shot; post 2 shows only the BTS and never the shot, so viewers go to your profile to find post 1. Intention: posts fuel each other; profile visits and follows. Downside: needs a very creative concept (example: green screen, slow-motion fall onto a bear trap) to create enough desire. 5 / 1 / 1. Pure brand awareness; needs other content to funnel to streams (he mentions an artist "J O" (unclear) using ManyChat to raise conversion). Tier B.
5. (07:31) Bandwagon effect: like a reaction video but at a house party with lots of people. Works via FOMO + social proof; you look more famous, "people are sheep". Downside: less wholesome, less personality, more about the music than you. 3 / 5 / 1 (or lower: needs many friends, a studio, a top editor). In his tests it didn't go that viral. Tier C.
6. (09:13) Visual hooks: a scroll-stopping visual (movement, prop) in the first 1-2 seconds while the song plays; keep hooking: example had three hooks in a 30 s video, resetting attention every ~10 s. Downside: little emotion, saturated. 3 / 3 / 5. Tier C.
7. (10:20) Text hooks: iPhone shot of the artist lip-syncing/standing/looking at camera with text on screen. Works because reading keeps people watching. Downside: looks like every other artist "begging for attention"; if it goes viral you are boxed in and unlikely to repeat. He calls generic ones (e.g. "fan base so small it could fit on a bus") the worst format; good text hooks are possible. 1 / 1 / 5. Tier D.
8. (11:41) Slide posts: pictures that tell a story. Like the Snapchat method: picture 1 = hook, picture 2 = curiosity/retention, picture 3 = the song/transition moment. Intention: storytelling that matches the song's emotion via characters, vibes and on-screen text. Downside: doesn't build a brand; the artist is rarely shown (images likely from Pinterest); song-driven. Volume game: make ~50 and one will likely hit. 30 slide posts for 30 days can be made in 2-3 hours. 3 / 5 / 5. Tier A.
9. (12:42) Performance grids: multiple angles of the performance on screen at once; viewer scans angles, stays engaged. Intention: world building, polished/famous look. Downside: looking cool isn't virality; only works with a semi-decent following, not with zero followers. 2 / 2 / 3 (camera, location, visual identity). Tier C, along with "all other performance-driven formats".
10. (13:48) Movie scene format: a cinematic video with characters and extreme emotions like a film scene. Works because viewers want to see the story unfold. Intention: "universe building"; the song becomes the soundtrack to a relatable storyline/emotion. Downside: movies don't go viral on social, so it still needs hooks and shocking moments (as in the Snapchat method). Scores n/s. Tier A, if you can write a storyline and characters that match the song.
11. (14:51) Mini music video: clips from the same angle cut together; switches keep it dynamic; intention is just selling the music. Only works with a following. 1 / 3 / 4. Tier D ("if you're still doing this in 2026 you're living in 2019", unless you are already big).
12. (15:33) Seasonal adaptation: any format tailored to a season/event (New Year's countdown, Capricorn season). Works like hopping on a massive trend that everyone experiences together. Downside: if the song gets too tied to the season, engagement can collapse after it, and the next song may feel disappointing. 4 / 3 (higher if the song itself is about the season) / 4. Tier C; only usable at that moment.
13. (17:10) Geo-targeting: any format tailored to people of a specific nation so it feels made for them (flags on screen, naming a nationality in the text hook). Works best for smaller countries / language barriers; harder for English-speaking countries like the UK. 4 / 3 / 4. Tier B. Subtle version: national visual cues (e.g. UK rap in front of a red bus or phone box).

Other points:
14. (19:04) His diagnosis of three problems: not knowing these formats = "expertise problem"; can't match others' output volume/quality = "team problem"; too time-consuming = time problem.
15. (19:26) Many formats depend heavily on execution quality.

## Examples he uses (numbers claimed)
- Laura: Snapchat method video, confronting abusive ex, matches song message; also geo-targeting via "Hungarian" text hook.
- DeCarlo (also spelled "the Carlo"): BTS vs shot (TV setup) ~2M views; a performance-grid video 1M+.
- Eric Bellinger: BTS vs shot, 30M views.
- Citizen the Artist: "pioneered" BTS vs shot, ~100M views; also a New Year's countdown seasonal video.
- Adry: slide posts promoting his EP, now ~1M streams.
- Zaina: movie scene format, "girl entering a new big city for the first time" storyline.
- MRG: Capricorn season video in a petrol station with text on screen; went very viral, then engagement dropped after the season ended. Song name "Rest in Peace to My Ego" (from captions).
- Caldo: Italian and English flags on each video (song switches languages); viral moment.
- Cairo Keys (spelling unclear): exception for mini music videos.
- Scotty (Rapville creative director): described as the man behind Citizen, helping him to 1M followers in a year.

## Process / checklists
Tier summary: S = BTS vs shot, reaction videos. A = Snapchat method, slide posts, movie scene. B = BTS tease, geo-targeting. C = bandwagon, visual hooks, performance grids (and other performance formats), seasonal adaptation. D = text hooks, mini music video.
