## Core idea
The "RapVille Fanbase Growth Roadmap" has four stages: (1) Brand and Music, (2) Go Viral, (3) World Building, (4) Conversion. Brand comes first: he says campaigns do 10x better when it is dialled in. Ideas come from stripping viral videos to their bare-bones principles and remixing them for your brand and song. Winners get doubled down with small angle tweaks, each song gets its own world, and streams follow from all of this, helped by a few conversion hacks.

## Lessons and tactics
### Stage 1: Brand and Music (00:00-07:59)
1. (00:16) Getting brand and music right first gives 10x better results than jumping straight to ideas and filming (his claim from experience).
2. (00:38) Brand isn't just aesthetic or visual identity, which is only "one or two arms of the wheel".
3. (01:04) The brand has three parts, the "three A's": **Art, Artist, Audience**. You need the music (or it isn't music marketing), the artist (or it's just trending sounds) and the audience.
4. (01:34) Under **Art**: (a) what problem your music solves for the listener; (b) the sound, feeling or emotion you're known for; (c) engineer it for virality.
5. (01:55) Under **Artist**: (a) **unique angle**, the one thing nobody can replicate; (b) **character, values, personality**, how you show up on camera; (c) **visual identity**: clothes, but also the video background, the characters and the world you build for the music.
6. (02:32) Under **Audience**: (a) who it's for and why they care, i.e. the one specific person (his own channel: artists only); (b) **your relationship** with them: icon / hard to reach (Drake, Justin Bieber) vs best friend (JVKE-style social artists talking face to face); (c) give them **something to belong to**, and make clear what your music is *for* (e.g. one artist for melancholy, another for going out to party).
7. (03:22) Art pillar example (KP): random performance reels averaging 10-15k views. After a branding call they heard the sadness in the song. Idea: he voice-notes his mum ("what time's dinner, I'm still out grinding"), then the song starts. An emotional, branded intro sets the viewer up so the song "hits 10 times harder".
8. (04:03) Artist pillar example (Citizen): unique angle = filmmaking quality; character = "lover boy", told as a story across car reaction videos (friends -> crush -> asking her out; he is in love, she isn't, a familiar movie storyline); visual identity = consistently in the car, locked in, glasses on.
9. (04:50) Audience pillar examples: Laura (Hungarian) with a Snapchat-style text "this is why you don't piss off a Hungarian", so Hungarians know it's for them. Adri (Italian rapper): slide post using paintings by "Jack Vettriano", whom he calls a famous Italian painter. Italian music plus a matching aesthetic makes the target audience more receptive, and the paintings also matched the song's romantic emotion.
10. (05:39) **Engineer your song for virality**. He says it's your art and up to you, but these are themes he has seen raise the odds:
    - **The Citizen method** (06:00): lyrics that are clear and **action-based**, so they can be acted out ("I caught you falling for me" -> he catches her). This makes ideas easier and more viral.
    - **Unpredictable moment** (06:16): beat drops, rises, high vocal shifts. These hook viewers sonically rather than visually.
    - **Emotion obvious instantly** (06:29): e.g. he gets slapped and the lyric is "rest in peace to my ego". Visuals match the music.
    - **The "central C" method** (06:39; name as captioned, possibly "Central Cee"; unclear): start the song with an outrageous opener, a musical hook so outlandish people can't scroll away. He gives two shock-lyric examples (one attributed to "Doja", both captioned unclearly).
    - **The DC3 method** (07:06): create your own genre or lane by mixing two worlds and be the **first mover** in your market (DC3: UK rap + Christian music; Adri: Italian rap + jazz).
11. (07:35) **Action point**: define your unique angle, because everything flows from it. It can be the music (if unique), religion, the way you film, the way you look, who you are.

### Stage 2: Go Viral (07:59-21:43)
12. (08:17) Problem: "post and pray", with no system for ideas, shoots or editing and no time (most artists aren't full-time).
13. (08:41) Career move: remove yourself from marketing as much as possible by hiring a team or finding someone on the same page (friend, another artist). He says that without a team "you've got no chance".
14. (09:02) Mindset shift (credited to Citizen): learn to love the process (creativity, shoot-day fun, the 10/10 edit coming back). He says energy is the difference between viral and flop; "if you don't want to be there, don't be there".
15. (09:37) **Rehearse in the mirror** before ideas and shoot day: who you want to come across as on camera; what performances in viral videos look like; what your unique angle brings; what to wear. The worst thing is looking like you're faking it, which is how you get called a "cringe artist".
16. (10:06) **Idea setup**: the music playing on your phone; your **branding document** on the monitor (who the idea is for); social media or a **viral video bank** to research concepts that already went viral. "Inspiration equals creativity", not copycatting.
17. (10:44) **Turn any idea into your own** ("bare-bones and remix formula"):
    - Step 1: find it. Scroll even for 2 hours and save every music-marketing reel.
    - Step 2: strip it to its bare-bones principles.
    - Then spin it with your brand and your song's concept.
18. (10:58) Example: DC3's **"Snapchat method"** (normal everyday situation, then as the emotion hits a snap cut to a cinematic close-up music video) became Laura's version. Hungarian brand plus a song about confronting an abusive ex: she tries to break into the ex's house, tugs the door, gets in, disappears, is banged onto the floor, then the music video starts.
19. (11:42) Example: inspiration from "Pertinence" (name as captioned, unclear): 10-15 s casual relatable moment (greeting passers-by, "I don't care if they're staring"), then stops talking and bursts into a high-energy performance matching the song. KP got the same principle in reverse: sad and emotional (voice note to mum, puts the phone down, performs).
20. (12:49) Example: merge two formats. Citizen **TV transition** (standing in front of a TV, then transition to actually being at that location) + Dice's **bandwagon effect** (surrounded by people, zooms on their reactions). For Desiree: TV transition with a party on the TV, matched to her party song, after seeing the TV transition work for her that month.
21. (13:36) Cheat code: with 15-30 ideas, use only **one or two locations** (Mike Manos example). It saves travel and setup time and gives the song "one world to live in".
22. (14:07) **Pre-production list**: find a videographer, shot list, buy props, hire extras, find or hire locations, plan a route. To film 30 reels in a day you can't lose 90 minutes to a wrong turn.
23. (14:50) **Videographer: vet and train**.
    - Vet (interview): what camera/lens; what equipment (lighting, tripods, boom stands); look at their portfolio; ask about **short-form content experience**. Biggest mistake: hiring a music-video videographer and expecting virality.
    - Train (15:40): a PDF with all ideas, shot list and concept + a **15-minute Loom** walking through each idea; follow up to check they watched it.
24. (15:53) **Pre-production call** with the videographer, extras and team. Not to explain the ideas, but to problem-solve shots, get the most out of the shoot and plan location and route.
25. (16:19) **Master sheet** (one doc for everyone on shoot day): shot list per idea, props and extras, locations, timing, **song timestamps**, outfit notes.
26. (16:44) **Shoot day**: re-state the goal (30 reels in 6-8 hours); the artist is the "CEO" and mission driver who sets the pace; song timestamps on your phone into a speaker; lift your energy fast if you're down.
27. (17:12) **What to watch for on shoot day**: if you feel lost in the first 45 minutes, stop and re-prep for tomorrow; check the first couple of clips, and if the videographer is "trash" stop the shoot; team chemistry shows up in the videos.
28. (17:37) **Editing essentials**: transition timing (smooth, perfectly timed cut); lyric font and colour (branded, complementing the video); colour grading (cinematic); **sound effects**, "arguably the most important". In the hook transition the audio is echoey and slightly distorted, then full volume and clean when the cinematic part hits ("a mini hook"). Add slaps, car sounds, wind, footsteps to heighten the senses.
29. (18:44) **Editing review**: get a second opinion from someone who knows what they're doing.
30. (19:01) Don't edit yourself. Every part needs a specialist. **Hiring rule for editors**: they've made a viral video before, in any niche (business, food, lifestyle), because they can be trained into music.
31. (19:42) **Posting**: "don't miss a day". No hack: what matters is how good, engaging and on-brand the video is. Hashtags are "irrelevant". Description = the first thing that comes to mind relating to the video. Timing = when the audience is most active, usually after work, **4-9 pm local**. Stay consistent with a **content calendar**.
32. (20:12) **Seven-video buffer**: have the editor deliver 7 finished videos before you start posting, so slow edits or revisions don't break the schedule.
33. (20:40) Two team options: figure it out with friends and family (costs time "failing") or hire a team.

### Stage 3: World Building (21:43-25:58)
34. (21:52) World building sets up conversion and sustains virality beyond one viral moment: meaningful views, not empty ones.
35. (22:13) Problem: you went viral but don't know why, and the momentum fades.
36. (22:28) Move: **treat viral results like data**, not luck. Strip back why your own video worked, **tweak the angle slightly**, do it again, and build a world around the format.
37. (22:50) Dice example (not a client): bandwagon effect done normally, then a white version and a blue version. The tweak can be subtle but makes it novel, so a strategy lasts longer.
38. (23:15) Adri example: the Vettriano paintings ran out (recycling them worked less well). Pivot: keep the principles (romantic, conversational between characters, in-the-moment authentic pictures with on-screen text) but use **AI-generated "real human" photos** instead of paintings. That rebuilt momentum.
39. (24:00) Gary Gray example: first TV transition video claimed 15M views; repeated with ocean, park and car backgrounds. "Instead of making up different strategies", do the same one with different spins.
40. (24:25) **Song universe**: every song needs its own world. Citizen's last three tracks: "Sunkisses" = nature world, greens, white clothing; "Vertigo" = snowy winter / mountains world; "Time" = Miami world, blue car, glasses, city backgrounds.
41. (25:03) Why it matters: a distinct world makes each song memorable (more likely streamed) and ties the song to an emotion.
42. (25:14) Laza (name as captioned) example: profile grid in distinct colour blocks (blue, red, green, black and white). Loading the profile instantly reads as an artist and as art.
43. (25:37) **Write the song with the world in mind**, not the world after the song (Citizen said he watched car-racing clips while writing "Time").

### Stage 4: Conversion (25:58-end)
44. (26:18) Problem: some numbers and followers, but streaming is "down the drain".
45. (26:28) Conversion isn't a hack. It's the compounding result of branding + viral videos + world building done intentionally. Content must inspire viewers to switch platforms. If you post random stuff not linked to the music, that is why there are no streams. Hacks exist but "you cannot rely on them".
46. (27:14) **Test songs**: a song has the viral edge or it doesn't. Run format experiments *and* song experiments. Read the comments and DMs for **song response** ("this track is fire, where do I get it?", "I love your music"). If ~25 videos have low views *and* poor song response, it's likely the song.
47. (28:10) **Conversion hack 1: plant the comment**. A friend, family member or burner account comments "what song is this?"; you pin it and reply with the song name, which plants the seed to go to Spotify.
48. (28:44) **Conversion hack 2: ManyChat automation**. In the caption, ask viewers to comment a keyword and you'll send the track; ManyChat auto-DMs. He says it can also build an email list, gain followers and handle fan conversations.
49. (29:12) **Trend domino effect**: once you have attention, give fans a reason to make content with your song, e.g. a contest. Michael Bibi: post a sunset video with the caption "best video with a sunset gets free tickets to my Ibiza show" (paraphrased). This "engineers a trending sound".
50. (29:54) **Soulless page strategy**: a separate account with influencer-style content where every video uses your song (Camille, via their creator network).

## Process / checklists
- **Idea workstation**: music on phone + branding doc on monitor + viral video bank.
- **Idea method**: find (save every reel) -> strip to bare bones -> remix with brand + song concept -> 15-30 ideas -> 1-2 locations.
- **Pre-production**: videographer (vet: lens, equipment, portfolio, short-form experience; train: PDF + 15-min Loom + follow-up) -> shot list -> props -> extras -> locations -> route -> pre-production call -> master sheet (shot list, props/extras, locations, timing, song timestamps, outfit notes).
- **Shoot day**: restate the goal (30 reels / 6-8 h); artist = CEO; song timestamps on a speaker; abort rules (lost at 45 min; bad first clips); watch team chemistry.
- **Edit**: transition timing, branded lyric font/colour, colour grade, SFX (echoey/distorted hook -> clean full volume at the transition, plus foley), second-opinion review; the editor must have made a viral video before.
- **Post**: daily, 4-9 pm local, content calendar, 7-video buffer, ignore hashtags, simple description.
- **After virality**: treat it as data -> tweak the angle -> repeat -> per-song world.
- **Conversion**: test songs via comment/DM response -> plant + pin comment -> ManyChat keyword DM -> trend domino (contest) -> soulless page.
