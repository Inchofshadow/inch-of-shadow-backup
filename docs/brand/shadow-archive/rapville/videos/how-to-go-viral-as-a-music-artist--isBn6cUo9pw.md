# How To Go Viral (As A Music Artist)
URL: https://youtu.be/isBn6cUo9pw · Length 14:06 · Views 1,983 · Speaker: Niall

## Core idea
Five components, woven into two threads, make an artist recognisable in the first second before the song plays. Thread 1 = brand (visual identity/costume + character, shaped by the audience); thread 2 = what the camera sees, decided by the song (performance energy, visual world/setting, edit). The goal is a repeatable viral format instead of one unexplained viral moment.

## Lessons and tactics
1. The five components (per description): visual identity, character, performance, setting, editing; they weave into two threads (brand / song) (00:00, 01:31-01:35, 08:28-08:43).
2. "Visual no man's land": not bad enough to laugh at, not good enough to remember (00:55-01:04).
3. Diagnostic: ask someone who has heard your music to describe it; generic answers ("good music", "it's a vibe") mean nothing unique is coming across, and the same is true of your marketing (01:04-01:26).
4. Defaulting to your own taste lands you somewhere generic (01:53-01:57).
5. The test for a recognisable brand: "If I put on one of your outfits, would people know it's you?" (01:58-02:03).
6. Passing the test is not one outfit forever; the "thread" can be a pattern, but it must be predictable (02:03-03:11).
7. Tyler, The Creator model: each album owns a colour (Igor pink, Chromakopia green) and a full theatrical costume/character (blonde wig + pastel suit; military uniform + mask); nothing carries over, but "there's always a costume"; reinvention is the consistency (02:16-03:11).
8. Oliver Tree model: a constant anchor (the bowl cut) while outfits change and get more extreme each era (giant jeans + scooter, jetpack jacket, cowboy era); rule: absurd, and under the bowl cut; the anchor gives familiarity across eras (03:13-04:01).
9. Keep the anchor past the point a normal artist would have changed it; that turns a look into a brand (03:53-04:01).
10. Engineer the look to provoke a question ("is this a joke?"); that question is the funnel; people found Oliver Tree through the visual, not the music (04:04-04:33).
11. Character = who wears the costume. Standing out and embarrassment feel the same; fear makes artists pick "slightly interesting" (04:35-05:16).
12. Workaround: don't do it as yourself; build a character and let the character wear it; the character absorbs the embarrassment (05:18-05:49).
13. Alter ego is a dial: from full absurdity to "just you with the fear turned off" (Beyonce's Sasha Fierce, built because she was too shy to perform) (05:51-06:24).
14. Exercise: create a character with a new name; decide what they'd wear, how they'd stand, what they'd never do; then ask what the character would post (06:25-06:40).
15. The character is "permission" to be as strange as your creativity rather than your ego allows (06:45-06:56).
16. Rule for all visual decisions: your audience decides, not you. Pick the one person you make music for and get specific: age, content they consume, artists they love, videos they'd screenshot and share. Build the character to be admirable to them; pick locations because that person thinks they're cool (07:08-07:44).
17. If you have an existing audience, build the visual identity from what they already love about you (Bianca Taylor) (07:46-08:26).
18. Thread 2: the song is the blueprint for the video; three decisions it makes for you (08:32-08:58).
19. Decision 1, energy: every performance video is basically a person looking into the camera and moving their mouth; energy is the main variable. It is a spectrum decided by the music, from completely still/emotionless to exaggerated and fully committed; both ends must be extreme; the middle ("no man's land") never works (08:58-10:56).
20. Decision 2, visual world: every song is about something; choose a visual people already know so they relate before a note is sung; familiarity makes people stay; ideally the visual makes the song "guessable" (10:58-12:05).
21. Make the feeling visible with on-screen text that changes over the video (Cats and Dogs: "99 problems" counting down to zero as a drink is handed over) (12:05-12:23).
22. Decision 3, the edit: every pause, drop and switch in the track signals a change on screen; "when something changes in the music, something changes on screen"; cuts disconnected from the music confuse viewers (12:25-13:12).
23. A simple setup (one artist, white room) works if lights and position changes land on the beat; no big location or budget needed (12:41-13:05).
24. Pre-shoot brief, three things on one page: the song's energy in one word; what the song is about in one sentence; the specific effect only possible with this song (13:20-13:34).
25. The song components keep videos fresh; the brand threads give the audience something to remember; together they give a repeatable viral format "views on demand" rather than one unexplained viral moment (13:34-13:51).

## Examples he uses
- Oliver Tree: unnoticed for ~15 years, rebrand (bowl cut, giant jeans, tiny scooter) -> billions of streams (claimed) (00:07-00:31).
- Bianca Taylor: from vegan fitness influencer to artist; visual identity built on her audience's love of her "girl boss" confidence and boldness; 16M views in first 30 days on debut single (claimed) (00:33-00:53, 07:46-08:18).
- Tyler, The Creator: per-album colour + costume/character.
- Beyonce: Sasha Fierce alter ego to overcome shyness.
- Citizen: still, emotionless performance that matches the numbness of the song; "Time" track's back-and-forth effect only possible because of how the music sounds (09:22-09:33, 13:14-13:20).
- Scott Helman ("Mr. Record Label"): lifts his top lip, attacks the camera, reads as anger (09:42-09:56).
- Steven Ridley (their client): flamboyant, huge smiles, theatrical; millions of views playing piano in public (claimed) (09:56-10:13).
- "Contrast versus" / "Khan" (name unclear in captions): blew up partly via streamer reaction videos; ran through New York streets singing, overconfident with strangers (10:15-10:39).
- EV, "Broken Britain": opens with someone bottled in a beer garden; familiar to British viewers (11:15-11:37).
- Cats and Dogs (Polish producers): beach, two mates, boombox, doing nothing; "99 problems" text counting down (11:39-12:23).
- "Love Shy" (name unclear), "Like That": one artist in a white room, lights flashing and rapid position changes on beat (12:39-13:05).

## Process / checklists
- Brand thread: pass the outfit test; choose an anchor or a predictable pattern; build a character (name, clothing, stance, never-do list, what it would post); define the one target person (age, content, artists, shareable videos).
- Song thread / pre-shoot brief: energy in one word; meaning in one sentence; the effect only this song allows. Then pick extreme energy, a familiar visual world, and edit changes on every musical change.
