# How to become a FULL TIME artist
URL: https://youtu.be/GRQcms1PQ24 | Length: 36:45 | Views: 411 (uploaded 2026-03-06)
Note: a live workshop/presentation to a room of artists. From about 28:46 to the end the transcript repeats the 21:00-28:30 section almost word for word (re-edited duplicate). The chapters list a live "Q&A and strategy session", but the transcript ends at "let's break for 5 minutes" before any live song strategy happens.

## Core idea (2-3 sentences)
Income as an artist = people buying your product (streams first, then merch/tickets), and that needs a community. He gives a funnel acronym for a fan (Find, Anchor, Name dropping): get found through intentional content (brand first, then "copy and remix" viral formats, experiment then double down), anchor with a consistent aesthetic and repeated exposure, and convert through one-to-one engagement (replies, DMs, close friends stories). "The audience is the director", not you.

## Lessons and tactics
Mindset ("growth mindset", three points) (00:24-02:39):
1. Responsibility for your results: treat where you are as the output of your own work; if a video does not go viral, do not blame the algorithm, go acquire the information to go viral (00:36-01:24).
2. Comfortable being uncomfortable: different outputs need changed inputs; "too cringe" / "doesn't feel right" objections block results (01:26-01:56).
3. "You only have one brain": find someone who loves content the way you love music; delegate and build a team (Drake's team plans his posts) (02:01-02:38).

Money and community (02:39-03:41):
4. Income comes from two things: streaming, then products (merch, tickets, etc.), usually streams first; both require community (03:00-03:21). Brand power example: Apple can sell anything (03:30-03:41).

Fan funnel acronym (03:43-04:08, then throughout): a fan moves from not knowing you to paying you. F = Find (awareness). Then an "anchor" stage (21:18-21:58; the letter is not spelled out in captions, the stage is called "anchor"). Then N = Name dropping: trust so high from seeing you so often that they feel they must be part of the brand and drop your name.

Find (04:08-21:10):
5. Playlists: avoid; streams may come from places that are not your target audience, and playlist listening is passive so it does not move people to other platforms (04:12-04:46). Radio ("it's not 2001") and posters: dismissed (05:23-05:29).
6. Labels look at community, not just streams; some label meetings now come with a social media spreadsheet rather than the music (05:15-05:20).
7. Content is the way to be found, because people feel they found you (05:30-05:48).
8. Do not "just post anything" 3-4 times a day; quantity is not the game in 2026, understanding the audience deeply is; intentional, brand-building, experimentation, reviewing numbers (05:50-06:22).
9. Branding is the foundation (more important this year than last, for conversion and community) (06:26-06:44). Simple brand question: imagine your song playing as the background of a movie scene at the cinema; what scenes come to mind? Write them down. That gives target audience, visuals, colours; optionally add small things like the font; "that is brand" enough for viral content (06:55-08:03). Example: a moody 3am love song -> bed, night, one yellow lamp, moon through window, pink/orange strobes, an 18-25 couple holding each other (07:09-07:47).
10. "Copy and remix" framework: you do not need to understand the algorithm; understand why a video went viral, take the principles, remix them for your brand; claimed to generate 28 viral ideas in about an hour / a month's strategy (08:13-08:54).
    - Step 1: find three music creators who inspire you and are already viral (08:56-09:04).
    - Step 2: from each, choose their three most viral videos = nine inspo videos. Check against their baseline: a 1M-view video may be their baseline/failure point; look for the outlier far above their follower count, which proves it reaches new people (09:07-09:50).
    - Step 3: remix, "the most analytical stage"; ask why every element is in the video (09:51-10:14). Elements to analyse: (a) the hook, first 3-5 seconds (10:14-10:24); (b) the payoff, what keeps people to the end / what you make them want to predict about the ending (e.g. trick-shot videos) (10:24-10:58); (c) artist positioning, e.g. a former humour creator's video fits their history; know how the artist was already known (11:00-11:26); (d) lyrical relevance/congruence: the setting must match the music or communication is unclear; viral posts are about communication (11:28-11:45).
11. Every second has a reason; breakdown of a Citizen video (12:19-15:28): starts with Snapchat-style text (along the lines of "Bro, what did I just witness?") so it does not read as music marketing and casts a wider net than people interested in unknown artists (12:39-13:12); candid, no eye contact with camera, looks recorded by someone else (13:24-13:33); curiosity and confusion, then tension (will she get run over?) (13:41-13:50).
12. "Audience prediction": if viewers start predicting what will happen, they stay to see the ending (13:52-14:31). Multiple possible outcomes, then deliver something different, tied to a lyric ("I caught you falling for me") = lyrical congruence plus mood (14:32-14:59).
13. Smooth transition point so viewers do not realise it is marketing until the comments; they then recognise the artist after seeing them multiple times in the feed (15:07-15:28).
14. Same principles reused on other artists: Snapchat start, candid, transition, shock factor, prediction (15:33-15:51). Laura Jaddyy video opens with a geographical statement ("Hungary"), she runs into a house, confusion/curiosity/shock rise, she flies out the door into the transition (16:00-16:27). "Before and after prediction" used for Eric Bellinger, recorded in 15 minutes at his house (16:44-16:52).
15. The algorithm is quick now; no need for 100 warmed-up posts; a correctly strategised, copy-remixed video can go viral the next day (17:40-18:00).
16. Experiment with "formats" (others call them hooks) (18:08-18:20). Three named: transition method; HQA content = high-quality authentic content, tasteful performance videos that represent the mood/brand visuals; public reaction, based on why flash mobs fascinate people (18:24-18:49). Adriano also tested: slide posts (three pictures as slides with on-screen text that works with the music); "lazy videos" (authentic phone-in-hand); Snapchat method (20:04-20:25).
17. Experiment with ~30 videos in a month, then double down; you earn the right to build a strategy only once you have data; do this for every song; you are not the audience (18:55-19:20).
18. "The audience is the director" of your page and career; listen to the data (19:21-19:37).
19. A format that wins for others can fail for you (Snapchat method got 5x his old views but counted as a failure; slides won) - that is why you experiment (20:23-20:36).

Anchor (21:10-21:58):
20. Make the brand stick with a consistent aesthetic (Adriano's page is all slide posts with a similar look); like Nike keeping one slogan (21:18-21:37).
21. Consistent awareness: someone must see you seven times to stream; once something goes viral, keep hammering that strategy (21:38-21:46).
22. Great music: bad music goes viral for the wrong reasons (21:46-21:58).

Name dropping / getting paid (21:58-23:59):
23. Audience building is one-to-one now; you cannot just post your way to a community (22:11-22:23).
24. Reply to every single comment when you go viral, even thousands (22:24-22:41).
25. DM people who commented to build a personal relationship (22:42-22:47).
26. Close friends story: after a conversation, add them; they feel a personal relationship with "a famous guy", click your story first, tell others, name dropping starts, they buy (22:47-23:16). Sell only in close friends stories, which feel exclusive (24:51-24:56).
27. Summary: consistency plus feeling part of an exclusive community gets people to pay you (23:16-23:24).

Q&A (24:03-28:30, repeated 31:46-36:45):
28. Own platform (Discord, website, Bandcamp): same mechanism, content drives traffic since such platforms have no algorithm; post things there people cannot get anywhere else, not even on the promoting platform (e.g. private performances) (24:10-24:51, 32:24-33:21).
29. "Pillars" by platform/mechanism rather than reach-vs-community videos: conversion differs little video to video; it is driven by engagement and how you engage commenters, plus stories (25:36-26:00). Story posts and private Instagram communities are for selling/deep relationships; IG and TikTok posts for exposure (26:00-26:09).
30. TikTok: best for conversion to streams ("a beast for music"), but community is not built there because the feed ignores who you follow; Instagram keeps and re-engages community. Move TikTok viral viewers to Instagram via bio, stories and comment interaction; Instagram can be more tasteful, building a "universe around the music" (26:09-27:28).
31. Two kinds of content: lyrically focused content and pure viral videos; a viral format with no lyric relevance can still drive streams (Eric Bellinger TV transition, spinning office chair in his garden) (27:41-28:23).

## Examples he uses (numbers claimed)
- Denny: 1.4M Spotify monthly listeners but 5.4K IG; labels would not take him on because he has listeners, not community (04:48-05:15).
- Citizen: multi-million views, 380k likes on the "caught you falling" video; claimed 0 to 1M in 11 months with their team (12:35, 15:26).
- Laura Jaddyy: ~300 average views, ~200 followers, 130k views on first video, ~500 Spotify streams/day after (15:33-16:33).
- David: 235k views (16:36). Eric Bellinger: 30M views on a 22-second video (16:39), TV transition 30M IG views, "millions of streams".
- Robin: ~10k followers, 14 posts did not work, then 4.6M views and 113k streams in two weeks, 10k+ streams in a day, no ads (16:52-17:40).
- Adriano (Italian lo-fi jazz rap): ~200 views/video before; Snapchat 5k, another 5.6k; slides 29k and 73k; 1M TikTok views in 20 days; 15k followers, 92k monthly listeners, 1M+ streams, merch, shows in Italy, now full time (19:39-23:55).

## Process / checklists
Copy and remix: 3 viral creators -> 3 most viral videos each (outliers vs their baseline) -> 9 inspo videos -> analyse hook, payoff, artist positioning, lyrical relevance -> remix for your brand.
Song cycle: brand (movie-scene question) -> ~30 experimental videos across formats in a month -> read data -> double down on winners -> anchor (consistent aesthetic, repeated exposure) -> convert (reply to all comments, DM commenters, close friends stories, sell there).
