# how to build a cult-like fanbase for your music
URL: https://youtu.be/3Mj9YnSz8EI | Length: 15:18 | Views: 298 (uploaded 2026-06-02)

## Core idea (2-3 sentences)
A live strategy session in London with "Pand/Pav" (a Greek rapper who is also a RAPVILLE strategist): first define the artist's brand strength and goals, then pick content pillars from the brand and the song, then brainstorm concrete shootable ideas that tap into those pillars. They end with seven video ideas for one release, all built around visualising the lyrics so the viewer is constantly re-hooked.

## Lessons and tactics
1. Before making any strategy, define the brand's strength and "unique angle" (00:40-00:47). Artist's answer: technical, lyrical rap that is also playful, with pop/internet-culture references, fusing 2000s style with new-gen music, connecting two generations (00:50-01:13).
2. Watch the artist's existing best videos to see the brand visually rather than only talking about it (01:14-01:22).
3. A raw studio performance with "no hooks" can still get decent engagement; theirs mixed sampling viral audio (the "now or never" trend) with raw performance (01:35-01:51).
4. A strong intro hook: use conflict/a challenge, then answer it. His best video opened with a screen recording of an IG live where the biggest Greek rapper says he can flow but is not a rapper, then cuts to him rapping (02:41-02:59).
5. A video that is not visually great can still perform if the song and lyrics connect (03:16-03:34).
6. Visualising the lyrics in the video is a core idea even when execution could be better (03:45-03:53).
7. Set a concrete goal before the strategy: here 100k monthly listeners by end of year, Greece as the home market first then branch out, grow shows from ~110 tickets to 500 people by end of next year (04:16-05:11).
8. Build an email list: you cannot sell tickets if you cannot reach the buyers; Instagram is owned by Instagram, the email list is your own platform (05:12-05:31).
9. Listen to the song to choose content pillars (06:07). Pillars chosen for this release: (a) Nostalgia (old school / 2000s hip-hop), (b) Culture: Greek culture plus Gen Z / internet culture, (c) Lyric visualisation (06:11-06:57).
10. Use a "signature object" that recurs across videos, like Citizen's blue Mustang; proposed a lowrider here (06:21-06:31).
11. A recurring lyric-visualisation gag (pot and pan appearing on "let me cook") can become a "signature format" (06:54-07:02).
12. Idea 1, reaction video: people who look like the target audience in the background reacting to lyrics that tap into their culture; made unique by filming in a traditional Greek coffee shop with older people bopping to rap. Unexpected scenes make the BTS perform; zoom in on reactions in the polished version; with trial reels this is about five pieces of content (07:04-08:02).
13. Idea 2: artist as the main focal point in the foreground, "lifeless" lip-syncing, while multiple people run in and out of frame in the background visualising each lyric (e.g. the zip-up bag) so there is constantly something new to look at (08:02-08:58).
14. Idea 3: play the nostalgia but do not market the song right away. During the instrumental build-up (DJ scratches) show nostalgic things "we need to bring back in 2026" (e.g. Nintendo DS, Y2K clothes), adapted from an American trend to the local (Greek) culture; viewer does not expect it to be the artist and keeps listening because it looks like UGC (09:01-10:12). Suggested upgrade: cinematic camera in a pool location (10:12-10:22).
15. Idea 4: split screen, BTS vs the shot. Bottom = polished cinematic shot (artist hops out of the car, girls follow); top = raw BTS (girls scrambling out, cameraman and lighting guy chasing). Add a sequence of actions synced to lyrics (door swings open, bag thrown with zip sound) (10:26-11:23).
16. The split-screen idea is two ideas: the polished shot stands alone, and the BTS separately; he says BTS often performs better than the actual shot (11:23-11:39).
17. Idea 5, "Snapchat method": filmed from a balcony, zooming in on the artist with friends on the lowrider, song blasting loud and echoey (like the Citizen videos), text on screen along the lines of "WTF, I feel like I'm back in 2002", then cut to the performance (11:39-12:29). A format is not cringe in itself; it is cringe only if it is inauthentic to the brand (11:44-11:51).
18. In such a hook the song should flow smoothly and the cuts should time with the performance, not jump to a different song (12:34-12:44).
19. Idea 6/7, freeze-frame trend: the subject (artist) freezes, e.g. money thrown in the air frozen, while a clone of the artist creeps into frame performing the song (12:44-13:33). Total: "seven ideas" (13:37).
20. Summary logic: tap the homeland culture, tap internet culture to go viral, always visualise the lyrics because it (1) matches the song and (2) keeps re-hooking the viewer; plus targeting hip-hop heads as part of the brand (13:45-14:03).

## Examples he uses

- Pav's first show: claimed 110+ ticket sales (05:00).

- Citizen (the Artist): blue Mustang as signature object; echoey hook sound in his videos (06:25, 12:08).
- Reference videos shown on screen for idea 2, idea 3 (American nostalgia trend) and the freeze-frame trend; not named.

## Process / checklists
Strategy order: 1) brand strength / unique angle, 2) watch best existing videos, 3) goals (listeners, market, shows, email list), 4) listen to the song, 5) pick 3 content pillars, 6) brainstorm ideas against the pillars, adding reference videos, 7) count ideas and check each one taps a pillar.
