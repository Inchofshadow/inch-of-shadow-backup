# The @fakeplasticbrands sites, checked one by one (2026-10-13)

Each site opened on 2026-10-13: what it really is, cost, rights, quality, overlap with the kept kit, and a verdict. Read-only: no sign-ups, no logins, no forms, no downloads.

Kept kit (toolkit.md): FILMGRAB, Cosmos, Are.na, Public Domain Review, Fonts In Use. A new site only counts if it clearly beats one of these or does a job none of them does.

Method: WebFetch, WebSearch, a headless browser pane, and the Internet Archive's public search API for collection counts. Where a page could not be opened, it says so. Numbers are only given where the site (or a named source) states them.

Sponsored posts: flim.ai and Ditherboy were paid ads in his feed. "Mountain" was also sponsored, but it was not in the list I was given, so it was not checked.

## Inspiration / search

### 1. SOOT (soot.com)
Sources: https://soot.com (raw HTML), It's Nice That sponsored article, Oct 2024 (itsnicethat.com/news/soot-digital-creative-industry-sponsored-content-091024)

- What it is: a visual file organiser that maps your own images by colour and shape into browsable "clouds" (the product page is titled "SOOT SPIRAL"). You feed it your own Drive folders, Figma files, Are.na boards or URLs. It is not a search engine over the open web.
- Status today: the site shows a maintenance page ("temporarily down for upgrades"). Could not see the product, prices or terms.
- Cost: unknown. In Oct 2024 it was closed beta with a waitlist.
- Rights: not applicable; it organises material you already have.
- Quality signal: the only coverage found is a sponsored It's Nice That piece. Down at the time of checking.
- Overlap: Cosmos and Are.na already hold his boards; SOOT would only re-display them.
- Verdict: SKIP. Down, and it sorts images he already has rather than finding new ones.

### 2. Flim (flim.ai) [SPONSORED AD]
Sources: https://flim.ai, https://flim.ai/pricing, third-party reviews (cined.com 2022, robinpiree.com); terms page returned 404

- What it is: an AI search engine for film and video stills, with boards and newer AI generation/editing tools.
- What you get (stated on its page): 1.5M stills, 5.8K movies, 5.5K music videos, 15K ads, 2K TV series, 150K animations. Categories like Noir and Giallo. Third-party reviews describe a colour picker; the pricing page lists advanced filters and reverse image search, but I did not see colour search named on Flim's own pages.
- Cost: Free plan (unlimited search, 1 download per day, only part of the library, public boards). Basic about 12.90 EUR/month, Pro about 29.90 EUR/month, Teams on request.
- Rights: not stated on the pages I could open (terms link 404). These are stills from films and ads owned by others: reference only.
- Quality signal: active (2026 footer), large library; one review says direct film/TV clips are thinner than ads and music videos. Operator not named on the page.
- Overlap: FILMGRAB (free, curated whole films) and ShotDeck (the paid slot already reserved for "find this exact light").
- Verdict: SKIP for now. It does the ShotDeck job; the free tier is capped and the main differences are AI extras and ad/music-video depth. Reconsider only if a ShotDeck month is ever needed and Flim's colour search proves better in a test.

### 3. Genery (genery.io)
Sources: https://genery.io (fetch + browser), /pricing and /plans (404), /terms_of_service (empty page)

- What it is: a moodboard and pre-production workspace with a cinematic reference library (frames from movies, TV, music videos), searchable by shot type and lens, with motion previews on hover, plus storyboard tools. AI features "coming".
- Cost: FAQ mentions a free plan; no prices found anywhere I could open.
- Rights: not stated (terms page had no text). Film frames: reference only.
- Quality signal: operator not named, no dates, no library size. The "replaces ShotDeck" claim is Genery's own FAQ marketing.
- Overlap: FILMGRAB + Cosmos (references and boards). The one new thing is moving previews of camera moves, relevant to reels.
- Verdict: SKIP. Unknown owner, no prices, no terms, no size. Not "clearly better" than FILMGRAB. Possible one-off look later for camera-move references for a music video.

### 4. Death to Stock (deathtostock.com, redirects to deathtothestockphoto.com)
Sources: https://www.deathtothestockphoto.com, https://explore.deathtostock.com

- What it is: a paid stock photo and video membership, shot by commissioned photographers around monthly trend research ("cultural zeitgeist"). States 15,000+ visuals, about 500 new per month, "No AI".
- Zeitgeist / "Zeits" section: not found. The only match for "zeit" on the site is the trend-research marketing line. No named Zeits section on the pages I could open.
- Cost: Brand 20 USD/month, Agency 69 USD/month, 14-day trial; no permanent free tier (a newsletter gives 10+ free visuals).
- Rights: USABLE in published work, but rented: you may use visuals while subscribed; after cancelling, existing social posts may stay up if credited. Extended licences for TV/outdoor. No resale or merch.
- Quality signal: active (2026), funds 100+ artists via its "Internet Artist Fund"; shoots are lifestyle themes (Run Club, Soft Luxe, Fatherhood).
- Overlap: none in the kit (the kit is reference-only plus Public Domain Review). But his covers, reels and flyers are built from his own photographer's shoots and public-domain art; brand-style lifestyle stock does not fit his world.
- Verdict: SKIP. Usable footage, but lifestyle brand stock on a rental licence; nothing his own shoots plus Prelinger (below) do not cover better.

### 5. MUBI (mubi.com)
Source: https://www.mubi.com/en/de

- What it is: a hand-curated film streaming service, plus Notebook (print magazine and online), a film database, MUBI GO cinema tickets.
- Cost: 7-day free trial; price for Germany not shown on the page I opened (memberships page not read).
- Rights: watching only.
- Quality signal: well established, active.
- Overlap: FILMGRAB shows stills of films; MUBI lets him watch the whole film in motion, which is what a reel needs (cut, movement, pace).
- Verdict: SPECIAL JOB: watch the reference film in motion before a reel or music video. A personal viewing subscription, not a reference tool.

### 6. Radio Garden (radio.garden)
Sources: https://radio.garden (page text only: "explore live radio by rotating the globe"), press coverage (Digital Trends, KOB/WRDW 2024)

- What it is: a free globe of live radio stations; run by Amsterdam studios Puckey and Moniker, started as a Netherlands Institute for Sound and Vision project. Press cites about 30,000 stations. Described as non-profit.
- Cost: free.
- Rights: listening only.
- Overlap: no visual job at all.
- Verdict: SKIP for the brand kit. Fine for listening and scene discovery, not a reference tool.

### 7. Atlas Obscura (atlasobscura.com)
Source: https://www.atlasobscura.com/things-to-do/berlin-germany (browser; WebFetch got 403)

- What it is: a user-contributed atlas of unusual places. Berlin page lists 173 places, "updated August 7, 2026" (Berliner Unterwelten, Liquidrom, Monsterkabinett, Design Panoptikum, Cafe Strauss in a cemetery, and more). Free to browse; account optional.
- Rights: text and photos are for reading; not usable material.
- Overlap: none. The kit has no location-scouting tool; his current spots are listed in memory (cemetery, Tempelhofer Feld, Koernerpark, Neukoelln streets).
- Verdict: SPECIAL JOB: location scouting for a new shoot or video in Berlin (or on tour). Open it when a shoot needs a new place.

### 8. NOWNESS (nowness.com)
Sources: https://www.nowness.com (browser), Wikipedia/CB Insights for ownership

- What it is: a video channel of short art, fashion, music and culture films. New films every few days (Oct 9, Oct 7, Oct 5, 2026...), series like Sound & Vision, NOWNESS Shorts, Portrait of a Place, Video Art Visions (e.g. Romain Gavras & Surkin). Owned by Modern Dazed (Modern Media / Meta Media and Dazed Media) per Wikipedia and CB Insights.
- Cost: free.
- Rights: watching only.
- Overlap: Directors' Library (already "saved for special jobs") covers current music-video craft; NOWNESS is more art/fashion film.
- Verdict: SPECIAL JOB: reel and music video ideas (short-form movement, editing, fashion-film looks). Free, current, high craft. Watch, do not file.

### 9. GeoCities archives / Cameron's World (cameronsworld.net)
Sources: https://www.cameronsworld.net, https://geocities.restorativland.org, https://gifcities.org

- What they are: Cameron's World is a single scrolling web collage of GeoCities graphics (1994 to 2009) by Cameron Askin (a Berlin-based designer, per Vice). Restorativland's Geocities Gallery sorts restored sites by neighbourhood (early version, Jan 2019). GifCities is the Internet Archive's search over GeoCities animated GIFs.
- Cost: free.
- Rights: none stated on any of the three. The material is anonymous amateur web art: treat as reference only.
- Overlap: none in the kit, but the webcore look is not his world (post-punk, new wave, dark electronic, carved/romantic type).
- Verdict: SKIP. A strong aesthetic, but not his; no usable rights.

## Tools

### 10. pixelCrash (pixelcrash.xyz)
Source: https://www.pixelcrash.xyz (browser; WebFetch saw only the title)

- What it is: a free browser tool, v1.2 by @van_der_ex. Upload or record a video, then stack layers that map brightness or edges to cells, solid blocks or symbols (ASCII-like typography), live.
- Export: WebM only. The maker asks for credit or #pixelcrash on published work.
- Cost: free, no account seen.
- Rights: your own footage in, your output out; credit requested.
- Overlap: none in the kit. HyperFrames' registry may already have ASCII effects (not checked here).
- Verdict: SPECIAL JOB: an ASCII/text-texture treatment on his own footage for a dark-electronic lane reel or visualizer. Runs locally in the browser, so his footage is not uploaded anywhere (not verified).

### 11. Space Type Generator (spacetypegenerator.com)
Source: https://spacetypegenerator.com (readme page 404)

- What it is: a free set of 23 kinetic-type generators (Cylinder, Field, Stripes, Coil, Flag, Ribbon, Layers, String and more) by Kiel Mutschelknaus (kielm.com). Donation-funded ("Buy me a coffee").
- Export: not stated on the page I read.
- Rights / licence: not stated.
- Overlap: HyperFrames already does kinetic type for him.
- Verdict: SPECIAL JOB (minor): quick kinetic-type sketches for a title or lyric moment before building it properly in HyperFrames. Check export before relying on it.

### 12. Schultzschultz tools (schultzschultz.com/tools)
Source: https://schultzschultz.com/tools

- What it is: 7 free browser tools from a Frankfurt design studio: ScriptScript (beta, make a script style), Tangents (lettering/patterns), Smudge, Stretch (pixel-stretch an image), TouchType, GridPaint, Calligraphy (broad nib).
- Cost: free, but the page asks you to sign up or log in to try them (not done).
- Rights: own terms apply ("by using the tool you agree to our terms"); terms not read.
- Overlap: none directly; Calligraphy and Tangents touch the logo/wordmark job.
- Verdict: SPECIAL JOB (minor): lettering experiments for the logo/wordmark (Look-02). Needs an account, so his call.

### 13. Dither Boy by Studio AAA (studioaaa.com) [SPONSORED AD]
Sources: https://studioaaa.com, reviews at digitalproduction.com (Jul 2025, Mar 2026) and 80.lv

- What it is: a desktop dithering app by Studio AAA (Jack McArdle and Danny Void). Version 6.0 (Mar 2026) adds animation timelines, better video, CMYK halftone; 60+ effects; exports PNG, SVG, JPEG, MP4, plus vectors for print and embroidery. "No AI" approach.
- Cost: one-time purchase, 49 GBP on the shop today (was 60), all future updates included. No free trial seen.
- Rights: you own your output; it processes his own images.
- Quality signal: active, regular big updates, covered by trade press.
- Overlap: none in the kit. HyperFrames and local tools can do grain, but not this depth of halftone/dither for print.
- Verdict: SPECIAL JOB: print texture and halftone for flyers, posters, merch and the electronic lane's covers. Buy only when a release or flyer actually calls for this look. Note: sold only via Studio AAA's own site; "free download" pages are spam.

## Free font sites

### 14. UNCUT (uncut.wtf)
Sources: https://uncut.wtf, https://uncut.wtf/legal/

- What it is: a one-person curated catalogue of 163 "somewhat contemporary" free typefaces (Sans 66, Serif 27, Mono 23, Display 47). Active 2021 to 2026.
- Licence: says "free for commercial use", but names no licence; fonts stay the property of their creators and the user must check each font's licence with its creator.
- Overlap: Fonts In Use (seeing fonts in real use); font-sources.md (buying).
- Verdict: KEEP-candidate as the free-font first stop (alongside Fontshare), with a per-font licence check. Contemporary, small, quality-picked.

### 15. Free Faces (freefaces.gallery)
Sources: https://freefaces.gallery (about page 404), Fonts In Use entry on the site, Brandfetch profile

- What it is: a curated gallery of free typefaces by Simon Foster, online since at least 2016, redesigned 2023. Categories Cursive, Display, Mono, Sans, Serif, Slab; 8 pages of latest entries. Links out to each font's source.
- Licence: mixed. Fonts come under various licences (SIL OFL, free for commercial use, but also personal-use-only ones). Not all are free for commercial use: check each.
- Overlap: UNCUT does the same job with a clearer commercial-use stance.
- Verdict: SPECIAL JOB: second free-font stop, especially Cursive and Display for a title. Always check the licence on the font's own page.

### 16. Fontshare (fontshare.com)
Sources: https://www.fontshare.com (browser), https://www.fontshare.com/faq

- What it is: a free font service run by Indian Type Foundry (ITF). Homepage shows 100 families (Satoshi and others), copyright 2021 to 2026.
- Licence (from its own FAQ): all fonts free for personal and commercial use in print, web, video/broadcast, logos (you may register a logo, not claim the font). Two licence types: "Closed Source" under ITF's Free Font License (no redistribution, no editing the font file; outlines may be modified for a logo) and "Open Source" under SIL OFL. Credit optional.
- Quality signal: a professional foundry; the clearest licence of the three.
- Overlap: font-sources.md (paid fonts). Mostly modern sans; less on the carved/romantic serif side his brand uses.
- Verdict: KEEP-candidate as the free-font first stop for clean modern faces (e.g. the electronic lane's Jost-like light sans). Safest commercial licence of the three.
