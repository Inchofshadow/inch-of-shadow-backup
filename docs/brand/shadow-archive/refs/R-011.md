# R-011 · Artist · A Perfect Circle: logo, wordmark, merch (not the album art)

- **Source:** A Perfect Circle (Billy Howerdel and Maynard James Keenan; Maynard's third band next to Tool and Puscifer). Looked at 2026-10-13: the emblem, the wordmark, official merch and 2018 tour posters, through image search; the designer's own explanation from an interview.
- **Why Amir sent it (his words, 2026-10-13, in chat, not a 📌 answer):** "I feel it's connected to our yin and yang stuff." Then: "I like what you said about the colour, the one accent, the wordmark across the circle ... I'm also open for something like Billy and Maynard did, like the two perfect circles. Maybe we can do something similar to me, and also in our design language, like effects, the formats and stuff, not just the logo."
- **Scope Amir set:** their logo, visual language and merch, not the album covers.

## What is in it

1. **The emblem: two crescents that hide two circles.** It reads as a large crescent and a smaller one facing each other, a broken circle. Billy Howerdel, who drew it, says it hides two perfect circles, one for him and one for Maynard; meant to be hidden in plain sight, like the arrow in the FedEx logo. Fans read it as life and death, balance, infinity; the band never fixed one meaning.
2. **The wordmark across the circle.** "a perfect circle" in thin, lowercase, wide-spaced letters runs straight through the middle of the emblem, often on a fine horizontal line. One horizontal axis cuts the circle.
3. **One accent colour.** The emblem is red (later gold or amber) on black; the wordmark is white or grey. One colour carries the mark.
4. **Merch: small seal, lots of black.** On shirts the emblem sits small and centred on the chest.
5. **Posters: a small seal above free art.** On the 2018 tour posters the emblem is a small seal at the top; the art below changes every time. The same strict-frame, free-picture idea as the Tool posters (R-010).
6. **Recurring motifs:** moon and crescent phases, one eye at the centre of radiating op-art lines, mirrored ornament.

## How it connects to Amir (Claude's note, not a decision)

- **Two in one is already the brand.** Inch of Shadow is one name with two lanes (the songs and the electronic drops), a person and his shadow, light and dark in one frame. The APC idea gives that a form: two shapes that together make one circle, with the meaning hidden rather than spelled out.
- **Logo (Look-02):** two forms that close into one circle: an inch and its shadow, a sun and a moon, two crescents of different weight. It avoids the "too obvious" moon-O from the first round, because two abstract crescents only read as a moon once you look.
- **Covers:** one axis through the picture (the wordmark or a horizon line through the centre), one accent colour from the release's mood, the two halves answering each other (his balanced opposition pick, R-010).
- **Reels and formats:** a mirrored two-half frame; cut between Amir and his shadow; the closing sting as two shapes sliding together into one circle.
- **Merch and posters:** a small seal centred on black; the art changes per release while the seal stays.

Nothing of this is decided; Amir guides each use.
