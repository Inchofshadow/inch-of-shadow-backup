## What it teaches: the 60-30-10 colour rule

- 60% of the frame is one dominant colour, 30% a secondary colour, and 10% an accent that pulls the eye. It is an old guideline from painting and interior design.
- The caption claims classic Disney backgrounds follow it, mostly to keep big landscapes calm and balanced.
- Examples in the reel, with the 60 / 30 / 10 shown on each chart:
  - Robin Hood: stone grey, olive shadow, lilac on Maid Marian.
  - Alice (the Cheshire Cat): slate blue, black, hot pink.
  - The Jungle Book (Bagheera and Kaa): royal blue, navy, ochre.
  - 101 Dalmatians: blood red, white, olive.
  - Lady and the Tramp: grey-white bedding, purple quilt, brown dog.
  - Bambi: forest green, dark maroon, pale stag.
  - Peter Pan (Big Ben): steel blue, near-black, yellow clock.
  - Fantasia (the Sorcerer): deep navy, grey-blue, magenta.
  - Dumbo: pale sky, grey elephant, yellow hat.
- The accent (the 10) is almost always the character or the one object the story needs you to look at. The 60 is the world around it.

## The format (measured)

- **Structure:** a fixed frame. A two-line title stays on top for the whole reel, the film clip plays in a rounded window, and a pie chart under it shows that clip's three colours as 60 / 30 / 10.
- **Rhythm:** 10 clips in 25.7 s, about one every 2.5 s, cut like a slideshow. The chart changes colour with every clip.
- **Sound:** a 1960s crooner song (Dean Martin), no voice-over. The teaching is in the caption, the reel only shows.
- **Hook:** the title names a famous brand plus a "rule", and the first clip is recognisable at once. There's no call to action in the reel; the caption ends on a question ("What's the best classic Disney movie?") to get comments.
