## Poster read, 2026-10-13 (Amir's scope)

Amir's brief: Tool gig posters from the Lateralus (2001-02), 10,000 Days (2006-07) and Fear Inoculum (2019-24) eras, not the album packaging; Puscifer art that is not about their characters and logo. These are high-level references; nothing gets copied. Claude analyses what is in the art; Amir then says in his own words what he wants from it.

About 115 real gig posters looked at (limited screen prints and official show posters; print-on-demand fan "album posters" left out), found through image search: 20 from 2001-02, 24 from 2006-07, 34 from 2019-24, 39 Puscifer. Images were only looked at, then deleted.

### Tool, Lateralus era (2001-02)

- **Many hands, one rule: each city gets its own artist.** There is no house style; the posters range from Emek's detailed line work to plain two-colour wood-type layouts.
- **The body as a machine or a map:**
  - a profile head with gears and a mechanical skull (Berlin 2001);
  - a spine drawn into the back of a head;
  - an anatomical man with visible organs and veins (from the Lateralus art);
  - a figure seen through the skin.
- **Eyes everywhere:** walls of eyes rising behind a figure in a tunnel (the "in concert" posters), a single third eye.
- **The grotesque and the half-human:** two-headed figures, a pale creature with its organs showing, a face pressed into another face.
- **Colour:** loud and limited, two to four flat inks. Red with cyan, purple with yellow-green, orange on black. Halftone dots and visible print grain.
- **Type:** the name huge, often hand-drawn, stretched or condensed to fill the top third. Dates and venues in heavy condensed capitals at the foot.

### Tool, 10,000 Days era (2006-07)

- **More restraint, more contrast:**
  - a grey poster with a gold sun disc and a black winged angel statue;
  - Aleister Crowley in black and white, framed by hands, on pale paper;
  - a red Sacred Heart Jesus in front of a crowd of riot police (Las Vegas "Jesus");
  - Freud with a cigar.
- **Found portraits and icons recontextualised:** a historical photo or a religious painting re-printed in one or two colours, with one thing changed or added. The meaning comes from the collision (holy figure vs riot police).
- **Statues and stone:** winged figures, carved angels, stone faces.
- **The X-ray skull** again (Emek, Berlin 2006): a skull in profile with brain and gears, pale green on black.
- **Formats:** many are long and narrow (about 1:2.5), a tall vertical band with the name at top or bottom. Official German promo posters (München, Frankfurt) are plain: the logo, two eyes and the date.

### Tool, Fear Inoculum era (2019-24)

- **Rainbow foil is the signature of the era.** Almost every show poster has a holographic foil layer, so the print flashes rainbow spots when it moves.
- **One central figure, posed like a painting:**
  - a horned creature with a glowing heart;
  - a robed woman with many hands making gestures;
  - a skull inside a geometric sphere;
  - a smoking skeletal saint in armour;
  - a classical woman in a gold arch;
  - a statue-like man holding a long staff.
  
  Classical oil-painting light, dark backgrounds, a lot of detail in one figure.
- **The surreal and the uncanny:**
  - figures melting or dissolving into ribbons;
  - a cathedral rising from the sea with hands coming out of the water;
  - a skeleton in a green landscape;
  - a cobra in a flower world.
- **A fixed frame, a free picture:** the TOOL name in the same tall serif across the top on every poster, and the date, venue and city in one line at the bottom. Everything in between belongs to the guest artist. A strict frame plus a free picture is what makes 100 different posters read as one series.
- **Alex Grey's own posters:** his see-through anatomical figures over a net of light, with the name hidden in the art.
- **The 2019 Berlin show poster** breaks the pattern completely: flat overlapping circles in warm gradients and the name in clean white sans. Modern Swiss poster design.

### Puscifer (the art that is not the characters or the logo)

Puscifer's own world shows up on most posters: luchador masks, grey aliens, UFO beams, men in black suits and sunglasses, and the hand-drawn logo. Amir likes it, but these notes are about the rest.

- **A lone figure in a flat landscape** (Las Vegas 2023): a man in a black suit walking out of a desert of cacti toward the viewer, his head on fire. Lilac sky, flat colours like a film poster, a lot of empty space, the name small at the bottom.
- **Melted and surreal landscapes:**
  - a pink landscape melting like wax, with the letters lying in it;
  - a city of mushroom towers under a crescent moon;
  - a castle-pyramid under a purple beam.
- **Classical motifs drawn new:**
  - a Medusa in teal with a lace mask and a geometric cube;
  - three monkeys on a television (see no, hear no, speak no evil) in flames;
  - a woman in a mask holding a burning heart.
- **Psychedelic poster tradition:** dense ornament filling every millimetre, 60s-style melting letters, mirrored symmetric layouts.
- **Retro illustration:** 50s comic and pulp-magazine drawing (an old car full of passengers), vintage pin-up and paperback covers, Mexican folk-art colour.
- **Colour:** much warmer and more playful than Tool. Fluorescent pink, acid green, orange, lilac and teal, often neon on black, with blacklight and foil editions.

### What runs through all of it (Claude's note, not a decision)

1. **Each poster is its own artwork made for one night**, by a different artist each time, inside a strict frame: the same name, the same date line.
2. **One figure, one idea, a lot of craft.** A single central figure, portrait or object carries it.
3. **The body opened up:** skulls, spines, organs, X-ray, see-through skin, the inside shown.
4. **Classical and religious imagery recontextualised:** saints, statues, Medusa, Vanitas skulls, all given one twist.
5. **Print as material:** limited screen prints, two to four inks, halftone, foil, blacklight ink, metallic gold. The object itself is the collectible.
6. **Tool goes dark and ritual; Puscifer goes warm and absurd.** The same craft with two different temperatures.
