## His video format, measured (2026-10-13)

Seven reels read frame by frame (Swang, STXLKXR, No easy way out, Pages, Switch, symmetrical context, a 29s piece; one more was region-locked). All 8 to 29 seconds, 24 fps, vertical.

- **What happens:** one subject, one locked composition (a figure walking toward the camera, a portrait, a character), and dozens of variations of that same image: the same pose with a different face, hair, mask, outfit or distortion. The edit hard-cuts between them every 1 to 4 frames and keeps returning to earlier variations, so the picture seems to breathe, twitch or be possessed rather than move. This is the "goes back over and over" Amir saw.
- **Names for it** (to use in briefs and prompts): variation flicker, stutter cut, frame-by-frame cycling, "boiling" (the animation term for a line that never stands still), image strobe. Not a morph: there is no blend or interpolation, every change is a hard cut.
- **Measured rhythm:** the image changes 3 to 10 times a second (in Pages almost every frame). The music is fast, about 144 to 160 BPM (one reel reads 72, which is half-time of 144). That is 9 to 10 frames per beat, so two to four image changes per beat.
- **On top of the cuts:** exposure flicker (the brightness jumps from frame to frame, a big swing), ghosting and double exposure (a second face half-visible beside the first), motion blur and smeared hair, heavy vignette, grain. Mostly black and white; in STXLKXR one flat magenta frame flashes in as an accent.
- **Length and loop:** 8 to 12 seconds is the usual piece, built to loop.

## Second read: his 7 most-viewed recent reels (2026-10-13)

The top 7 by views among his last 50 reels (the first seven already read are left out, and one region-locked reel is replaced by the next): Outlaw 157K, Tonight is right 118K, Bounce 105K, Dare to Uninstall 75K, Too close to the edge 74K, Strobotomy 54K, The rapture 53K. All 10 to 13 seconds, 24 or 30 fps. The variation flicker is in all of them; these are the changes on top of it that the first read did not catch.

- **Two halves with a switch in the middle.** In 6 of the 7, something changes at about 5 seconds and holds to the end. It is the strongest structural pattern:
  - **The cut rate doubles or triples.** Outlaw goes from 7 to 8 (average frame change) to 20 to 49. Tonight is right goes from about 25 to 80 to 114. Too close to the edge goes from about 26 to 72 to 180.
  - **A new effect enters.** Negative frames join in Tonight is right and Bounce.
  - **The grade changes.** Dare to Uninstall goes from grey to a cold blue tint. The rapture goes from a green colour image to black and white plus negatives.
  - **The angle changes.** Tonight is right goes from the skull in profile looking up to a frontal view.
  - In Outlaw and Too close to the edge, the music has an audible break just before 5 seconds and the visual switch lands on the drop after it. In the others the music runs flat and the switch falls on the phrase change.
  - Name for briefs: **a two-part loop, build then drop.**
- **Negative flashes (invert frames).** Single frames, or pairs, of the same image in negative (white background, black figure) are cut in among the normal frames. They appear in 6 of the 7. They read as a lightning or X-ray hit. In Too close to the edge the normal, negative and near-black versions of one scream alternate almost every frame.
- **Blackout frames (strobe).** Frames that drop to near-black, or to an empty black screen, between the image frames, so the figure blinks on and off like under a strobe. They are strongest in Outlaw (a figure with a halo appearing and vanishing against pure black) and Strobotomy (a dark club crowd, with only every few frames lit). His caption "Strobotomy" names it.
- **Brightness pulse, two levels.** In Tonight is right the first half is the skull alternating between a bright and a dim version every frame or two, like a heartbeat, before the negatives come in.
- **One different image as an accent.** In Outlaw, an X-ray arm with a skeleton hand flashes in a few times among the halo figure. One foreign element among the variations.
- **The composition is locked, but the content is not always one subject.** In Bounce (a woman walking toward the camera through a crowd) every frame is a different outfit and crowd in the same framing. In Strobotomy every frame is a different dancer in the same club light. So the rule is **same framing and light, anything else can change**.
- **Colour is rare and used as a section.** Five are fully black and white. Colour appears only as one half of the loop (The rapture's green, Dare to Uninstall's blue), never mixed frame by frame.
- **Music:** 144 to 161 BPM in five (one reads 89 and one 112, likely half-time or a slower track). The captions are one-line titles, mostly phrases or song-like lines ("Outlaw", "Tonight is right", "Dare to Uninstall"). No text on screen.

## How we could make it ourselves (Claude's note, not a decision)

- **Without AI on his face:** shoot a burst (10 to 20 frames a second) of Amir moving (a head turn, hair, hands, a walk toward the lens), with a slow shutter for the smear and one hard light; cut the burst out of order with HyperFrames: hard cuts every 1 to 3 frames on a 24 fps grid, cycling back and forth, a small brightness jitter per frame, the density rising into the drop. The sunglasses would carry the "mask" his figures wear.
- **With AI, only on what is not his face:** variations of a figure, a statue, an object or a background plate, cut the same way.
- **Music:** it wants a fast passage; at 110 BPM (the songs' usual tempo) the same feel comes from 3 to 4 changes per beat or from a double-time section.
