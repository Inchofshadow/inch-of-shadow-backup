# R-004 · Artist · Lucas Garcete (@sepulcrodelaluz)

- **Source:** https://www.instagram.com/sepulcrodelaluz/ (192 posts, 18.5K followers; Amir already follows him). Bio: "In black and white, and the night in between." Black & White Series of the Year '24, born 2000, www.lucasgarcete.com, story highlights "inspiration", "process", "prints".
- **Why Amir sent it (his words, 2026-10-11):** "i really liked his style (except the satanic figures)", "he is direct influence", and maybe one of the references for the Misty Night cover art.
- **Not for us:** the satanic figures (horns, demons, devil wings). Amir excluded them.
- **What the style is made of (seen across his grid, about 70 posts viewed):**
  - **Black and white only.** No colour at all.
  - **Pure black silhouettes** against grey-to-white skies. The figure has no inner detail; the sky carries all the tone.
  - **One light source in the frame:** a full moon, sun, crescent or eclipse, placed right behind or beside the figure's head, often as a halo.
  - **Tiny glowing white eyes** on some silhouettes: the only detail inside the black.
  - **Low horizon, figure on a ridge, lots of empty sky.** Low camera angle, portrait format, the figure usually fills the lower half.
  - **Heavy grain, film dust and soft gradients** in the sky: it reads like an old print, not a clean digital image.
  - **Cast and props:** plague doctor, vampire in a cape, couples dancing or kissing, swans, cats, flocks of birds and bats, dead trees, wide-brim hats, capes.
  - **Mood:** gothic but romantic and fairy-tale, quiet, a whole story told by one gesture in one silhouette.

## Full read, 2026-10-13

Sources: all 191 posts on his grid with their captions, his website (About, Himnos a la noche), LensCulture, and the award pages. The words below are a summary, not his text.

### How he makes them (answered by his own site)

- **Photomontage built only from his own photographs.** Every element is shot with his own camera; the editing only assembles and transforms real material. It is not drawing and not AI. The award categories he wins say the same: photomanipulation, collage, conceptual.
- **The silhouettes come from backlight (contralight).** He photographs friends, family, strangers and statues against the light so the body goes solid black. He also uses cut-outs of 19th-century silhouette portraits. He assembles the pieces until he finds the exact pose for the story.
- **Settings are real too:** ruins, night landscapes, bare trees, water, all shot by him and resized in post so the figures fit into them.
- **The texture: a broken piece of a dirty window.** His series statement says the images are built around a fragment of old, dirty glass, which gives them a "fossil" look. That is the dust, the soft blotches and the grain.
- **Slow and selective.** He spends months on each piece. The poetic idea usually comes after the photograph, not before.
- **He started as a street photographer.** His oldest posts (Madrid, 2016–2017) are plain black-and-white street photos. The silhouette night world started around 2022–2023.

### What he draws on

- 19th-century silhouette portraits (Auguste Edouart) and Victorian profiles.
- Lotte Reiniger's silhouette animation: the silhouette telling a story by itself.
- German Expressionist cinema, and the game Limbo.
- René Magritte's surrealism (a birdcage for a head).
- Greek myth, the Romantic poets (the series title is a homage to Novalis), and the Czech symbolist František Kobliha.

### His central idea

Terror and tenderness in the same picture. A couple can read as lovers or as two ghosts; a bird can mean freedom or an omen; the moon can be beauty or a watching eye. He avoids shock and lets the picture suggest, never explain. Every caption is a short bilingual poem (Spanish and English) with a title like "Moon of Conscious Love" or "Funeral dance".

### His series

- Himnos a la noche (Hymns to the night): his main series, 2024 Black & White Series of the Year (MonoVisions).
- Heraldos de la peste (Heralds of the plague): the plague doctor as a messenger of death.
- Muerte de la luz (Death of the light).

### Techniques, in our words (also on the Effects page, family "Silhouette night")

1. Backlit silhouette composite.
2. One moon, one halo.
3. Glowing eyes in the black.
4. Low horizon, big sky.
5. Dirty window texture.
6. Two figures, one gesture.
7. Object for a head (one Magritte swap per picture).

### What this teaches us (Claude's note, not a decision)

- **His method is safe for Amir's face by construction.** A backlit silhouette hides the face completely, and it is a real photograph assembled by hand, no AI. Amir in sunglasses and a long coat against a moon or a streetlight is already a shape that reads.
- **We can make it without buying anything:** a backlit shoot (the cemetery next door, Tempelhofer Feld at dusk), a real dirty window photographed as a texture layer, and the assembly in Photoshop or the local tools.
- **Not for us:** the horned and demonic figures (Amir's exclusion). The plague doctor, the cape, the hat, birds, bare trees, the moon and couples are all open.
- **Next to his "balanced opposition" pick:** many of Lucas's pictures are two figures facing each other around a light. The two ideas fit together.
