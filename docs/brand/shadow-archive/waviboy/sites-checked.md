# The 29 sites, checked one by one (2026-10-12)

Each site opened by an agent on 2026-10-12: what you actually get, cost, image rights, quality, the job it would do, and a verdict. Source URLs under each. Part of Shadow Archive R-006; the short list is in sites.md.


Method: WebFetch, WebSearch and a headless browser pane. Where a page could not be opened, it says so. Numbers are only given where the site (or a named source) states them.

## 1. ShotDeck
Sources opened: https://shotdeck.com/welcome/about (via browser; WebFetch got 403), https://help.shotdeck.com/plans/subscription-pricing/, https://help.shotdeck.com/content/how-can-i-use-the-images-on-shotdeck/

- What it is: a paid, searchable database of stills from films, TV, music videos and ads, built for cinematographers and directors.
- What you get: shots tagged and searchable (by title, colour, lighting, shot type and so on; filter list itself not visible without login). The about page calls it "the world's largest fully searchable cinematic image database" and says "thousands of new shots" are added every week. It gives no total count (a 2022 cined.com article said 246,107 stills; that is old and third-party).
- Cost: $12.95/month or $99.95/year (stated on help centre). Student/faculty discounts "available" (no amount stated). "Sign up free" link exists, but I could not confirm a free tier or trial length on an official page.
- Rights: reference only. Help page: images are for "reference, research, and educational purposes only". Allowed: internal pitch decks, presentations, personal projects. Not allowed: "External commercial use", reselling, "Republishing or redistributing them publicly". Copyright stays with the rights holders.
- Quality signal: founded 2014 by Lawrence Sher, ASC (DP of Joker); run by Filmmaker's Research Lab, LLC; copyright 2026; active, weekly additions. Homepage featured a WizKid music video, so music videos are in scope.
- Job for this artist: find the exact light, colour and framing for a cover or reel shot and put it in a shoot brief for the photographer. Overlaps with FILMGRAB (free, smaller) and Directors' Library Frames.
- Verdict: KEEP-candidate. Biggest tagged, searchable film-still library; the only one here built for "find me this light" searches.

## 2. FILMGRAB
Source opened: https://film-grab.com/about/

- What it is: a free, one-person archive of film stills, organised by film.
- What you get: galleries of stills from "nearly 4,000 films" (stated). Browse by film, director, DP, genre, country (browse only; no colour or lighting search found).
- Cost: free. Supported by Patreon; the site says it sells nothing.
- Rights: reference only. Presented under fair use for education and reference; the site says it does not own the images.
- Quality signal: run by Dawn, a filmmaker/cinematographer, who picks and posts every image herself. Started 2010. Footer says 2021 but images were uploaded as late as October 2025, so it is still updated.
- Job for this artist: free mood-board pulls for a shoot or reel, whole-film galleries to study one film's look. Overlaps with ShotDeck (ShotDeck has the search, FILMGRAB is free and shows whole films in sequence).
- Verdict: KEEP-candidate (as the free backup to ShotDeck). Free, carefully chosen, whole-film galleries; weaker search.

## 3. Directors' Library
Sources opened: https://directorslibrary.com, https://directorslibrary.com/membership, https://directorslibrary.com/frames (legal page returned an image file, not terms)

- What it is: a curated showcase of current music videos, commercials, shorts and films, with frames, behind-the-scenes and director interviews.
- What you get: project pages, a Frames section (stills from 2026 projects), BTS, numbered interviews (CXLIII published October 2026), "Guest Selects". Search plus suggested terms for director, location, format, festival, agency, production company, record label. No totals stated.
- Cost: browsing showed no login or paywall. Company Membership $75/year (for production companies submitting work). Submission costs exist but were not itemised in what I read.
- Rights: not stated on the pages I could open (legal link did not load as a page). Treat as reference only; the work belongs to the directors.
- Quality signal: curated, current (October 2026 items). Operator not named; partner collectives listed (Arena Collective, Bleck, Coldharbour Lane, Landia, to Be Magazine).
- Job for this artist: see what music video directors are doing right now, find a director or DP to hire for a video, study current music video looks. Overlaps with ShotDeck (stills) and Recent (current design feed).
- Verdict: NICHE. Useful when commissioning or planning a music video; not a daily tool.

## 4. Magnum Photos
Sources opened: https://www.magnumphotos.com/about-magnum/, https://www.magnumphotos.com, https://pro.magnumphotos.com (licensing portal; no terms or prices shown)

- What it is: the website of the photographers' cooperative founded in 1947, with stories, archive, store and licensing.
- What you get: photo essays and portfolios by member photographers, a searchable archive on the pro site, a store (books, prints, posters), Magnum Learn courses, print sales. States an online audience of "over 6.6 million". No archive size stated.
- Cost: browsing stories appeared free. Licensing and prints are paid; no prices shown on the pages I opened.
- Rights: copyrighted. Usable in published work only through a paid licence via pro.magnumphotos.com (no licence term names or prices published there). Reference only otherwise.
- Quality signal: the most respected documentary photo agency; current (Sept/Oct 2026 stories, Ian Berry obituary 2026).
- Job for this artist: study real-world documentary light, street and portrait framing for the Berlin shoots and photo direction. Overlaps with ShotDeck/FILMGRAB on "reference for light", but this is photography, not film.
- Verdict: NICHE. Top quality but browsing photo essays, not a searchable reference tool for a job; licensing is out of budget.

## 5. Fonts In Use
Sources opened: https://fontsinuse.com/about (via browser), https://fontsinuse.com (staff, terms and uses pages returned 500 errors)

- What it is: a public archive of real-world typography, indexed by typeface, format, industry and period.
- What you get: 34,785 uses ("All" count on the homepage), 6,374 staff picks. Directly relevant counts stated on the site: Music 6,833 uses, Album Art 4,563, Posters/Flyers 6,185, Social Media 1,310. Each use names the typefaces, so you can see a font in an actual album cover or poster.
- Cost: free to browse; account needed to contribute. Funded by sponsors (Mark Simonson named).
- Rights: images are of third-party work, contributed with a copyright field; reference only. The site's Terms page would not load (500).
- Quality signal: running since 2010 (blog) / 2012 (collection), copyright 2010-2026, staff picks curated by an editorial team (staff page did not load, so names not confirmed). Still current (uses dated 2026 at the top).
- Job for this artist: pick a title font by seeing it on real album covers and posters, and check how a candidate (e.g. Cinzel, Marcellus, Albertus) has been used. Overlaps with The Brand Identity (shows type in context) and Letterform Archive (history).
- Verdict: KEEP-candidate. The single best "how does this font look on a real record cover" tool, free, with 4,563 album-art entries.

## 6. ABC Dinamo
Sources opened: https://abcdinamo.com, https://abcdinamo.com/faq, https://abcdinamo.com/licenses; prices from search result summarising https://abcdinamo.com/news/about-our-pricing (not opened directly)

- What it is: a Swiss/Berlin type foundry selling its own typefaces (Diatype, Favorit, Gravity, Oracle, Ginto, Marfa, etc.).
- What you get: a catalogue of contemporary families, free trial fonts, a free Font Customizer, an Early Access section of unreleased designs. Latest releases listed: Arizona, Schengen, Ikarus.
- Cost: paid. Priced by the client's company size, not number of users. A 2020-era pricing article lists desktop licence per style from €90 (3 people) to €630 (500 people); I did not confirm current prices on a live product page. Student font pack exists.
- Rights: usable in published work once licensed. Licence names from the EULA: "Desktop/print", "Logo/wordmark", "Social media", "Web", "App/game", "Video", "Unlimited/enterprise", "Trial". Useful detail for a one-person act: desktop/print includes free logo/wordmark under 50 people and free social media and video licences under 3 people. Trial fonts: only to decide on buying and "to pitch design directions to your clients", so not for published work.
- Quality signal: one of the leading independent foundries; active releases; copyright current.
- Job for this artist: buy one modern sans or grotesk for the electronic lane or credits, if a free font will not do. Overlaps with Future Fonts (paid fonts) and Velvetyne (free fonts).
- Verdict: NICHE. High quality, but a single foundry is a shop, not a reference tool; use only when a specific Dinamo face is chosen.

## 7. Velvetyne
Sources opened: https://velvetyne.fr (homepage is currently a strike banner only; /about pages returned 500), mirror https://velvetyne.alwaysdata.net/ and /about/faq/, font page /fonts/terminal-grotesque/, news post /news/your-next-font-is-not-velvetyne/, https://t.me/s/velvetyne

- What it is: a French collective (about 12 members, started 2010 via Frank Adebiaye) publishing free, open-source display fonts.
- What you get: the "Libre Typefaces" list shows 41 fonts on the mirror homepage (a 2026 book says the full catalogue is over 70). Mostly experimental, expressive display faces.
- Cost: free; donations optional.
- Rights: usable in published and commercial work. Most fonts use the "SIL Open Font License, Version 1.1" (confirmed on Terminal Grotesque). Not all: Velvelyne uses the "CUTE Conditions" (Bye Bye Binary), which restricts some uses such as by big companies. Credit to the designer and foundry is requested. Check the licence file per font.
- Quality signal: respected in experimental design circles; main domain is partly down (strike splash, 500 on inner pages). A Feb 2024 news post says they are rethinking what the OFL means for them. A search result headline mentioned joining Monotype; I could not verify that and the channel page I opened does not mention it.
- Job for this artist: free, rights-clear display fonts for a poster, reel title or the electronic lane. Overlaps with Future Fonts and ABC Dinamo (paid).
- Verdict: NICHE. Free and legal to publish, but the styles are very experimental and rarely fit the songs line; site is unstable right now.

## 8. Future Fonts
Sources opened (via browser; WebFetch only saw an empty JS shell): https://www.futurefonts.com/about, https://www.futurefonts.com/faq (Licenses section), https://www.futurefonts.com/

- What it is: a marketplace where independent type designers sell fonts while still in progress.
- What you get: early versions of new typefaces at lower prices, free updates as they progress, version notes showing what is finished, process journals. Foundries from "over 25 countries". Current examples on the homepage: Simulacra v0.1, Super Fat Bob v0.7, Cognate Grotesk v1.0, fonts by Ohno, Vectro, Alice Savoie.
- Cost: paid; "As more work is finished, the price goes up". No price list on the pages opened (a third-party site says from $5 per font; unverified).
- Rights: usable in published work once licensed. Licence is an "End User License Agreement (EULA)" priced by number of users (e.g. 1-3 users); "Each foundry on Future Fonts has their own EULA with unique terms".
- Quality signal: curated selection of foundries; created by Vectro; "independently run by a small team of designers"; active.
- Job for this artist: find a fresh, not-yet-overused title font for a release, cheaply. Overlaps with ABC Dinamo and Velvetyne.
- Verdict: KEEP-candidate (as the one place to buy a font). Fresh faces nobody else uses, low early prices, per-font EULA to check.

## 12. Letterform Archive Online Archive
Sources opened: https://oa.letterformarchive.org (shows "temporarily offline ... scheduled maintenance" today), https://letterformarchive.org/news/online-archive-update-december-2023/; image-rights page URL I tried returned 404

- What it is: the free online collection of Letterform Archive, a San Francisco museum of lettering, type, calligraphy and graphic design.
- What you get (when online): as of December 2023, "3,606 items represented by 27,485 images", browsable by people, firms, disciplines, decades, countries, formats. High-resolution scans of specimens, posters, books, ephemera.

## 1. Cosmos
Sources opened: https://www.cosmos.so (home), https://www.cosmos.so/pricing, /terms, /privacy (all JavaScript-only, no readable text), https://help.cosmos.so/ (section titles only). Price and company come from web search: Cosmos help page as quoted in search, US App Store listing, Creative Bloq, Dealroom.

- What it is: a visual inspiration site and app for saving, organising and searching images into collections.
- What you get: search by colour, "find similar" image search, collections ("clusters"), saving from anywhere on the web, collaboration. A setting to show, blur or hide images flagged as AI-generated. No image counts stated.
- Cost: free with account. Premium $8/month or $72/year ($6/month), per the help page and App Store as quoted in search; adds custom username, collaborating on clusters, bulk export, early access. I could not open the pricing page itself.

- Image rights: reference only. Images are other people's work saved by users.
- Quality signal: Cosmos Entity, Inc, New York, founded 2021, co-founder Andy McCune (ex Unfold), per search results; Series A Jan 2026 reported by Preqin (amount not confirmed). Lists Nike, Apple, Gucci as users. Active and current.

- Overlaps: Are.na, Savee, Same Energy (similar-image search).
- Verdict: KEEP-candidate. Best visual discovery plus colour search plus an AI-image filter in one place, but terms unread, so Are.na may win as the private board.

## 2. Huemint
Sources opened: https://huemint.com, https://huemint.com/about/, https://huemint.com/privacy/ (no privacy text found).

- What it is: a machine-learning colour palette generator that assigns colours to roles in a design.
- What you get: palettes for templates (brand, website, gradient, illustration) where each colour has a job (background, foreground, accent). Three models: Random (non-ML baseline), Transformer, Diffusion. Lock colours, adjust a "creativity" slider.
- Cost: no price shown; tool runs without an account as far as the pages show. API is free for non-commercial use, "as is".
- AI / data terms: the page states no privacy policy, no data terms, and does not name the operator. You do not upload anything; you only pick colours, so exposure is low.
- Image rights: no images. Palettes are colour values; no licence stated (colour values are not normally ownable, but the site says nothing).
- Quality signal: one-person project written in first person, author not named. The about page explains the models in technical detail, which is a good sign of care.
- Job for Amir: generate a palette for a cover or poster where background, type colour and accent must work together (fits his light/dark mood pairs).
- Overlaps: Khroma.
- Verdict: KEEP-candidate. Role-aware palettes match how a cover is built (ground, type, accent), free, no account, no uploads.

## 4. Khroma (the "pick ~50 colours" site)
Real URL: https://www.khroma.co. Source opened: https://www.khroma.co (home, via curl). https://www.khroma.co/faq/ returned 404. The "50 colours" figure comes from third-party reviews found by search (hackdesign.org, others), not from Khroma's own page.

- What it is: a colour tool where you pick colours you like, a neural network learns your taste, and it generates palettes.
- What you get: endless generated combos shown as typography (text on background), gradient, palette, or over an image. Search and filter by hue, tint, value, hex, RGB. Unlimited saved library. Colour names, hex, RGB, CSS and WCAG contrast rating per pair.
- Cost: no price on the home page; third-party sources say free, no account. Not verified on the site.
- AI / data terms: the home page says the model trains "right in your browser". Third-party sources say choices are stored in browser local storage. No privacy page found. The home page says it learned from "thousands of the most popular human-made palettes across the internet".
- Image rights: no images to reuse; palettes only.
- Quality signal: one maker ("George", credited as George Hastings), © 2025, so still maintained. Instagram @khromacolors curated by Lucie Bajgart.
- Job for Amir: find colour pairs that match his own taste (type colour on ground colour) with contrast ratings for legibility on covers and reels.
- Overlaps: Huemint.
- Verdict: NICHE. Pleasant and private (in-browser), but it learns generic "liked" colours, not a release-specific mood; Huemint does the cover job better. Keep one, not both.

## 5. Palette site that shows real design work for up to 5 colours: Designspiration
Identified by search: Designspiration (https://www.designspiration.com) colour search lets you pick up to 5 colours from a 120-colour palette and shows saved design work matching them (developer's blog https://blog.iso50.com/22275/designspiration/, Creative Bloq, htmlcolorcodes.com). Sources opened: https://www.designspiration.com, /about/, /pricing/ (via curl), and a search URL. I could not see the colour picker itself; it renders with JavaScript. The 5-colour feature is confirmed by third parties and the original developer blog, not by me on the live page.

- What it is: a design inspiration library (Pinterest-like) with a colour search that finds work using your chosen colours.
- What you get: search by up to 5 colours, keywords, collections, people. Save inspiration, collections, collaboration.
- Cost: Basic free with account. Pro $5/month or $50/year: unlimited "Visions" mood boards, "deeper inspiration searches", hide from search engines, ad-free. So the free tier has ads and shallower search.
- AI / data: no AI features mentioned. Did not read the privacy policy.
- Image rights: reference only; images are saved from other people's work.
- Quality signal: started by Scott Hansen (ISO50 / Tycho) per his blog post; current operator not named on the pages I read. © 2026, still online. Developer admits colour matching is imperfect when images have lots of white.
- Job for Amir: test a release palette by seeing real posters and covers that use it, before committing.
- Overlaps: Cosmos (colour search), Savee.
- Verdict: KEEP-candidate (for this one job). Only site in the list that answers "what does this palette look like in real work"; check the colour picker works live before adopting.

## 9. Savee

## 12. Library of Congress, Free to Use and Reuse

## 13. Internet Archive
Sources opened: https://archive.org (JavaScript only, title text only), https://help.archive.org/help/rights/. Scale figures from search results quoting the Archive's about text and an Oct 2025 news report.

- What it is: a non-profit digital library of books, film, audio, software, images and the Wayback Machine.
- What you get: per an older copy of its about text: 44M books/texts, 15M audio recordings, 10.6M videos, 4.8M images, 1M software programs; Wayback Machine passed 1 trillion web pages (Oct 2025 report). I could not confirm current figures on archive.org.

- AI / data: not an AI tool.

- Job for Amir: deep digging for old film footage, sounds, scans, record sleeves; then verify rights elsewhere.
- Overlaps: Library of Congress, Public Domain Review, GifCities (an Archive project).
