## 1. How to build disgustingly good taste

- Link: https://www.instagram.com/p/DbI75ONI7g6/
- Date: 2026-07-23 (851 likes)
- What it is: a reel. Taste is not inborn; it is three trainable skills: a real reference library, seeing patterns instead of trends, and remixing on purpose. Ends on a free pack page with four cards (read them at about 15.7s to 18s).
- Quote: "The filing is the skill, not the saving."
- Rules, in my words:
  1. Build a real reference library, not a mood board: filed, tagged, searchable, usable under pressure.
  2. Most people save thousands of images and can find none of them.
  3. Six folders, filed by property, never by brand or vibe.
  4. You never need "cool car photos"; you need a property search like hard sun, long shadows, 85mm.
  5. Filing by brand or vibe only finds what you already remember; filing by property answers questions you have not asked yet.
  6. One image goes in one folder, under its strongest property. If you cannot pick one, the image is not strong enough to keep.
  7. See patterns, not trends. A trend is this month; a pattern is why something works and was already true 40 years ago. Chase trends and you are always six weeks late; learn patterns and you can make the next one.
  8. Remix on purpose. Nothing is original; good work is two references collided with intent. Pick two that do not belong in the same frame, then find the one thing they share.
  9. Remix examples: a food packshot lit like a 1970s political portrait; a trainer shot like a museum artefact; a car ad shot like a wildlife documentary.
  10. Remix test: if you can already picture the result, it is a copy, not a remix.
  11. After a remix, run the five questions on the output. Each one you cannot answer is a decision you left to the machine.
  12. Question 4 (what was left out) is the one that trains the eye: anyone can list what is in a frame, taste is knowing what was removed.
  13. Question 5 covers the rest: halation, grain, flare and imperfection are choices, made on purpose by the people you admire.
  14. Never write "cinematic" in a prompt.
  15. The two references should feel wrong together; that is the point.
  16. Sites flashed while she talks about the library: Are.na, recent.design.
- Card 1, the library structure: six folders with sub-tags.
  - Light: hard sun, window, practical, overcast, neon, firelight
  - Colour: monochrome, two-tone, desaturated, blown-out, high-contrast
  - Lens: wide distort, 50mm neutral, 85mm compress, macro, anamorphic
  - Texture: grain, halation, dust, gloss, matte, wet
  - Composition: centred, negative space, frame in frame, low angle, flat lay
  - Mood: cold, warm, clinical, nostalgic, uneasy, luxurious (one more word cut off)
- Card 2, the five pattern questions (asked of every image you love before saving, about 60 seconds each), in my words:
  1. Light: where from, hard or soft.
  2. Colour count (usually two plus a neutral).
  3. What the lens does to the space.
  4. What was left out that you would have added.
  5. What would be lost if it were shot perfectly clean.
- Card 3, the remix prompt:
  - For: shooting one subject in an unrelated visual world.
  - Structure: Reference A (your subject) / Reference B (an unrelated visual world) / instruction (subject of A in the visual language of B) / keep from B / keep from A / four named slots / ban on "cinematic".
  - Keep from B: lighting, palette, lens behaviour, texture. Keep from A: only the subject and what it is doing.
  - Four slots: light (hard or soft), colour (two colours), lens (focal length), texture (film stock).
- Card 4, the 20-image drill (one week, about 20 minutes a day):
  - Mon to Tue: build the six folders, file 20 images you already love, one folder each, bin any that will not fit one.
  - Wed to Thu: answer the five questions for all 20, written next to each.
  - Fri: take your two most unrelated images and run the remix prompt.
  - Sat: run the five questions on what you made.
  - Payoff she claims: after the drill you can write a look from memory without a mood board; taste becomes a filing system, not a personality trait.
- Technique worth keeping: the remix prompt. Subject from A, the whole visual language from an unrelated B, named in four slots, never "cinematic", then audit the output with the five questions.

## 2. Reference moments, not brands

- Link: https://www.instagram.com/p/DPoZvGKiIFA/
- Date: 2025-10-10 (12,081 likes)
- What it is: a reel. Everyone's AI looks alike because everyone prompts brand names and gets recycled Pinterest looks. Real taste references the moments a brand came from, pulls from subcultures, collides them, and balances raw with refined.
- Quote: "you reference the moments Balenciaga came from"
- Rules and references, in my words:
  1. Do not prompt brands or generic labels. Her bad examples: "Balenciaga campaign", "Professional portrait", "Streetwear fashion".
  2. Reference the source moments instead. Named: Irving Penn's mid-century portraits (rigid, high-contrast formalism) and the raw energy of a 1990s Belgian runway.
  3. Her sunglasses example works because it fuses 90s rave culture with Y2K sleekness: messy hair, face tattoos, sharp frames that clash in the right way.
  4. Luxury brands pay a lot for that kind of cultural collision.
  5. Moment formula on screen: 1999, Berlin Love Parade, meets the early Oakley Thump era.
  6. Pull from subcultures, not trends.
  7. Balance raw and refined; originality sits there.
  8. Good AI design is about taste, tension and a sense of what fits now, not perfection.
- On-screen visuals: a "HARDCORE HAPPINESS" smiley rave top, a Y2K denim pocket with a gadget, a fisheye bathroom shot, a black and white 1999 crowd.
- Prompts: only short typed fragments of the bad brand-name prompt (around the "Balenciaga campaign" label). No full prompt.
- Technique worth keeping: swap any brand name for a dated collision of two moments: year, subculture event, meets a product era.

## 4. Curing the AI ick, part 1: portraits

- Link: https://www.instagram.com/p/DXrd_5GCH3V/
- Date: 2026-04-28 (972 likes)
- What it is: a reel. AI portraits look fake because people prompt in stock-photo language. Prompt as if directing a shoot: the photographer's look, a moment instead of a pose, and deliberate imperfection.
- Quote: "Real cameras leave fingerprints."
- Rules and references, in my words:
  1. The bad prompt is stock-photo language (beautiful woman, 8K, photorealistic) and has no soul.
  2. Prompt the photographer's look, not the person. Named: Petra Collins, with a Mamiya RB67, 35mm Portra 400 and a magic-hour pink wash (caption).
  3. Default smoothness gives AI away.
  4. Prompt the moment, not the pose: mid-laugh, glancing right, lips parting or eyes half closed (caption and screen differ on the last part).
  5. She says she tested 40 portraits changing one variable each, and moment-over-pose was the change that removed the AI look.
  6. Force small flaws: slight sunburn, one earring, smudged lipstick, dust on the lens, a half-blink.
  7. She credits Petra Collins with calling this controlled imperfection, and repeats that imperfection is the new luxury.
  8. You are directing a shoot, not prompting.
- Prompts on screen (two, one man and one woman; read them in the reel's typed sequence):
  - For: a candid editorial portrait.
  - Structure: subject (age, origin) / styling / location / era tag / photographer / film stock / lens flaw / moment or imperfections.
  - Ingredients: 26 years old, from Iceland, gorpcore, on a volcano, y2k, shot by Petra Collins, Portra 400, dust on the lens; man version adds mid laugh, glancing right, eyes half closed; woman version adds slight sunburn, one earring, half-blink, smudged lipstick.
  - Build-up shown before: the photographer name typed first, then the film stock, then the lens dust.
- Setting in all examples: Iceland, gorpcore, volcano, sky going from overcast to blue, dark lava ground.
- Technique worth keeping: a three-part portrait prompt: photographer plus film stock plus lens flaw, then a caught moment, then two or three small physical flaws.

## 5. The Reversal Technique: 3 tells of fake AI

- Link: https://www.instagram.com/p/DP9fPPdCLxy/
- Date: 2025-10-18 (5,423 likes)
- What it is: a reel. Fake-looking AI has three tells, and cheap-looking work does not get saved or shared. Reverse each tell.
- Quote: "If it looks AI, reverse it."
- The three tells: dead stare into the lens; vignette halo; colour too warm and oversaturated.
- The fixes, in my words:
  1. Eye line: move the eyes off-axis, glancing away, mid-laugh, an in-between expression.
  2. Add micro-actions: adjusting a sleeve, an exhale, hair catching light.
  3. Camera cues: 85mm equivalent, shallow depth of field, subject 30% off-centre.
  4. Why: off-axis eyes and in-between moments read candid, not staged.
  5. Colour: do not trust the default grade. Pull 2 to 3 real film frames as colour references. Named in the caption: In the Mood for Love, Euphoria, a Prada FW lookbook. Named in the video: Parasite and a Prada lookbook.
  6. Why: a grade grounded in real cinematography removes the generic AI sheen.
  7. Texture: run a pass through Seedream 4K (heard as "Seedreem" or "Seedroom") or Enhancor at low to mid strength. Aim to restore edge detail and natural grain without sharpening haloes. Stop when pores and fabric look real, not crispy.
- Mini checklist: eyes off camera; in-between expression; no auto vignette; palette matched to real film frames; texture pass without oversharpening.
- Mistakes she names: looking at the camera, smiling, perfect symmetry (all read posed); auto vignettes and glow filters; orange skin with teal shadows, the social-app preset look.
- Tools listed: Pinterest (references), Midjourney (images), Freepik and Weavy (Seedream 4 4K, Wan 2.5, Google Veo 3), Enhancor AI (enhancement).
- Prompts: one short typed fragment of the eye-line line on screen; the caption carries the fuller lines summarised above.
- Technique worth keeping: the five-point mini checklist as a gate before anything gets published.

## 6. The missing step after generating

## 8. ChatGPT can design posters if you split the prompt in two (carousel)

- Link: https://www.instagram.com/p/Dau0ZmCiHUR/
- Date: 2026-07-13 (carousel, 9 slides, 1,868 likes)
- What it is: a carousel. ChatGPT garbles posters when asked for the design and the words in one go. Send the art direction first, the exact words second.
- Quote: none used.
- The two-message method (slide 2):
  - Message 1: art direction only (look, layout, colour, texture), no text, and end by telling the model to hold it until the text arrives.
  - Message 2: the exact words only, the headline in quotes, nothing else.
  - Her claim: the text comes out clean every time.
- Examples, each beside ChatGPT's own default for the same brief: a club night poster (NO SLEEP, blackletter over a screaming duotone cut-out, acid green); a streetwear drop (Reliqua, blackletter on black); a cafe menu (Alba, red script on paper); a film poster (SLOW LIGHT, blue and orange duotone photo, condensed sans); a gig flyer (ECHO, stretched condensed type with a fading echo).
- The six rules for why yours looks AI (slide 8), in my words:
  1. Let either the title or the photo lead; the other stays quiet.
  2. Use a real photo, cut out and duotoned; never clip art or a render.
  3. Only two type sizes, one huge and one tiny, nothing in between.
  4. One loud flat colour; everything else is paper and ink.
  5. Overlap type and photo, let them break into each other, repeat elements.
  6. Make it look printed (grain, scan), not a clean render.
- The six fill-in prompts are behind the comment POSTER; not collected.
- Technique worth keeping: separate look from copy, and use the six rules as a poster grammar for a gig flyer, release poster or cover: real cut-out photo, duotone, one loud flat colour, two sizes, printed texture.

## Her taste method in 6 lines

1. File references by property (light, colour, lens, texture, composition, mood), one strongest property per image, never by brand or vibe.
2. Before saving any image, answer the five questions; number 4 (what was left out) trains the eye.
3. Study one era deeply (about 50 images) and look for the pattern that was true 40 years ago, not this month's trend.
4. Never prompt a brand or "cinematic"; prompt a dated collision of two moments, or subject A in the visual language of an unrelated B, with light, two colours, focal length and film stock named.
5. For people: name the photographer and film stock, prompt a caught moment with eyes off camera, and add two or three small flaws.
6. Finish and audit: grade from real film frames, kill vignettes and orange and teal, run a light texture pass without oversharpening, then ask the five questions again.
