## Claude's first read: WITHDRAWN (Amir, 2026-09-20)

The first version of this file concluded that the set was "the sleeves of his own music" and
ranked candidates. Amir corrected it: the references match each other only because of the
categories he happened to browse on the site, which has many more; nothing was sent twice on
purpose (the repeats were mistakes); and every font here is a POTENTIAL for some future
release, not a candidate in a race. The categorised list is `../font-library.md`.

**What the second batch adds (Claude's read):**
- **Cohen, twice.** His positioning names Cohen first in the writing lineage. Both Cohen
  pieces are the opposite of decoration: one typeface, capitals stacked and justified, black
  and white; or a lyrics booklet printed letterpress on kraft paper with visible
  imperfections. That is a reference for how WORDS are presented (lyrics, A Letter for
  Listeners), more than for the name.
- **A custom face from handwriting** (Niceshit). He chose, unprompted, a brand whose
  letters were drawn from its founders' own handwriting, with a plain support face next to
  it. Same idea as a hand-lettered "Inch of Shadow" plus a bought face for the rest.
- **Again a display face with a plain partner** (Niceshit + Onlysans, Zangezi + CF Asty,
  Signal + Hollandse Mediaeval): now 9 of 28 pages state a pairing.
- **Stencil** (Picasso catalogue) was already on his 09-19 list of looks.

**Where the text-only read was wrong or thin:**
- "Tight capitals" is not a rule in the set: The Smiths and Yovel are spaced wide.
- On about half the music covers the IMAGE is the cover and the type is a small label at the
  top edge or in a corner (03, 09, 15, 19, 26, 30, also 06 and 21). On the others the type IS
  the picture (04, 12, 27, 28, 25, the festival posters). Both ways are in his taste.
- Several use two voices: a loud face and a quiet one (01 capitals and script, 16 bold sans
  and thin lowercase, 11 thin serif and condensed sans).
- Colour is usually two inks plus the image: red and black, pink, orange and pink, white on
  blue, cream on maroon.
- So what he is collecting is as much a way of putting type on a picture as a list of
  fonts. A font from here will only look like its reference if it is set the same way.

**What this batch adds (Claude's read, to be corrected):**

- **Sacred Bones is not a cover, it is a system.** One typeface, a fixed slot order, and the
  art does the changing. That is the first reference here that answers "how do I make twenty
  releases look like one artist" rather than "how do I make one cover". Worth keeping in
  mind for `formats.md`, not just for the font list.
- **Both it and the Ovid book point at 4AD**, one by naming it as a source, one by a reader
  seeing it. 4AD sits next to The Cure in the positioning neighbourhood.
- **Novel Gothic is Deco capitals with wide letterspacing over a bright illustration**, which
  is the same move as Madonna (01) and A Flock of Seagulls (03): the picture carries it, the
  type is one calm line.
- **Three of the four Ovid faces are for a BOOK**, not a sleeve. Castellar with Diotima
  Italic on a title page is the closest thing yet to how A Letter for Listeners could be set.

**Canva, checked the same day:** Eckmann no (already known), ITC Busorama no, Gemini
Computer no, Neue Haas Grotesk no. Canva does have **Helvetica**, **Helvetica Now** (+
Display, Condensed) and Helvetica World, which is the same design family as Neue Haas
Grotesk, the 1957 original that Helvetica was renamed from.

**What this batch adds (Claude's read, to be corrected):**

- **Rizohm is the second SYSTEM he has sent**, after Sacred Bones (33). Same structure: one
  plain face carries all the information, the imagery changes every edition, and the series
  still reads as one thing. It adds two ideas Sacred Bones did not have: a **hand-made
  wordmark** next to a bought face (the same split as Niceshit, 29, and as his own planned
  hand-drawn mark, Look-02), and a **symbol kept separate from the wordmark** so either can
  be used alone.
- **Mort Garson is the opposite of a system**: three faces from three eras on one sleeve, and
  it holds because they are stacked tight as ONE block. It is the furthest thing here from
  "one face for everything", and it is also Eckmann's second appearance (first on Sonic
  Youth, 15).
