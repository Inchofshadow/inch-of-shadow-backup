# Artist marks: how other artists solved it (research, 2026-09-19)

Made for Look-02 (logo mark). Sources are named so claims can be checked. Use this
before proposing any mark, and add to it whenever new examples come up.

## The five archetypes

1. **Lettermark with a flaw.** Nine Inch Nails: "NIN" with the second N reversed.
   Designed by Trent Reznor with art director Gary Talpas (1989), who cites Tibor
   Kalman's typography for Talking Heads' "Remain in Light" as the starting point. The
   identity is not the letters, it is the one broken letter.
2. **Sigil or seal.** The Sisters of Mercy's Merciful Release mark: a head taken from a
   Gray's Anatomy illustration set over a five-pointed star, drawn by Andrew Eldritch.
   Reads as an occult stamp, works on a sleeve, a patch and a door.
3. **Found scientific image.** Joy Division's Unknown Pleasures: the radio plot of pulsar
   CP 1919, made by astronomer Harold Craft at Arecibo for his 1970 thesis, spotted by
   Bernard Sumner in an encyclopaedia and reversed to white on black by Peter Saville.
   Never designed as a logo, became one anyway.
4. **Logotype only.** Most new wave identities: the name set once in a geometric sans
   (Futura, Univers, Kabel, Avant Garde) and never changed. Saville's Factory work is the
   reference point. No symbol to explain.
5. **Emblem or badge.** Text locked inside a shape. Strong on merch, but it has to be
   simplified for small sizes or it turns to mud.

## What prints and what does not

- Wordmarks, lettermarks and simple pictorial marks survive screen print, embroidery and
  heat transfer. Emblems and detailed illustrations need a simplified second version.
- High contrast and few colours print crisply and cost less.
- Serifs work small only with strong letterforms; fine hairlines and scripts break.
- Test every mark at 32px (profile picture) and in one colour before deciding.

## What this means for Inch of Shadow

- The strongest route for him is 3, a found image that carries meaning, because the brand
  already runs on cycles and on light against dark, and because it avoids the goth cliche
  that route 2 risks. Candidate sources: moon phase plates, orbit and eclipse diagrams,
  optics diagrams of light and shadow, Lissajous curves, botanical or anatomical
  engravings.
- Route 1 fits the name literally: the inch mark, or a letter and its shadow, with one
  deliberate flaw.
- Route 4 is needed in any case: the name set in Marcellus is the wordmark, with or
  without a symbol beside it.
- Route 2 is available but has to stay quiet, closer to a printer's seal than to occult
  merchandise.

## Outcome (2026-09-19, Look-02): what Amir actually chose

The recommendation above did not win. After this research Amir set the pop rule: a mark
must be mass legible, a stranger feels something in one second. Found scientific images
(gnomon, obelisk, diagrams) read as smart but cold and niche. The direction he chose is
the RING: a thorn ring (halo, cycle, crown of thorns), used alone as the small mark, and
as the O in the capitals version; the obelisk as the I in the mixed-case wordmark; never
both in one lockup. Rejected: inch symbol, ruler tick, half shadow circle, moon phases,
snake ouroboros, classic laurel, letter monograms. Status: working set, final art to be
hand drawn by Amir. Drafts: `../mark/mark-drafts-v4.html`. Lesson for the next research:
test every route against the pop rule before recommending it.
