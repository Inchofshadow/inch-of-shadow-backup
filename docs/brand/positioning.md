## Who

Inch of Shadow is Amir Miller, Berlin. One person writes, plays, produces, mixes and
masters everything, at Ambertone Studios. Since 2026 the main line is songs, with
guitars, bass, synths and his own voice; the electronic lane continues under the same
name. Music is not a career move, it is his whole life, and he would make it with or
without an audience.

## The essence of the name

"One inch of love is one inch of shadow." Light and shadow as one thing. Duality runs
through the music, the words and the visuals, and everything in a song or a picture
should complete as one whole.

## The sound

**Constant:** his voice on every song, in English, usually through reverb and delay.
Played by hand, his own electric guitar included, more than programmed. A beat that
leans new wave and post-punk, around 110 BPM, with hip hop and electro in how the drums
and the low end sit. Songs built as songs, with a chorus and a hook you can sing back.

**Free to move:** the genre corner, the instrumentation, the production, and the pop,
hip hop and electro balance per song. The sound is still being explored, so the identity
rides on the voice and the writing, not on a genre. Not heavy metal, not folk.

**Neighbourhood** (for pitches and playlists, not for bios): R. Missing, The Cure,
Crystal Castles. Closer references come per release and per song, from Amir.

RULE, two different jobs: artistic words (dark dance wave, songwriter, poetry, the
influences) go in bios, captions and interviews. Platform tags (darkwave, synth-pop,
alternative dance, new wave, dream pop) go in metadata, pitch forms and hashtags.
Never mix them: the first is how it sounds, the second is how machines file it.

## The words

The lyrics are the edge. Poetic, between realism and fantasy, about love,
self-reflection, energy and cycles. Written sometimes fast, sometimes over weeks of
changing single words, because every word carries a purpose. The lineage is Cohen,
Dylan, Morrison and Lennon, and when a song needs it, plain and direct like The Cure.
The rule underneath: it has to be real, and people have to believe what he is saying.

## Influences

Deepest of all: Maynard James Keenan and Tool. Not in genre, in everything around it:
darkness, psychedelia, meaning, art and design as part of the work. It reaches the music
too, in a way Amir says cannot be explained or copied; sometimes a clean guitar or bass
riff comes out Toolish. The same lineage explains the Alex Grey symmetry he responds to
and the cycles running through his lyrics. It is one influence among several, never a
visual identity to copy: sacred geometry is not a house style (Amir, 2026-09-19).

In the writing: Cohen, Dylan, Morrison, Lennon, and plain directness when a song needs
it, like The Cure. In the sound world: The Cure, Crystal Castles, R. Missing. Post-punk
and new wave arrive as culture and attitude, not as guitars.

Depeche Mode is a major influence and the clearest model for the whole project: synths
and songs in one body, darkness carried by hooks, a dark pop that reached the masses.
They are the proof that the electronic lane and the songs are one artist, not two.

Gesaffelstein is a lasting electronic influence, now less on the EBM side and more in
texture, atmosphere and groove: how a track breathes and moves rather than how hard it
hits. He is also an influence on clothing and styling: sharp, monochrome,
controlled, closer to tailoring than to stage costume.

## What he wants from a listener

Connection first, someone who feels the same thing. Then the song that gets played
again. And devotion: this is not background music, and being adored matters more than
being streamed.

What he wants said about him: that he is a true artist, that the sound and the lyrics
are his own and keep getting better, and that the melodies are good and they catch. The
hooks are not an accident, they are the point.

## Reach: it has to be pop

The work is not made for a niche. The model Amir names is Lana Del Rey, not her sound but
how her design speaks to the masses: she uses symbols everyone already knows (America,
the flag, its colours), she broadcasts luxury, her type is a luxury style, and her
styling swings between distant luxury and city rock girl. The lesson is that a big crowd
is reached through familiar cultural symbols handled with taste, not through private
references.

So: symbols are welcome from myth, religion and legend, across cultures, because Amir
loves that material and because it is mass legible. The test is always the same, would a
stranger feel something in a second, without an explanation. Clever and academic loses to
clear and charged. Not too much at once.

## Who it is for

Not one scene and not one age. Younger listeners and his own generation, up to about
fifty. The crowd is mixed on purpose: a goth edge, hipsters, punks, shoegaze and
alternative people, weirdos, geeks, and mainstream listeners too. What they share is not
a genre or a look, it is being moved by something that feels real.

RULE: no single category or tag is put on this audience, in any pitch or brief. The
songs are not written for one scene.

## What he shows

His face, the studio, playing, the city: yes. Private life: usually not. Politics: no,
not at the moment. Exceptions are his to make in the moment.
"My songs are my face to the world. Nobody will know me besides my songs and my art."

Persona: more present than his model. MJK stands at the back in shadow; Amir will be
on stage and on Instagram and TikTok, because that is what reaching people now asks
for. What he keeps is the second half of it: he does not explain himself, and he does
not explain the songs.

## Where it is going

Living from the music. Touring and festivals across Europe and the USA, open to Asia.
Large streaming numbers and a familiar name in the scene. Collaborations with artists he
admires. Respected by musicians and producers, and heard by the masses.

## The scene (facts, mirror of proof.md)

Berlin. DJ sets and live shows at About Blank, Beate Uwe, KitKat, Insomnia, and Torture
Garden at Metropol. Released on MM Discos, Stereo Society, NaNa Disc (with Double
Figure) and his own Indigo Children Records. Curated the music for Shape of Us 2.0 and
3.0 at Dark Matter, Berlin Fashion Week.

### Platform jobs (songs lane, approved 2026-11-06)

One job per platform. Source and reasoning: `plans/strategy/platform-plan-songs-2026-11-05.md`
(approved by Amir 2026-11-06, D5). Each line: the job, what we do, what we measure.

- **Spotify.** Job: hold the fans, a follow puts every new song in their Release Radar. We do:
  pitch every single (Spotify + Amazon) on delivery day, Artist Pick and Canvas per single, aim at
  the Verified badge, never two pitches at once. We measure: followers, day-28 Release engagement
  per single, Release Radar adds shown in S4A.
- **Bandcamp.** Job: the owned, paying core and the email list. We do: every single as a private
  draft 8 weeks out, sent with the Bandcamp Daily pitch, release on a Bandcamp Friday when the
  dates line up, mailing list sign-up at every release and gig. We measure: followers, email list,
  sales per release.
- **Instagram.** Job: bring traffic, the face, the process, the first hours of each release. We do:
  round one of the content language (7 experiments), release-day morning as the main event, Music on
  Profile and notifications per song, one peer collab per song, Trial Reels as the hook test. We
  measure: views per follower vs 0.77x, non-follower share, sends, profile visits and follows per
  release.
- **TikTok.** Job: exposure, let others use the sound and post our own raw work. We do: open with
  the first single, certify with a delivered track, pick the 60 s clip start per song as a release
  decision, post the same clips as the Reels (clean files), 2-3 a week while a round runs, no AI
  images. We measure: videos by others per sound, own-account reach, Add to Music App saves, then
  Spotify followers in the same week.
- **YouTube.** Job: carry the video, one finished official video per single, Shorts Previews before
  it. We do: one performance or studio video per single (also the Spotify video pitch), Shorts
  Previews with the official audio from about T-14 if Symphonic delivers them, chase the Official
  Artist Channel. We measure: views on the official video vs channel size, Shorts to song-page
  clicks, subscribers.
- **SoundCloud.** Job: home of the electronic lane, a side door for the songs. We do: electronic as
  today; songs get one follower-only early drop per single (Artist Pro), then public and Amplify
  inside 14 days, uploaded direct so Amplify applies. We measure: electronic, plays in the first 14
  days, Amplify result, likes and reposts; songs, follows gained from the gated drop.
- **Email.** Job: the only audience no feed can filter. We do: Buttondown, sign-up link in bios and
  on every release page, one mail per release, nothing else. We measure: list size, opens per mail.

No platform is the engine; the song is. Exposure may come from TikTok or Instagram, it has to land
on Spotify and the email list.
