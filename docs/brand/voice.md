# Inch of Shadow - Voice (v2, 2026-09-20)

How Inch of Shadow writes: captions, bios, descriptions, pitches, messages. Decided with
Amir on 2026-09-20 (board ticket Words-04) from his answers and from his 262 real Instagram
captions. Who he is: `positioning.md`. Facts: `proof.md`. Read the PUBLIC STATUS block in
`positioning.md` first: the songs are not public yet.

## Voice in one line

Warm and first person. A real person who is glad you are here, not a brand and not a
marketer. It depends on his mood in the moment, but warm is the default. Length is free:
a caption can be one line or a long one. From now on he plans to SHARE MORE.

## How he really writes (from his own captions)

**These habits carry over to the songs (Amir, 2026-09-21: "for the text, for example the
emojis you found, you can keep").** They were read from 262 electronic-era captions, but they
are HIS voice, not the era's: the emoji set, first person, warm and thankful, plain words,
"Spiral out" when it fits. Only two things in that data are not rules: how short the
captions were (length follows his mood) and how little he shared (he plans to share more).

- **Mostly short so far.** Half of his past captions are under 85 characters, nine in ten
  under 210. That is a description of the past, NOT a rule: length follows his mood, and
  he intends to share more from now on. Never cut his text down to fit a length.
- **First person.** "I", "my". "We" only when it really is more than him.
- **Warm and thankful.** "It was a real pleasure", "big thanks to", naming the people and
  places involved, tagging them.
- **Plain words.** Says what is happening: where he plays, what is out, who helped.
- Example of the default, his own: "Released today. My remix for The Tour by
  @lagroovemachine_music. Etched into the night. Pure fun to work on this one."

## Two registers

1. **Warm (the default):** life, gigs, studio, thanks, most release posts.
2. **Cool and clipped (when the release calls for it):** short declarative lines, no
   warmth, the music described in a few words. His own example: "Farewell. Out now. An EBM
   track. Acid melody, brooding atmosphere, rolling bass."

It depends on the case and on his mood. When unsure, write the warm one.

## The song's meaning

Usually say NOTHING about what a song means. Describe how it sounds, where it was made, who
was involved, or just let it be. He decides case by case, by the song and his mood, and
an exception is his to make, never Claude's. He does not explain himself and he does not
explain the songs (`positioning.md`).

The one planned place where listeners get more is **A Letter for Listeners** (see below),
and even there it is poetry, not explanation.

## Emojis

Yes, his usual set, used lightly: 🖤 ✨ 🌹 🌀 🕸 (also seen often: 🪩 🐈‍⬛ 🤍 💫). About half
of his captions have one or a few. Never a wall of them, never as bullet points.

## "Spiral out"

His sign-off, sometimes, when it relates to the post. Not on every caption, never forced.

## Greetings and sign-offs in messages

"Hi all" and "Hope you will like it" belong to MESSAGES and emails to people (DJs, curators,
friends, labels). They are not caption language: none of his 262 captions opens with a
greeting. In messages he does not ask for feedback or opinions; he says it, shares it, stops.

## Hashtags and tags, per platform

Whatever is best for each platform, from `release_manager/seo_platform_specs.json` and
`docs/ig-agent-context.md` section 9:

- **Instagram post or reel:** 3 to 5 precise tags at the END of the caption. They label the
  content, they no longer bring reach. The caption text is what Instagram reads, so the key
  words go in the first line (first 125 characters).
- **Instagram story:** 1 to 3.
- **YouTube:** 3 to 5 at the end of the description (the first three show above the title).
  Shorts: 2 or 3, and #Shorts is not required (YouTube's own help, `youtube/PLAYBOOK.md`).
- Which words: `positioning.md`, the two-jobs rule. Platform tags (darkwave, synth-pop,
  alternative dance, new wave, dream pop for the songs; dark disco, EBM, coldwave, acid,
  electro, techno for the electronic lane) go in tags and metadata. Artistic words ("dark
  dance wave", the influences) go in sentences. Never mix them.
- An @handle in a YouTube description links to a YouTube channel, not to Instagram.

## Never

- Em-dashes or double dashes. Commas and full stops instead.
- Clickbait hooks ("Wait for this part", "POV:", listicle openers).
- Marketing filler ("new music out now, go stream") and hype superlatives.
- Lines that comment on the message itself (wry, self-aware, meta).
- Asking for feedback or opinions.
- A category or tag put on the audience.
- Anything not in `proof.md`. Nothing claimed as released before its date.
- His spoken answers used as copy. They are raw material: take the direction, never the
  vocabulary.
- The name written as IOS or IoS.

## A Letter for Listeners (Amir's idea, 2026-09-20, not built yet)

Something unique, in his words: not a thank-you letter and not how the album was made. It
is **a piece of poetry that reflects the period of his life the record comes from**,
something for listeners to engage with, and maybe even **a "playbook" for the listeners:
feelings, moods, colours**. Form: an Instagram **carousel**. For an **EP or an album**,
never per single, because singles are the working mode. He writes it. It takes something
from the record's cover art (maybe a texture) so it speaks the same visual language. The
content can vary from record to record. Board ticket Formats-06.
